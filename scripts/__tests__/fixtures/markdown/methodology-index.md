# Methodology

## Pricing Pipeline Methodology

The pricing pipeline collects observed quotes from aggregators, exchanges, oracles and on-chain pools, then applies reviewed protocol/NAV marks and bounded fallback enrichment. Source identity, confidence and observation provenance stay attached to the published price.

Consensus uses fully pairwise agreement clusters and publishes the winning cluster median, retaining its strongest member for provenance. Large independent DEX protocols can challenge soft consensus; executable wrapper redemption marks remain distinct from issuer-conversion references and nominal par. Neither a listed provider nor a nominal reference proves an observed market.

Freshness belongs to the observation, not the publication. Upstream timestamps are preferred; local-fetch and unknown modes remain explicit. Carries, retries and child valuations preserve original clocks and source-specific age limits. Replay-safe continuity may preserve a confirmed signal without inventing a new one.

Unavailable prices remain unavailable, never zero or a nominal peg fill. Supply and lifecycle rows can remain visible while price gaps degrade cron and public health. Recovery needs a fresh admitted publication; release dates, provider registration and local comparisons do not prove production acceptance.

Hive internal market (HBD/HIVE) evidence is HIVE/USD divided by the native midpoint. At least 500 HBD per side within 2%, narrow spread, recent non-self turnover, fresh non-regressing node heads and an independently timestamped CoinGecko HIVE/USD parent are required. The parent must be within five minutes; the effective local-fetch clock is the earlier leg, and neither publication nor retry refreshes either leg. This is one CoinGecko-dependent soft observation, not two independent votes or a conversion-price guarantee. It remains single-source without independent agreement and requires depeg confirmation, ordinary validation and pool challenge; a near-par direct CG quote can win the family over a native depeg. Failed gates remain unavailable, with no supply changes or historical backfill.

SOFID uses exact-identity CoinGecko/Kraken observed market consensus, not a nominal protocol-par override. Discounts and premiums keep their value and original clock; Kraken remains local-fetch and confirm_required. Missing quotes and carried nominal references stay unavailable, not $1. Issuer-conversion references for ONED/PYUSDx do not establish direct-wrapper identity or guaranteed liquid pegs, and fresh own-market discounts win.

CoinGecko Onchain is the sole enabled exact-address augmentation provider, key-gated and explicitly allowlisted. Only canonical deployments with missing prices or below-target source depth qualify, under identity, freshness, liquidity and request-budget gates. Unknown addressed tokens are not reinterpreted by symbol; reviewed deployment narrowing must still match current metadata. Eligible exact reviewed overrides reserve one bounded request before network round-robin; fairness rotates inside priority cohorts and persistent active gaps precede breadth refresh. Hourly corroboration and quarter-hour missing/address-priced refresh do not renew source clocks. DexScreener exact-pool fallback is separate from this address-provider adapter.

Promoted DEX lanes carry structured source-filter telemetry and confidence profiles; a lone protocol needs hard-source corroboration unless no non-DEX voice exists. Their presence withholds overlapping aggregate fallback even when their candidates fail corroboration. Direct-API quote legs prefer tracked live stablecoin prices, not unconditional $1 symbols. Fresh timestamped RedStone venue breakdowns map only to configured canonical identities.

Challenger snapshots retain qualifying protocol diversity before their TVL coverage cap. Challenger and DEX-bridge pools must pass pair-price coherence: the tracked leg's USD price agrees with the pool pair ratio × the counter-leg's USD price. A DEX row without a usable primary for deviation, outlier and display guards is withheld. Pool challenge uses per-protocol medians, not one rogue pool; high-TVL multi-protocol and narrowly hard-corroborated single-protocol exceptions retain their technical gates. NAV tokens are excluded from challenge. Replacement updates candidate prices; corroborated severe downside including depeg-authoritative evidence may lose confidence but retains its price and satisfies the temporal-jump guard. Blocked DEX slugs such as Bunni cannot contribute.

CoinGecko discovery tickers are price-only evidence, separate from curated Kinesis ticker/CEX primary lanes. Non-stale, non-anomalous USD-equivalent quotes need positive finite prices, at least $1,000 observed USD flow and non-future registry freshness within 24 hours. Exchange prices are volume-weighted; aggregate weights use observed flow × ticker confidence 0.55, never synthetic depth or TVL. Tickers request no depth and attribute zero TVL, including legacy rows; genuine pools retain TVL-based weights. Zero-source-TVL price publication still needs usable primary, peg, outlier and display guards and cannot clear primary, UI or depeg DEX trust floors. Price-observation counts may include tickers; liquidity pool counts cannot.

A failed live fetch can carry only in-cadence dated references; independent OXR, Chainlink and metals probes continue so a recovered intraday subset can return live. Corroborating candidates survive subsequent validation when the selected result is unchanged, and fresh replay-safe price_cache can preserve confirmed severe-depeg continuity. Releases do not add supply or override DefiLlama aggregates.

Unavailable supply and completeness survive fallbacks and history: old chain current stays null, observed zero stays zero, and historical buckets require complete peg/supplemental evidence within the 14-day as-of budget. Composite prices inherit their parent's original clock; sync writes and DEX publication cannot refresh family quotes or aggregate contributors. Future or impossible FX days and out-of-policy epochs stay unavailable until genuinely replaced. USDT-denominated Curve ratios use admitted USDT/USD, with DOLA retaining composite depth two.

Genuinely absent clocks retain the existing undated policy. Independent ZSD official circulation and preferred market quotes keep their own provenance; rejected scanner prices do not erase them.

## Stability Index Methodology

The Pharos Stability Index (PSI) compresses stress across active core stablecoins and cash equivalents into a 0-100 condition score. Tracked variants and stable-value investments stay browsable but are excluded from the monetary aggregate. PSI starts at 100, subtracts penalties for severity, breadth, and stress breadth, then adds a clamped trend term before mapping the result into condition bands from BEDROCK to MELTDOWN.

Severity captures how bad current deviations are. Breadth captures how many assets are involved. Stress breadth captures how many stablecoins are elevated in DEWS. Trend captures whether the system is getting better or worse over the current window: negative trend reduces the score, while positive trend can offset part of the penalty.

PSI is deliberately conservative: one small depeg should not move the entire market condition, but simultaneous broad stress should pull the index down even if no single coin dominates the tape.

Methodology v3.67 can change repaired historical scores without changing formula weights or condition bands. Audit repairs and bounded backfills use canonical daily-price, native-domain and DEWS archive inputs with a shared twenty-one-day supply lookback. Historical trend pairs admitted supply identities; unavailable prior denominators or required missing DEWS archives preserve accepted days rather than inventing flat growth or zero stress. Exclusion/restoration rebuilds the post-audit eligible event universe atomically with event provenance. Contributor factors retain scoring precision and thirty-day statistics use source-anchored UTC coverage. Fixed-clock changed-day attribution and owner review remain release prerequisites, not completed repair claims.


## Safety Scores Grading Methodology

Safety Score V10 evaluates Backing, Exit and Economic Control, normally weighted 40%, 35% and 25%. It allows only bounded headroom above the weakest included pillar: a strong unrelated pillar cannot erase a known weak path. Missing measurements are not asset risk. Known peg problems, structural constraints, dependency limits and track record still matter; missing-data ceilings do not.

Economic-supply captures align independently selected safe EVM heads before reading quantities, without moving the oldest pin forward, and align non-rewindable Solana observations with safe EVM pins. Actual finality remains required for pending-history proof; explicitly reviewed chains may prefer authenticated finalized pins over heuristic delays. The 120-second skew and 30-minute age limits, canonical hashes, original clocks and full conservation checks still bind. A bridge's reviewed creation and initial sequence may be authenticated without old state, but complete historical messages and current finalized liabilities remain required. Missing history and stale issuer disclosures are rejected. Recovery requires a fresh accepted producer publication, not a routing change.

Evidence binds to its actual source and scope. Fatal warnings invalidate retained live reserves regardless of display severity or degraded-warning exceptions. Audited fallback baskets retain admitted tracked exposures; live dependency graphs age from each asset's reserve fetch, not a fresher sibling. Missing or expired evidence stays unavailable, never zero or renewed proof.

Exit follows the selected route's actual payout, not reserve allocations. Single stablecoin outputs have full weight; basket impairment stays unquantified without admitted payout weights. Measured depth and maturity age each original quote at assessment and bind one canonical block hash across proof and execution. An executed method refusal differs from an unavailable read. Non-USD peg fallback requires fresh per-peg FX whose source time is no later than capture; independent capacity, cost, access and settlement gates remain. Exact-request model reviews remain valid through their exact expiry instant, but a current review alone establishes no executable capacity.

Numeric upstream constraints survive unresolved provenance: same-slice known and unresolved losses use their maximum, not their sum. Supported constraints retain their own evidence despite unknown sibling obligations; an unsupported aggregate does not borrow a supported sibling's published proof. Review-level evidence remains available, and inherited Backing alone does not make an asset a wrapper. Shared mint and upgrade controllers are diagnostic only within the same reviewed issuer group; common leading words do not join names. Exact reviewed aliases cover MakerDAO/Sky governance and the StraitsX SGD/USD issuance group. Grouping does not merge legal issuers or reserve pools; unresolved or crossed groups retain common-mode risk.

Supply attribution, aggregate fallback and bridge-route clocks retain their existing admission gates. On-chain quantities and executable identities bind the same canonical hash; bridge receipts are assigned to their exact directed lane before authentication, never enlarged by unrelated sends.

Explicit current-price collection failure remains unavailable even with no historical peg events; it does not establish parity or erase adverse history. Observations cannot postdate their review. Peg adversity uses full precision, not the six-decimal public rounding.

Pillar and aggregate freshness retain categorical current, stale or unknown values, with compact source-clock operands available for reproducible explanations. Exit uses its original captured DEX observation, source generation and effective inclusive age budget; missing or future clocks are unknown. Backing and Economic Control remain explicitly unwired. This metadata changes no score, grade, cap, selected route or source-admission budget.

Direct Morpho Blue lender-vault Backing uses allocation-weighted collateral-book risk across reviewed VaultV2/MetaMorpho V1/V1.1 deployments, including non-USDC denominations. This is not a general ERC-4626 or indirect issuer-reserve rule. Denomination remains the reserve principal; borrower collateral is not a new holding or dependency edge. At one finalized block, idle cash plus included accrued lender claims must reconcile exactly to the source-accounted book: VaultV2 realAssets, otherwise MetaMorpho totalAssets excluding skimmable donations. VaultV2 separately proves totalAssets = min(realAssets, max-rate-capped accrual) and records the undistributed buffer, without market-specific deductions or an idle/dust plug. Backing and oracle weights use this complete book denominator. Nonfungible deployments are evaluated separately and the minimum reserve quality binds; every required deployment must be available.

Collateral quality q uses Backing, not the final grade, from the frozen previous accepted publication when the collateral's chain/token matches exactly one active Pharos asset. Tracked quality wins whether higher or lower than its class. No recursive evaluator, live replay catalog lookup or curator letter grade is used. Held, stale, missing or ineligible source scores fall back to the authored class, never zero. An accepted-publication source failure retains a separate null, zero-weight diagnostic and its evidence; a successfully observed book still scores its class fallback. Class qualities are native ETH 95; BTC wrappers, ETH LSTs and precious-metal receipts 85; yield/restaked ETH 80; major non-BTC/ETH L1 assets and wrapped/staking receipts 70; synthetic dollars, principal tokens and yield-bearing stables 55; structured credit and other/unknown 35. Unknown is bounded U, not measured loss. siUSD and PT-siUSD do not inherit iUSD's Backing through intermediary identities.

For collateralized markets, fractional LLTV l gives transmission t = l² and lender quality L = 100 − t × (100 − q). With deployed claim weights w, Q = Σ(w × L). Effective parent Backing P blends as R = min(P, idleShare × P + deployedShare × P × Q / 100). Higher LLTV transmits more collateral-quality deficit without asserting actual borrower LTV equals LLTV. Genuine collateral-free debt-bearing lending transmits fully; verified zero-parameter idle sentinels remain idle. A complete all-idle book retains P with Q not applicable; zero NAV is unavailable. Both inherited reserve and concentration contributions use R without double charging or a favorable generic-floor escape. These are dimensionless methodology scores, not probabilities of loss, recovery forecasts or financial assurance. Capital reuse, parent caps, mechanism evidence, Exit, peg, pillar weights and grade bands are unchanged.

Complete same-parent multi-slice reserve envelopes are admitted only for this Morpho lender lane: reserve status and every slice must be required, known and live, all slices must map to the sole serial wrapper parent, finite nonnegative weights must sum to one within 10^-6 and residual weights must be zero. Existing cycle, wrapper-edge, parent availability and dependency checks remain. Effective reserve quality is min(previous generic reserve quality, lender-adjusted quality), retaining generic structural reasons, unresolved evidence and rateability. Only the binding path scores; the nonbinding factors are zero-weight diagnostics, not a second haircut. Unavailable books keep the prior generic numeric path and non-Morpho behavior is unchanged.

The live on-chain producer runs in the existing four-hour ERC-4626 reserve adapter, not score preparation. Root/parent identities, current adapters/queues, accrued claims and idle cash are finalized-block-pinned and exactly reconciled. Source pins, acquisition clocks, raw amounts, LLTV, code/account/core identities and typed outcomes persist in existing reserve metadata and frozen input. The older of block and acquisition clocks must fit the inclusive eight-hour book budget; previous accepted collateral scores independently need a current, identity-matched publication within one hour. Missing, partial, failed, stale, future-dated, unsupported or unreconciled books keep pre-v10.19 reserve scoring (inherited-parent or generic multi-slice, as applicable) with a null, zero-weight unavailable book diagnostic, not measured safe-book credit. Valid denomination/redemption observations survive book failure. Successfully measured unknown collateral instead contributes U35. Old captures remain explicitly legacy-unobserved; no D1 migration, endpoint, public score field or API-wrapper change is needed.

Track record follows the newest required implementation layer across a variant and its parent chain, using each layer's implementation launch date or product launch fallback. Exit compares every admissible route alone and every independently usable pair, then takes the best feasible portfolio. The stronger member is primary; its backup adds min(10, 100 − primary score) × backup score / 100. An improving correlated alternative cannot displace a better independent pair. Capacity, output, fees, access, settlement and shared-resource checks still apply. Capacity, completion, confidence and binding cap stay visible; zero Exit means no viable exit path was measured. Atomic protocol routes follow their reviewed settlement fact, not an assumed delay.

Genuine unresearched or undisclosed whole-exit uncertainty remains bounded at 35 where the policy permits it, not measured zero and not an Exit-wide ceiling. Proven pipeline or curation gaps are excluded rather than charged. The bounded floor is applied after portfolio selection; evidence explanations do not switch on or off when the floor binds. ## Dependency Exposure Lookup

Exposure mode on the [Dependency Map](/dependency-map/?mode=exposure) finds coins linked to selected upstream assets through mapped collateral and wrapper relationships. Linked coins; not a loss forecast. It does not estimate losses or changes in Safety Scores.

The lookup uses the full published edge set, regardless of Explore Focus, Type, Limit, or small-link visibility. Selected roots are listed separately and excluded from dependent counts and USD totals. Direct dependents are one link away; indirect dependents are reached through longer chains.

Each root starts with a share of 100%. Basket relationships sum mapped weights multiplied by their upstream look-through shares. Serial relationships take the maximum upstream share, not a sum. When both channels apply, the lookup takes the larger of the serial maximum and the basket sum. Each dependent appears once across selected roots. A published shared backing book counts once in USD totals; direct and indirect totals can still include the same underlying value at several layers, so the headline states that overlap. These gross layer totals are not additive independent holdings.

USD equals the mapped share multiplied by circulating USD at evaluation from the accepted publication. The result names its generation, methodology version, publication time, and supply clock, and labels held snapshots. It never silently substitutes current market cap. When evaluation supply was not published, USD remains unknown with the reason 'Supply at evaluation not published for this generation'. Unknown supply is not zero: the row remains visible and known-USD totals exclude its unavailable amount.

Coverage is limited to published relationships and their measured or reviewed weights, which may differ from current composition. Unknown weights and cycles leave shares unresolved rather than guessed or clamped. Role dependencies are listed as 'Role dependencies (not drawn)'; 'Known, not in the scored graph' relationships are outside the lookup and totals. An absent coverage field means not published, not no gaps. Peg, liquidity, confidence, and other transmission channels are not modeled. No mapped downstream exposure found does not establish that other dependencies are absent.

History examples are [case-study links](/learn/case-studies/), not runnable presets or a historical replay.

Earlier-generation results are explicitly labelled; stale or unavailable results withhold modeled numbers. NR remains NR. Modeled with the production Safety Score evaluator; not a forecast. ## Liquidity Score

TVL depth uses log-scale scoring so small assets can improve without requiring blue-chip depth, while very deep pools still receive credit. Volume rewards active markets. Pool quality adjusts for mechanism type, pool balance, pair quality, and risky counterparties. Durability measures persistence across observations, and pair diversity penalizes concentration in one venue or one unstable route.

## Redemption Backstop Route Score

The standalone Redemption Backstop score rates an issuer or protocol redemption route from 0 to 100. It composes access (20%), settlement (15%), execution certainty (15%), capacity (25%), output-asset quality (15%), and cost (10%), then applies route-family, eligibility, delay, queue, minimum-redemption, severe-depeg, freshness, and evidence constraints.

Its modeled capacity request is 5% of supply, floored at $100,000 and capped at $25 million. Capacity blends percent-of-supply coverage with absolute executable dollars so a small percentage can still receive bounded credit without pretending it satisfies the full holder request.

Since v4.39, a measured route scores zero when executable capacity is zero or below both the 1% completion and $100,000 absolute breakpoints; missing capacity remains unrated, and the same materiality gate applies to the eventual-redeemability headline. Reviewed settlement overrides are shared by the standalone row and V9, with favorable corrections subject to the 365-day evidence window. Reserve-sync routes publish full-supply eventual capacity only when dated route evidence explicitly opts into that claim.

Since v4.4, an open route with an unproven settlement completion bound publishes unestablished capacity and remains unrated rather than being scored zero.

Since v4.42, market-implied degradation on an open live downside incident requires a fresh authoritative current signed deviation at or below -2500 bps. The incident's historical peak does not establish present severity: when current evidence is stale, missing, cached, untrusted, or lacks an authoritative peg reference, the route publishes unknown / market-implied and remains impaired with its score withheld; a fresh non-severe deviation releases only this overlay without closing the incident.

Since v4.44, Noon's USN route is modeled as an issuer-processed, days-settled rail with an exact 7-calendar-day bound taken from the five-Business-Day processing clause and rules-based-nav execution, while sUSN's request/claim rail publishes a live on-chain settlement bound read from the WithdrawalHandler's own withdrawPeriod() (604,800 seconds today, behind a 48-hour timelock and retroactive to in-flight requests) with capacity measured from the vault's idle USN as live-direct-bounded. A documented-bound route's observation also uses its same-run chain-read time as observedAt when the observation carries a direct freshness kind, with the reviewed date as the fallback.

This route score is separate from Safety Score V10 Exit. The two share reviewed route-scoring primitives, but V10 re-evaluates exact same-notional evidence under its own stress request, evidence ceilings, danger interlocks, and independent-backup policy.

Methodology v4.49 keeps fractional observed fees unrounded through the existing 10, 50 and 100 basis-point cost thresholds and scenario evaluation. Rejected payout identities cannot grant favorable live fees, openness, holder eligibility or settlement; independently reviewed static fees and sourced adverse status remain separate. sBOLD requires readable collateral-health evidence for open status, Mento requires same-block execution predicates plus a bounded successful quote for capacity, and dEURO basket valuation retains the oldest complete member-price/FX clock. Physical commodity outputs require explicit source-timed USD valuation distinct from fiat proceeds without bypassing Safety Score commodity admission. These repairs can move route scores; weights and ladders are unchanged, and fixed-clock comparison plus owner review remain release prerequisites.

Redemption v4.5 withdraws unsupported capacity, completion and eligibility assumptions on reviewed routes. Avalon, Youves, hyUSD and wARS retain unquantified capacity; Solayer's USDC holder path is queued QLP fulfillment, not atomic redemption. pathUSD processing terms do not guarantee completed payout. The existing-route review grants no new config-only bound. Safety v10.18 smaller-probe receipts prove only executed input, not full-request completion or exhaustion. Weights and ladders remain unchanged; fresh observations and final fixed-clock comparison remain required. Activation: 2026-10-14 12:40 UTC (release cut; 1791981600).

MetaMorpho V1 donations cannot replace validated API capacity, and Liquity v1 gross ActivePool debt is a backing diagnostic rather than executable capacity. GBPA and MXNT retain unquantified capacity; FILQ and Sygnum FIUSD cash output is distinct from NAV share pricing. strUSD separates permissionless submission from issuer-discretionary holder eligibility. Weights and ladders are unchanged; fixed-clock comparison and fresh producer observations remain required. Activation is provisional for 2026-10-16 00:00 UTC, to be re-dated at release.


## Mint/Burn Flow Scoring

Mint/burn flow scoring tracks issuance and redemption pressure across supported token contracts. Pharos classifies transfers into mint, burn, bridge-mint, bridge-burn, atomic roundtrip, and ignored noise so the gauge reflects meaningful supply movement instead of mechanical churn.

The score compares recent net flow against trailing closed-day baselines, distinguishes canonical-chain activity from bridge effects, and flags pressure shifts when risky outflows and safer inflows diverge.

Mint/Burn v6.26 adds reviewed Ethereum BRSRV, CHFSAFO and GLDon issuance sources to the extended lane with explicit start blocks and token thresholds. GLDon uses its reviewed OFT adapter binding; other chains are not implicitly covered. Pressure formulas, closed-hour windows, valuation and gauge weights are unchanged. Configured history is not completed backfill or a mature observed baseline.

Mint/Burn v6.27 publishes the literal 24-hour minting-pressure balance only when both mint and burn volumes are fully valued; a side with unpriced or unrecorded events makes it NR with a reason, because a balance of lower bounds proves nothing. The aggregate response adds an optional per-coin gauge weight. Pressure formulas, valuation gates, gauge weighting and bands are unchanged.

Mint/Burn v6.29 uses one common fully scanned closed-hour watermark within the existing freshness budget for all public windows and baselines. Before the fixed October 20, 2026 noon UTC cutover, historical cursor/event assurance is explicitly labelled legacy-pre-cutover; later hours still require current-fingerprint v2 proof. Dirty materialization and missing valuation remain unavailable. The transition expires after 90 days plus the watermark budget. The gauge retains its admitted-coin weighted mean, with explicit incomplete scan membership rather than an all-roster veto or zero-filled excluded inputs.

Coverage is intentionally explicit. Unsupported chains, deferred configs, null-price repair, and stale lanes are surfaced as metadata rather than hidden behind a clean-looking aggregate. Quiet assets retain mature coverage from completed block-scan evidence even after old event rows age out.

Mint/Burn v6.24 aligns N-hour windows to N closed UTC hours ending at the current hour boundary. ## Yield Intelligence

Yield Intelligence resolves stablecoin yield from direct on-chain reads, curated pools, protocol APIs, price-derived NAV movement, and rate-derived sources. The pipeline prefers precise sources and only uses fallback tiers when identity and exposure remain unambiguous.

Pharos computes effective yield by comparing APY against the relevant cash or peg benchmark, then combines source-risk-adjusted yield efficiency with sustainability to produce a Pharos Yield Score (PYS). High APY is not automatically good: unstable rates, weak safety scores, low TVL, source-risk penalties, or ambiguous exposure reduce the recommendation quality.

External opportunities — lending markets, fixed-yield products, and structured tranches — are scored at the market level. The underlying stablecoin's Safety Score is one input to an opportunity-level safety score that also weighs reviewed venue risk, market size, observed utilization, and access or withdrawal constraints. Missing critical market evidence produces NR rather than a precise-looking score, and the underlying coin's own Report Card is never altered.

Warnings explain why a venue is risky, missing, stale, modeled, or benchmark-adjusted so yield pages do not promote fragile opportunities as clean income. Calculation mode is separate from evidence class: deterministic proxy math is still estimated evidence. Expired or critically incomplete evidence remains visible as NR context but cannot carry an exact current PYS, and new history points retain the versioned formula inputs needed for exact recomputation.

Yield v8.48 requires receipt-symbol ownership alongside address evidence, rejects contradictory underlying sets and prevents generic PT/LP attribution to wrappers. Royco requires critical market, reviewed-venue and measured-size evidence, and cannot replace explicit NR or missing underlying safety with a rated tranche grade; incomplete otherwise eligible rows retain APY with null PYS. Signed holder returns preserve losing constituents in weighted denominators. Midas uses oracle observation intervals, Zephyr counts each hourly observation once across publication/backfill, and Hashnote excludes reports beyond five-minute future skew. Structured benchmarks retain unknown clocks, and EFFR admission applies observation bounds before provider fallback. PYS weights and freshness budgets are unchanged.

Yield v8.49 admits XGLD's reviewed BNB Chain exchange-rate provider to the existing Tier 1 reader. XAUt per XGLD measures issuer-administered gold-denominated NAV separately from gold's USD appreciation, not reconciled strategy profit. The auxiliary provider supplies no measured TVL; existing comparison-anchor, freshness, sanity and publication gates remain. PYS weights and benchmark selection are unchanged, and source admission alone does not establish a current APY.


## PegScore and Depeg Early Warning Score (DEWS)

PegScore measures historical peg quality from time-at-peg and event severity. The tracking window starts at a reviewed replay-coverage date when one exists, otherwise it is capped by asset age or the earliest durable observation. Pharos also publishes a coverage-aware recent 90-day companion without treating unobserved days as stable.

DEWS is a forward-looking stress score. It combines price deviation, source divergence, liquidity erosion, pool imbalance, supply velocity, blacklist activity, mint/burn pressure, and yield anomalies into a 0-100 warning signal.

Pending depegs require source-family-aware corroboration before promotion. Pharos treats contradictory evidence as a reason to hold or reject an event, not as weak support, and it records canonical source keys behind every confirmed mutation.

Shared Depeg/DEWS v6.33 admits the nearest positive-TVL weekly DEX-history row with confidence >=0.5 inside an inclusive 36-hour tolerance, retaining its original score/TVL/date. Missing eligible evidence leaves both history anchors unavailable; weak public history and >=0.75 durability/digest gates remain unchanged. Input availability, DEWS scores and bands may change. Release requires owner-approved production-history replay; depeg and PegScore rules are unchanged and no replay result is claimed.

Shared Depeg/DEWS v6.34 keeps native/USD quote domains comparable, excludes rejected pool peaks and applies the opposing-group vote even to the single-$5M-pool exception. Historical floors use USD-valued supply and at-peg classification uses raw trigger precision. Trusted DEX-only divergence remains score-bearing; stale or invalid foundational stablecoins or retained PSI evidence holds accepted publication. Canonical projection failures withhold recomputation, complete eligible history underpins Worst Depeg, and historical diagnostics disclose unevaluable events and partial coverage. Input changes can move event histories and DEWS scores/bands; weights and numeric thresholds are unchanged.

DEWS mint/burn inputs use complete 24-hour and 30-day windows at the published common closed-hour watermark, including explicitly admitted legacy pre-cutover windows with their weaker assurance. Both windows and dirty-hour checks share that end; original-window freshness and valuation gates remain. Restored evidence can change scores and bands in either direction without changing formulas or weights; unavailable evidence is never calm.


## Depeg Duration Resolver

When Pharos confirms an active depeg, the Depeg Duration Resolver (DDR) answers two questions at the public forecast lock: will it come back, and if so, when. Stage 1 emits an ordinal Resolution Outlook (Recovery Likely, At Risk, Recovery Unlikely, or Insufficient Signal) from transparent kill signals and recovery anchors over the coin's structure and the depeg's fingerprint. It is a calibrated mechanistic rubric, not fitted machine learning, because the terminal-label corpus is too thin to train a supervised classifier.

DDRv4 uses a forecast-readiness-or-72h contract. Active confirmed incidents show live facts before lock, then the first healthy run with readiness score strictly greater than 0.75 freezes exactly one official prediction or no-call for the canonical incident. If readiness never triggers first, the first healthy run at or after 72h seals through the backstop. Health failures defer the lock rather than creating no-calls or predictions.

Stage 2 runs only when Stage 1 is not terminal-leaning. It is an empirical landmark-survival estimate over the clean corpus of recovered incidents, conditioned on the depeg's structural stratum (depth, direction, structural class, and peg currency) most-dependable-first. It reports a median time-to-repeg with a typical range (15th-85th percentile) plus per-horizon resolution-likelihood cells, support-gated and Wilson-bounded so thin cells show their support state instead of a fabricated number.

The Depeg Duration Resolver Reviewer (DDRR) scores frozen first-published predictions against later canonical depeg-event outcomes and reports coverage accountability for no-calls, pre-lock recoveries, terminal-before-lock outcomes, missed locks, publication retries/failures, data-quality gaps, invalidated rows, and legacy sticky 24h outcomes. Rollout-active incidents that predate the DDRv2 public contract use that boundary for fair coverage classification. Recovery-likelihood accuracy and duration error use only scoreable public predictions.

DDR v4.7 uses timed onset/current-deviation evidence, not final peaks backdated into earlier duration landmarks. Sparse temporal support can suppress duration bands until recalibration and can move new assessment scores or forecast verdicts, while sealed predictions remain unchanged. Audit-ineligible histories are removed before the row cap; nominal prices and unavailable/nonpositive supply cannot manufacture live at-peg inputs. Reviewer accuracy-policy selection is independent of whole-universe coverage. Per-coin track records use producer aggregates before the browse cap; retained snapshots without them show unavailable until refreshed.

DDR consumes the same confirmed depeg events as the detection pipeline; it does not run its own detection. Forecast readiness is a publication trigger, not a probability or confidence level, and DDR is not investment advice or a credit rating.

The dedicated compute-depeg-resolver scheduled producer precomputes DDR every 15 minutes, independently of sync-stablecoins; readers serve the result from D1 cache.


## Blacklist Tracker Methodology

The blacklist tracker monitors issuer-controlled freeze, blocklist, account-pause, and token-destruction events across supported centralized stablecoin contracts.

Events are normalized by chain, stablecoin, action type, native amount, USD amount, and amount-status provenance. Recoverable amount gaps are queued for repair, while permanently unavailable rows remain auditable without polluting public aggregates.

Frozen-total summaries use last-known successful freeze-ledger snapshots. New snapshots are contract/config scoped so same-symbol deployments do not overwrite each other; legacy rows can fall back to older address identity until remediated.

FreezeWatch uses the reviewed four-status model: Yes, Possible, Upstream and No. Measure each deployment against the material-supply threshold (currently 10%); never aggregate sub-threshold deployments. Measure missing shares before treating unresolved deployments as material. Yes requires a current arbitrary-holder-targeting power, including enforced allowlists/sanctions oracles, active exit gates or arbitrary-holder burns. An admin able to assign denylist state directly to a victim already has that power.

Without Yes, Possible covers global transfer pauses, all admin/DAO-upgradeable token implementations regardless of delay, grantable empty/incapable operator roles, dormant configurable transfer/exit gates, a vault/share's own globally pausable redemption/withdrawal, material canonical-bridge burner surfaces and issuer-shipped consensus halts. Deposit-only gates, abdicated setters, protocol-global redemption or peripheral PSM/swap switches, mint-only authority and increase-only rebases are not holder-facing controls.

Upstream requires no direct Yes/Possible plus a holder claim on an exposed declared parent, full CEX reserve custody, or strictly more than 50% of measured external backing terminating in issuer/custodian control. Qualifying collateral counts categorically at full share; DAO-only collateral controls do not count. Custodial fiat, bank/fund/T-bill claims and allocated physical commodity qualify. CDP/lending books use debt-weighted exposure; reserve-backed tokens use published composition. Split LP/basket rows by constituent value; exclude the coin and its own wrappers/savings shares from both numerator and denominator because they are not external backing. Different tokens from the same issuer count at their own status. Unmeasurable reserves never establish Upstream. No means no test establishes exposure, not proof unresolved reserves are risk-free.

Reviews cite deployments, supply shares, current authorities and reachable holder-targeting paths, pinned decisive reads, and any parent or measured reserve basis with denominator. Possible and Upstream describe exposure, not an asserted active blacklist on the token. The full decision procedure is at https://github.com/TokenBrice/pharos-watch/blob/main/docs/blacklist-tracker.md#freezewatch-decision-procedure.

Blacklist Tracker v4.3 counts canonical EVM logs and Tron byte identities once. Bounded progress is not caught-up coverage and cannot renew success/publication clocks; durable maintenance debt retries skipped or ambiguous snapshots. Unknown USD remains unavailable and mixed totals disclose partial valuation coverage, including all canonical tracked gold cohorts. Historical repair requires event-time quotes, ordering requires execution evidence, and RPC timestamps require matching block hashes. Failed observations and explicit gold zero substitutions retain distinct provenance.


## Chain Health Score

Chain Health Score evaluates how healthy each chain's stablecoin stack is. It combines supply-weighted Safety Score quality, chain environment, stablecoin concentration, peg stability, and RWA/crypto backing diversity.

The score is computed from current stablecoin data and only a fresh, accepted V10 publication rather than a separate opaque dataset. Missing, held, stale, or invalid V10 leaves Safety quality and the composite NR; there is no V8 or stale-score fallback. A chain with large supply but one dominant issuer, weak backing, poor peg behavior, or narrow backing diversity can score below a smaller but more diversified chain.

Chain Health is intended as market-structure context: it helps users understand whether a chain's stablecoin liquidity is deep, resilient, and diversified enough to support activity during stress.

## Definitions

- **Algorithmic Stablecoin**: A stablecoin that uses on-chain mint/burn rules (not 1:1 backing) to defend its peg. Generally riskier; UST/Terra was the canonical failure.
- **AMO (Algorithmic Market Operations)**: Smart contracts that programmatically mint or burn stablecoins to manage the peg without direct user redemption.
- **Attestation**: A periodic statement, typically by an audit firm, that the issuer's reserves match circulating supply at a point in time. Weaker than a full audit.
- **Bank Run Gauge**: A market-cap-weighted composite of each tracked stablecoin's Pressure Shift: a signed -100 to +100 signal. Scores below -10 indicate worsening redemption pressure; scores ≥ +10 indicate improving issuance pressure. It measures unusual redemption pressure, not literal net direction, and is unavailable when data is insufficient.
- **Basis points (bps)**: The unit Pharos uses for peg deviation: 100 basis points (bps) equal 1%. Depeg values are the peak signed deviation from the target peg during the event window.
- **Basis Trade**: Capturing the spread between spot and futures, typically by holding spot and shorting perps. Yield = the funding rate paid by perp longs.
- **Blacklist Tracker**: Pharos's record of issuer interventions (freeze, unfreeze, pause, block, destroy) drawn from on-chain freeze-ledger events on supported centralized stablecoin contracts, normalized by chain, action, native amount, and USD amount. Published as FreezeWatch and on each coin's page; events outside the supported asset set are excluded.
- **Bluechip**: Pharos's editorial roster of stablecoins that are A-tier in both the external Bluechip reference rating and the Pharos Safety Score. It is an intersection of two current feeds, not an average or a separate floor model: if either input stops clearing A-tier, the asset leaves the roster.
- **Bridge**: Infrastructure that lets a stablecoin move between chains. Carries smart-contract and validator risk that the issuer often doesn't control.
- **CDP (Collateralized Debt Position)**: A vault that mints stablecoins against locked crypto collateral, liquidated if collateral value falls below a threshold.
- **Chain Health Score**: A 0-100 score for the quality of a blockchain's stablecoin ecosystem, weighting five factors: supply-weighted Safety Score quality, chain environment, supply concentration, peg stability, and backing diversity. Chains whose supply is concentrated in few assets score lower.
- **Custody**: Who physically holds the backing assets. Institutional custodians (BNY Mellon, BlackRock, Copper, Ceffu) carry different counterparty risk profiles.
- **DDR (Depeg Duration Resolver)**: For each confirmed depeg incident, DDR publishes a Resolution Outlook (Recovery Likely, At Risk, Recovery Unlikely, or Insufficient Signal) from transparent mechanistic rules and, when recovery is plausible, an Expected Duration band drawn from Pharos's corpus of recovered depegs. Each incident gets one immutable prediction, sealed at forecast readiness or the 72-hour backstop. It is a structural read, not a probability or investment advice.
- **DDRR (Depeg Duration Resolver Reviewer)**: The audit companion to DDR. It compares each sealed, first-published DDR prediction with the incident's later canonical outcome, and reports coverage (how many incidents received a call) separately from accuracy (recovery-call accuracy, duration error, and horizon calibration). It never re-runs today's resolver over old events.
- **Delta-Neutral**: A position constructed so small price moves in the underlying don't change its value. Synthetic-dollar stablecoins like USDe use this design.
- **Depeg**: A deviation from peg that crosses the detection threshold (100 bps for USD pegs, 150 bps for other pegs) and passes multi-source confirmation. Contradictory secondary evidence is treated as contradiction, not weak support. Events with a peak deviation of 500 bps or more get a dedicated incident page.
- **Dependency Risk**: How a stablecoin's reserves or mechanism rely on other stablecoins. Dependencies are causal inputs to the Safety Score rather than a standalone score: a serial wrapper cannot score above its parent, and basket exposure is weighted and bounded by reviewed materiality.
- **DEWS (Depeg Early Warning System)**: A forward-looking per-coin stress score from 0 to 100, recomputed every 30 minutes from 8 weighted sub-signals: Supply Velocity, Pool Balance Drift, Liquidity Erosion, Price Confidence, Cross-Source Divergence, Blacklist Activity, Mint/Burn Flow, Yield Anomaly. Weak PSI and same-peg contagion can amplify the score before it lands in a band. It is a normalized stress score, not a calibrated probability.
- **DEWS bands**: The five DEWS zones, from cool to hot: Calm up to 15; Watch up to 35; Alert up to 55; Warning up to 75; Danger above 75. Band names are proper nouns in Pharos copy; lowercase “calm” or “watch” is ordinary English and never names a band.
- **Effective TVL**: TVL adjusted for mechanism quality, balance health, and pair quality to estimate usable exit depth under stress.
- **Freezable**: A stablecoin is freezable when its issuer or protocol admin can freeze, block, seize, or destroy user balances through resolved on-chain controls. Pharos also distinguishes upstream exposure (a collateral or parent asset can be frozen) and possible exposure (mutable or pause-capable admin surfaces without confirmed address-level freezing). Freezability is a trust and centralization risk, not an instant harm.
- **Funding Rate**: Periodic payment between perpetual-futures longs and shorts that keeps the perp price tracking spot. Positive = longs pay shorts.
- **Liquidation**: Forced closure of an overleveraged CDP or perp position when collateral falls below the safety threshold. Common in CDP-backed stablecoins.
- **Liquidity Score**: A 0-100 score for how easily a stablecoin can be exited through DEX markets, combining TVL Depth (30%), Volume (20%), Pool Quality (20%), Durability (20%), Diversity (10%). Thin, stale, or identity-poor pools stay visible for diagnostics but receive no scoring weight. It is an aggregate market score, not a single-route execution test.
- **Mint Authority Score**: The 0-100 mint component inside the Safety Score's Economic Control pillar: how much durable, unbacked supply privileged mint paths can create. Higher is better. Since methodology v9.1 mint risk is graded once, inside Economic Control; the former standalone score is retired. Missing or unresolved review data stays NR and never implies safety.
- **Mint/Burn Flows**: Net mint-versus-burn flow on a stablecoin's supported issuance-chain contracts. Transfers are classified into mint, burn, bridge-mint, bridge-burn, atomic round-trip, and ignored noise, so the flow reflects meaningful supply movement rather than mechanical churn. Raw direction and pressure versus the coin's own baseline are related but separate metrics.
- **Money-Market Fund (MMF)**: A SEC-registered short-duration fund holding T-bills, repos, and cash. Used as the reserve vehicle for several major fiat-backed stablecoins.
- **Overcollateralization**: Holding more collateral than the value of stablecoins issued, so a price drop in collateral doesn't immediately undercollateralize the system.
- **Peg Score**: A 0-100 score for a stablecoin's historical peg behavior, combining time-at-peg (50%) and event severity (50%), minus penalties for active depegs and erratic behavior. It needs at least 7 days of tracking; scores under 30 days are marked early. NAV tokens return NR because they are not meant to hold a fixed price. Peg Score enters the Safety Score as a multiplier.
- **Perpetual Future**: A derivatives contract with no expiry, kept aligned to spot via a periodic funding-rate payment between longs and shorts.
- **Pressure Shift**: A signed score comparing a stablecoin's current 24-hour mint/burn pressure with its last 30 fully closed daily baselines. Positive means stronger-than-normal mint pressure; negative means stronger-than-normal redemption pressure.
- **Proof of Reserves**: Issuer-published evidence — independent audit, real-time on-chain feed, or self-reported attestation — that circulating supply is matched by reserve assets.
- **PSI (Pharos Stability Index)**: An ecosystem-wide condition score from 0 to 100, recomputed every 30 minutes. It subtracts penalties for active depeg severity, depeg breadth, and DEWS stress breadth, then adds a bounded market-cap trend term. The result maps into 6 condition bands from BEDROCK to MELTDOWN. PSI is conservative by design: one small depeg should not move the index, but broad simultaneous stress should.
- **PSM (Peg Stability Module)**: A protocol module that lets users swap one stablecoin for another at a fixed rate, defending the peg through arbitrage.
- **PYS (Pharos Yield Score)**: Yield per unit of risk, from 0 to 100. It starts from APY, adds a weighted slice of benchmark spread, re-bases the local hurdle onto the USD risk-free rate, then discounts by source risk, stablecoin safety, and yield consistency. It is not a recommendation or a safety verdict: a low-grade coin can rank high because it pays a lot for a lot of risk.
- **Redemption**: Exchanging stablecoin tokens for the underlying asset (e.g., 1 USDC -> $1 USD wired to your bank) directly with the issuer.
- **Redemption Route Score**: A standalone score for a stablecoin's issuer or protocol redemption route, rated on access, settlement, execution certainty, capacity, output quality, and cost. It is a diagnostic: the Safety Score Exit pillar separately re-evaluates exact same-notional route evidence under its own policy.
- **RWA (Real-World Asset)**: Off-chain assets — typically U.S. Treasuries, repos, or bank deposits — held as backing for an on-chain stablecoin.
- **Safety Score**: Pharos's V10 risk grade for a stablecoin. It evaluates Backing (40%), Exit (35%), and Economic Control (25%) with bounded aggregation, so a strong pillar cannot average away a weak material path. Peg behavior, dependencies, wrappers, evidence quality, track record, and structural caps can then only constrain the result. Grades run from A+ (87+) to F (below 40); NR means required evidence is insufficient.
- **U.S. Treasury**: Short-dated U.S. government debt — the lowest-credit-risk USD-denominated reserve asset, used as the dominant backing for fiat-pegged stablecoins.
- **Yield-Bearing Stablecoin**: A stablecoin whose units accrue value over time rather than always trading at the peg. Examples: sUSDe, sUSDS, USDM.
