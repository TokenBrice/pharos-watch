# Live Reserve Sync

Dedicated documentation for the live reserve-composition subsystem that powers `GET /api/stablecoin-reserves/:id`, the stablecoin-detail reserve card, and `/status` reserve-sync health.

## Overview

- **Cron:** `sync-live-reserves` (`worker/src/cron/sync-live-reserves.ts`)
- **Schedule:** `11 */4 * * *` (every 4 hours at :11 UTC)
- **Shared 4-hourly lane:** after live reserve sync, the same slot runs redemption backstop sync, Kinesis supply sync, and the named `reserve-post-sync-watchdog` child for collateral-drift cache updates and stale-source alerts (`worker/src/handlers/scheduled/hourly-live-reserves.ts`)
- **Current coverage:** derived from `liveReservesConfig` in stablecoin metadata and the adapter declarations. Use those sources and the generated coverage reports for the current enabled, configured, bound, and unbound sets.
- **Storage:** `reserve_composition`, `reserve_composition_history`, `reserve_sync_state`, `reserve_sync_attempt_history`
- **API:** `GET /api/stablecoin-reserves/:id`
- **Frontend consumers:** `useStablecoinReserves()`, stablecoin detail view model, `/status` reserve-sync health
- **Operational telemetry:** `sync-live-reserves` emits per-coin progress into `cron_run_progress`, including the current coin, adapter, breaker key, and running synced / failed / skipped counters

This pipeline is intentionally separate from curated reserve metadata in `StablecoinMeta.reserves`. Historically, Safety v5.8 through v8.17 used score-grade snapshots for `collateralFromLive`, collateral-quality, and Dependency Risk inputs. Current V9 instead compiles accepted fresh, clean, independent live slices into Backing reserve exposures and reconciles tracked `coinId` slices into effective dependency edges; unmapped share remains self-backed/non-stablecoin exposure. A configured adapter is not enough by itself. When no accepted live rows are captured, V9 falls back to reviewed static/curated reserve rows rather than treating a degraded live attempt as score evidence. Adapter validation and stored-row decoding reject unknown or self-referential `coinId` links and `depType` without `coinId`; subject-aware adapters keep self-held reserve slices visible without turning them into dependency edges. Cyclic or invalid live dependency evidence falls back or fails closed according to the V9 fact-set contract while the reserve snapshot remains available for reserve/status surfaces.

Score-grade adapters may emit a namespace-qualified stable `sourceKey` for each source-owned reserve category. V9 joins a keyed live slice to reviewed metadata only when that key is unique on both sides; an explicit mismatch or collision fails closed. Historical rows without keys retain a unique normalized-name compatibility join. Every adapter output must pass the complete `ReserveSliceSchema` before replacing a persisted snapshot, including the source-key namespace syntax and 160-character limit; invalid output records a failed attempt and preserves the last good snapshot. Daily percentages are never part of slice identity: the live percentage remains the scoring and dependency weight, while the reviewed percentage is evidence about the reviewed composition rather than a join threshold.

Stable keys use source asset codes, token/feed addresses, or fixed source bucket identifiers, never display labels. The live producer and reviewed sidecar must be updated together. Aggregate NAV buckets for `tbill-openeden` and `wtgxx-wisdomtree` intentionally remain unkeyed: their reviewed compositions describe multiple underlying holdings, not the aggregate fund-share observation. Assigning the aggregate key to one constituent (or every constituent) would create a false or ambiguous classification join.

Assurance reports also retain source-native category keys when the reviewed sidecar is at a different level of detail: AUSD's combined cash review does not match separate fund/company cash observations, USDG's individual fund look-through rows do not match the aggregate government-MMF category, AUDD's aggregate deposits do not match individual bank accounts, and BRLV's bond/ETF aggregates do not match separate primary/secondary report rows. Newly disclosed PYUSD Treasury bills have no historical reviewed slice. These categories retain their source classifications without forcing a reviewed join; a composition review, rather than an identity migration, is required to reconcile the different scopes.

Adapters that can emit mapped dependency targets are enrolled in the reviewed mapping registry in `shared/data/coverage-dispositions/dependency-target-dispositions.ts`. Each adapter-level record identifies the implementation source files that were reviewed and records the reviewer, date, rationale, and source-file provenance. The dependency coverage gate rejects missing, stale, duplicate, or source-free adapter reviews when a report-card dependency set reports `live-reserve` as its base source. The registry attests to the adapter mapping rule; canonical target identities remain owned by the adapter and its per-coin configuration, so the registry does not invent per-target fingerprints or duplicate provenance onto every emitted live slice.

`sodax-sonic` reads bnUSD's Sonic SODAX borrower collateral, not the historical ICON-hub contract inventory. The issuer API supplies candidate addresses; their pinned `scaledBalanceOf` sum must exactly equal the non-transferable variable-debt token's `scaledTotalSupply`, otherwise the census fails closed. The pool's live reserve list (31 at the September 9 verification, not the earlier 24 nonzero holdings) determines every aToken read. At most 128 borrowers × 40 reserves plus source timestamps fit eight 650-call Multicall3 pages, run two at a time; two discovery batches precede them, with at most five reviewed oracle code checks and separate two-at-a-time supply diagnostics. Every positive reserve uses the pool Aave oracle price: zero/unreadable prices fail closed, and timestamps older than one hour degrade. Reviewed immutable fixed-price sources require exact runtime-code hashes; the two RedemptionOracle wrappers use their immutable redemption-rate feed's timestamp because their fixed USD leg has no timestamp method. Each live slice uses `sodax-sonic:<reserve-address>`; the reviewed aggregate tail intentionally remains unkeyed, and new/unmapped reserves remain quantified. The adapter is `weak-live-probe`, publishes `totalReserveUsd` but no collateralization ratio, and emits `supply-inventory-unverified` until the v2 global issuance/contract inventory is reviewed and reconciled; that review is the promotion prerequisite. Fixed-constant oracle legs (for example the USDT reserve priced by the immutable MockAggregator constant 1e8) are valued with the live DefiLlama quote, keeping the reviewed constant only as a fallback flagged `price-insensitive` in `details.priceSources`; the census fails closed when neither a live quote nor a readable oracle price is available.

## Metadata Contract

Every provenance field below is an evidence claim about the current run, not a feed-family label — rule R6 (ADR-33 in [architecture.md](./architecture.md#architectural-decision-records)), enforced for route attribution by `buildRedemptionSnapshotMetadata` in `worker/src/cron/reserve-adapters/redemption.ts`.

Live reserve support is declared per coin in `StablecoinMeta.liveReservesConfig` (`shared/types/live-reserves.ts`, loaded from `shared/data/stablecoins/coins/*.json` via `shared/lib/stablecoins/registry.ts` and validated by `shared/lib/stablecoins/schema.ts`).

`LiveReservesConfig` fields:

| Field              | Meaning                                                                               |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `adapter`          | Registered adapter key from `shared/lib/live-reserve-adapters.ts`                     |
| `version`          | Increment when adapter semantics or parsing change materially                         |
| `semantics`        | One of `collateral-mix`, `protocol-reserve`, `attestation-mix`, `single-asset`        |
| `breakerScope`     | Optional circuit-breaker grouping override; defaults to adapter key                   |
| `display`          | Optional UI/source link metadata (`url`, `label`)                                     |
| `inputs.primary`   | Primary source input                                                                  |
| `inputs.fallbacks` | Optional fallback inputs                                                              |
| `params`           | Adapter-specific validated settings enforced by the shared live-reserve config schema |
| `scoring`          | Optional per-coin scoring policy (`maxSourceAgeSec`, `allowedDegradedWarningCodes`)   |
| `suspended`        | Optional operator kill switch (`reason`, `since`, optional `reviewBy`, ISO dates)     |

A `suspended` marker is for dead or semantically unusable upstream feeds (for example a provider moving to keyed API access with no key issued, or a reachable feed whose measurement basis cannot represent the configured reserve composition). The registry strips a suspended `liveReservesConfig` at load (`shared/lib/stablecoins/registry.ts`), so every consumer — the sync queue, V9 backing evidence, presentation, and circuit breakers — treats the coin as having no live feed and falls back to curated reserve evidence. The source file keeps the full adapter config, so re-enabling is a one-line revert. First use: the seven `m0`-adapter coins on 2026-08-19, after M0's Protocol API moved to keyed access; re-enabled on 2026-08-20 once an issuer API key (`M0_API_KEY`) was configured and the adapter was reworked against the surviving keyed schema. USDXL uses the same fail-closed mechanism because its branch feed measures lending-pool cash instead of gross collateral and omits the separate USDT0 PSM facilitator.

All configured source URLs must be absolute URLs. The schema enforces this for HTTP/indexer inputs, `display.url`, and adapter-specific URL params such as RPC endpoints, dashboard APIs, and liquidity endpoints. Adapter definitions also declare their supported `semantics` values and config `version` set, so metadata changes cannot silently pair an adapter with unsupported reserve semantics.

### Registry-Defined Adapter Classes

`shared/types/live-reserve-adapter-declarations.ts` is the single authoring surface: it owns each adapter key, its Zod params schema (referenced directly by the declaration entry, not through a string identifier or a separate schema module), accepted primary input kinds, config validation policy, public source definition, provenance status, and display badge metadata. Shared `validation` tiers are named constants in `shared/types/live-reserve-adapter-policy.ts` so adapters on the same policy share one definition. `shared/lib/live-reserve-adapter-descriptors.ts` enriches the declarations and publishes `LIVE_RESERVE_ADAPTER_DEFINITIONS` as the single map for config, provenance, display, and adapter consumers; the Worker's `ReserveAdapterDefinition` is a `Pick<>` of the declaration type rather than a hand-copied structural type. `LIVE_RESERVE_ADAPTER_KEYS` and `LiveReserveAdapterKey` remain declaration-derived key views. Five important definition properties are not user-configured per coin:

| Property              | Meaning                                                                                                      |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `sourceModel`         | Distinguishes `dynamic-mix`, `validated-static`, and `single-bucket` reserve shapes                          |
| `evidenceClass`       | Distinguishes scoring-eligible `independent` feeds from `static-validated` and `weak-live-probe` feeds       |
| `sourceOriginClass`   | Records who produced the evidence without changing its admission, liveness, or scoring strength             |
| `sharedSourceMode`    | Distinguishes per-coin fetches (`none`) from explicitly source-invariant result sharing (`source-invariant`) |
| `redemptionTelemetry` | Declares whether the adapter can emit direct/proxy redemption capacity and current-fee telemetry             |

- `dynamic-mix`: independently measured reserve compositions. These can be `independent` evidence for scoring when the retained snapshot passes admission.
- `validated-static`: live validation/probe adapters over curated/static slices. These remain authoritative for the reserve detail API, but they are tagged `static-validated` and do not count as independent live collateral inputs for report-card scoring.
- `single-bucket`: one-slice live proofs/attestations. Some are true independent evidence (`anzen-usdz`, `astherus-earn-wrapper`, `blast-usdb-yield-manager`, `btcfi`, `chainlink-nav`, `chainlink-por`, `chronicle-nav`, `erc4626-single-asset`, `escrow-balance`, `hive-hbd-protocol`, `initia-wrapper-vault`, `liquity-native-active-pool`, `liquity-v1`, `m0`, `m0-wrapper-underlying`, `sgforge-coinvertible`, `spiko-api`, `superstate-liquidity`, `united-por`, `usd1-bundle-oracle`, `usdai-hub`, `yamato`), while weak liveness-only or proof-class summary feeds such as `single-asset`, `solstice-attestation`, and `river-protocol-info` are tagged `weak-live-probe`. wiTRY (`witry-brix`) binds `erc4626-single-asset` over its Ethereum ERC-4626 staking wrapper.
- `independent`: scoring-eligible live evidence when the retained snapshot is fresh, authoritative, config-matched, and has no unexcused snapshot degradation; a later failed attempt does not revoke it.
- `static-validated`, `weak-live-probe`: detail/status-visible evidence classes that never override curated collateral scoring. Nested same-run redemption telemetry (`live-direct`, `live-direct-bounded`, `live-queue`, `live-proxy-validated`, or `documented-bound` with `same-run-onchain`, `same-run-api`, or `verified-source-timestamp`) may still bound the holder-facing exit route when the snapshot class is `weak-live-probe`; legacy `immediateRedeemableUsd` on that class stays rejected.
- `sourceOriginClass` is orthogonal to `evidenceClass`. It records `issuer-attested`, `onchain-observation`, `independent-assurance`, `reviewed-curation`, or `unknown` for diagnostic provenance. Unreviewed adapters resolve explicitly to `unknown`; the registry never guesses origin from an adapter name or input kind. Origin does not change reserve admission, standalone reserve facts, V9 facts, scores, or public API fields. The private V9 evidence-journal schema can carry the resolved value when journal writers are enabled.
- `source-invariant`: opt-in within-run result sharing for adapters whose returned payload is coin-invariant. All other adapters run per coin even when configs look similar. The shared-source cache key canonicalises adapter, version, semantics, inputs, and params and deliberately omits the coin id, so an adapter whose *result* depends on the coin must declare `none` even when its configs are byte-identical: Reservoir reads the SavingModule `redeemFee()` only for srUSD and wsrUSD, so its three coins each run the adapter and the shared upstream payload is de-duplicated by the URL-level request cache instead.

`single-asset` now supports optional `reserveProbe`, `supplyProbe`, and `timestampProbe` paths so weak single-bucket feeds can persist honest reserve/supply ratio telemetry when the upstream exposes it. The family remains `weak-live-probe` unless the source is strong enough to justify promotion into a more independent adapter class. `getJsonPath` now accepts numeric array indices (for example `chains[0].lastSyncedAt`), so QCAD (`qcad-stablecorp`) drives a `timestampProbe` from the Stablecorp balances API for verified freshness, and MXNe (`mxne-real-mxn`) binds the onchain-EVM `single-asset` probe as a CETES Stablebonds wrapper mirroring `cetes-etherfuse`.

`attestation-pdf-index` is a validated-static adapter for issuer pages that expose dated PDF attestations. It selects the newest dated attestation, audit, or report PDF link, including Webflow-style gated PDF attributes, ignores unrelated dated documents such as whitepapers, validates source freshness from the report date (or the exact URL-bound reviewed balance date), and emits configured static reserve slices until full PDF text extraction is implemented. ZARP (`zarp-zarp`) binds its Kempen AUP transparency index (September 1 report, August 31 tested balances) and emits the reviewed single ZAR-fiat-reserve slice. Ripio's four wFIAT coins (`wbrl-ripio`, `wcop-ripio`, `wmxn-ripio`, `wars-argentine-peso`) bind the shared Ripio local-stablecoins page, whose accountant-certification PDFs carry compact `YYYYMMDD` dates in their filenames (newest 2026-03-31) — the parser now accepts these as the lowest-precedence day format while rejecting 8-digit tokens that are not valid dates, and each coin emits its reviewed 100% local-currency bank-deposit slice. Each Ripio config also binds that exact per-token PDF URL to its reviewed March 31 balance date; a newer unreviewed report omits verified source freshness and degrades instead of letting a filename refresh the previous static composition. An optional `linkMatch` regex param (validated to compile) is matched against each dated link's href plus anchor text, so Ripio's four wFIAT coins can each pin their own currency token; when no link matches, the adapter omits `reportPdfUrl` and emits an info `attestation-pdf-index-link-unmatched` warning instead of citing a sibling currency's certificate.

Both bound publishers certify on slow, irregular cycles rather than a monthly cadence: consecutive Kempen ZARP AUP report dates from 2025-01-16 to 2026-08-12 are 41-176 days apart (median 87), and Ripio's shared index still serves 2026-03-31 as the newest certification for all four currencies (164 days stale on 2026-09-11, with no Q2 2026 document on the page, on the localized `wfiat` pages, or at the predictable HubSpot paths; each currency token resolves to its own PDF). The adapter therefore keeps its 33-day `MONTHLY_DISCLOSURE_SOURCE_MAX_AGE_SEC` cap rather than the legacy quarterly disclosure tier (115.7 days): that tier is itself exceeded by the observed gaps, so widening would mute ZARP's longer silences without clearing Ripio, and a readable but stale index publishes a `degraded` source-age snapshot instead of the binding being widened or suspended.

The independent-assurance family (`shared/lib/independent-assurance.ts` plus per-publisher adapters such as AUDX, EUROP, PAXG, and StraitsX XSGD/XUSD) is the score-grade successor for report-backed issuers: each reviewed report is compiled OFFLINE into a manifest entry (source URL, SHA-256 of the official PDF bytes, report date, examiner identity, asset/liability figures, composition slices) via `scripts/maintenance/refresh-independent-assurance-reports.ts`, and the Worker only verifies the exact official artifact at runtime — fetching the PDF bytes and failing closed on hash mismatch, URL/host drift, PDF magic/content-type/size drift, missing liabilities, inconsistent reported totals, or a newer unreviewed report appearing on the index. Observed reserve shortfalls and non-positive liability rows publish the slices with a structured reconciliation finding and a `reserve-undercollateralized` degraded warning; the ratio is omitted when total liabilities are non-positive. Freshness is the examined report date (`verified` mode) under per-publisher cadence age caps; a stale verified report degrades the sync and drops score eligibility rather than pretending recency. No PDF parsing happens in the Worker.

Assurance manifests register through `shared/data/live-reserves/independent-assurance/index.ts`; offline extraction profiles register independently through `scripts/lib/independent-assurance-profiles/registry.ts`. The schema derives `assuranceTier` from the reviewed `conclusion` and rejects contradictory explicit tiers: favorable examination/assurance conclusions are `independent-assurance`, while `agreed-upon-procedures` and `issuer-attested` conclusions retain those non-independent tiers. Hash-pinning does not promote examiner quality: these latter reports must use a sibling descriptor with `evidenceClass: static-validated` and `sourceOriginClass: issuer-attested`; the shared fetcher rejects other descriptors before fetching. Freshness remains `verified` only from the report's examined as-of instant, never its download time. Paxos products select reviewed `{pin, profile}` entries using `params.product` (default `PAXG`); unregistered products fail closed. Bare compiler `--check` checks all registered manifests against profile metadata, row definitions, and reconciliation without PDF re-extraction; `--product X --pdf /path/report.pdf --check` retains strict full recompilation, including Poppler provenance. See `agents/live-reserve-upgrade/ASSURANCE-HOWTO.md` for the addition sequence.

Every independent-assurance profile supplies an explicit report-date parser. Runtime discovery binds that date to the manifest's exact URL, SHA-256, and byte length; an undated candidate or a newer unreviewed report fails closed. Safety Score V9 treats `proofOfReserves.latestReport` as current only through 100 days from the conservative UTC start of `periodEnd`, then records the assurance component as stale with report-specific evidence. Operational publication windows and the next dated boundaries are maintained in [Dated source age caps](./runbooks/dated-source-age-caps.md).

`flying-tulip-ftusd` consumes Flying Tulip's timestamped ftUSD dashboard API, requires the reviewed Ethereum and Sonic chain set and exact USDC/USDT/USSD collateral addresses, aggregates collateral by underlying, and retains borrow/stake leverage and health-factor diagnostics. The API is a first-party index of publicly verifiable on-chain reserve state. The reviewed chain set is now Ethereum, Sonic, and Binance Smart Chain (whose reviewed collaterals add FDUSD, `coinId: fdusd-first-digital`); a chain that ships with zero TVL and zero supply is treated as an inactive launch placeholder, every expected chain must be present, and an active chain outside the reviewed set surfaces as a `degraded` `unexpected-chain` warning rather than a sync error so an issuer deployment on a new chain never flips ftUSD to `error`. The adapter remains deliberately `weak-live-probe` with `issuer-attested` origin because Pharos currently consumes that index instead of deriving the complete wrapper inventory and values through direct RPC reads; the classification describes the ingestion path, not off-chain custody or an unobservable reserve balance.

`attestation-pdf-index` and the shared independent-assurance index fetch normally request issuer index pages with browser-style `Origin`, `Referer`, and `Accept-Language` headers, falling back to the neutral Pharos identity when that fails, because some WordPress and hosting stacks rate-limit generic Worker HTML requests while still serving normal browser navigations. Two hosts invert or replace that order. `attestation-pdf-index` tries the neutral headers first for `schuman.io`; Fidelity's index is served only to `Pharos/1.0 (+https://pharos.watch)`, which the FIDD profile declares as its own index header because that host rejects both the shared browser-shaped and the shared neutral identities. Schuman's rendered reserve-attestations page no longer exposes report anchors, so EUROP discovery moved to `europ-independent-assurance` and reads the issuer's public WordPress media JSON for `SALVUS_Attestation` attachments, still applying exact URL, date, uniqueness, and newer-report gates.

USDv uses the dedicated `solomon-protocol` adapter against Solomon Labs' public protocol-data JSON API (`data.solomonlabs.io/api/solomon-protocol/protocol-data`) rather than a scraped attestation index.

`dgld-gold-mapper` consumes Gold Token SA's live bar registry (`production-gold-mapper-backend.onrender.com/v1`), summing the live PAMP-bar fine-oz weights (`/bars`) against the per-network reconciled token supply (`/networks`, `reconState: matched`, zero `gap`) to publish the reserve-oz-per-token ratio with `verified` freshness from `reconCheckedAt`. The registry is issuer-maintained with no third-party audit, so the adapter stays `weak-live-probe` with `issuer-attested` origin and an isolated breaker scoped to the Render host.

`matrixdock-frs` reads Matrixdock XAGm's FallbackReserveFeed (`latestRoundData()`) and MTokenMain (`totalSupply()`, `ozPerToken()`) on Ethereum plus the Sui `coinMetadata` supply, pinning the feed answer unit against `ozPerToken` (the issuer maintains `totalSupply == totalReserves / ozPerToken`) to emit silver reserve oz and the oz-per-token coverage ratio. The feed is issuer-transmitted (not a Chainlink proxy) and updates only on reserve changes, so the adapter is `weak-live-probe` with `issuer-attested` origin and `not-applicable` freshness anchored to the pinned Ethereum block and Sui checkpoint. Because `setReserve()` is the feed's only writer, a round advances only when the issuer actually changes the reserve, so the frozen-round budget follows the contract's own `ReserveSet` history rather than a heartbeat: the 2026-09-24 review measured inter-round gaps of 7.0, 7.1, 25.7, 36.0, 15.3 and 52.7 days across rounds 1-7 (2026-03-13 to 2026-08-04) and replaced the 4,000,000-second (46.3-day) budget, which had degraded a healthy feed for ~6 days during the 6/12 to 8/4 cycle, with `MATRIXDOCK_BULLION_RESERVE_FEED_MAX_AGE_SEC` (60 days = worst observed interval plus issuer operating lag). A stale round still publishes and is reconciled by the unit pin against live supply; only a round older than that budget degrades.

`icp-gldt` measures Gold DAO GLDT's backing directly on the Internet Computer: it queries the pinned swap canister's `get_swap_configs` to enumerate the ORIGYN ICRC-7 NFT canisters and their per-NFT `division` (100 GLDT per gram, i.e. 10^10 base units), reads each canister's `icrc7_balance_of` for the swap canister's default account, and divides the summed locked-NFT grams by the ICRC-1 GLDT ledger supply. Every config must fractionalize against the pinned ledger, and `Custom` per-token divisions fail closed. The slice is a single allocated-gold bucket with the `chainlink-por` `XAU_G` gram convention, `onchain-observation` origin, and `not-applicable` freshness carrying the certified state time in `details`.

### Fallback Inputs

`inputs.fallbacks` is implemented in `runAdapter()` inside `worker/src/cron/sync-live-reserves.ts`.

- The cron tries `inputs.primary` first.
- If the adapter throws, the orchestrator retries the same adapter against each fallback by temporarily swapping that fallback into `inputs.primary`.
- If every fallback fails, the attempt history records the primary and fallback failure chain, and the cron-run metadata includes per-coin `attemptFailureSummaries` for automated triage.

USDD currently uses this path: the cron retries `latest-collateral` on Ethereum if the Tron source fails, and the adapter now derives the matching `collateral-history` URL from whichever chain endpoint is active so fallback freshness stays chain-consistent. FDUSD retries the issuer's official Webflow origin when the canonical transparency host rejects Worker traffic, and the three Reservoir assets pin the protocol application's un-geofenced Vercel deployments (`fireworks-git-master-fortunafi.vercel.app`, then `fireworks-fortunafi.vercel.app`) as primary and fallback because the canonical `app.reservoir.xyz` zone answers Worker egress with its country gate. These transport fallbacks do not change the displayed source identity or relax snapshot freshness. USDT held in USDD PSM vaults is emitted as proportional `collateral` exposure; the `mechanism` relationship type is reserved for serial dependencies rather than fractional reserve inventory.

Supported input kinds:

| Kind             | Meaning                                                              |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `http-json`      | JSON API endpoint                                                    |
| `http-html`      | HTML page parsed by the adapter                                      |
| `indexer`        | Indexed external data feed                                           |
| `onchain-solana` | On-chain Solana mint-supply reads via keyed RPCs when configured, then public mainnet RPCs |
| `onchain-evm`    | On-chain EVM reads via `etherscan-proxy`, `alchemy`, or `public-rpc` |

The shared live-reserve config schema enforces adapter-specific primary and fallback input kinds. For example, `curated-validated` can use `onchain-evm` or `onchain-solana`, `single-asset` can use `http-json` or `onchain-evm`, and HTML scrapers such as `circle-transparency` cannot accidentally be configured with an on-chain input that would pass metadata validation but fail during cron execution.

`curated-validated` can now use `onchain-solana` when a tracked coin publishes its canonical Solana mint in `coin.contracts`, allowing the adapter to validate non-zero supply without downgrading the reserve mix to a weak single-bucket feed.

MAI, lisUSD, and GGBR have no active live-reserve configuration after the September 2026 source review. MAI's supply probe did not validate a complete reserve composition; lisUSD's five-branch configuration omitted positive collateral, and the September 2026 re-census of all 22 documented GemJoins still leaves material positive branches without a supported price path — the BNB ilk now holds Ceros `ceaBNBc` receipts (100% of that token's supply, no DefiLlama price and no underlying rate exposed on-chain) and sUSDX's only DefiLlama price is a broken pool print — so it stays unbound rather than valuing a ~$110M branch at a proxy unit price; GGBR's ION.au configuration did not establish the current issuer reserve mapping. Their reviewed static data remains available, but `/api/stablecoin-reserves/:id` returns 404 without reading an old cached snapshot. Reactivation requires a supported complete valuation path, not omission of unpriceable branches or a proxy unit price for a non-unit-rate receipt. THBILL retains its configured proof-style source with a label naming both Wellington ULTRA and Fidelity International FILQ; their allocation is undisclosed.

`onchain-solana` now tries configured keyed RPCs first — Alchemy, then dRPC when each credential is available — followed by any explicit adapter RPCs and the three public RPCs: `https://api.mainnet-beta.solana.com`, `https://api.mainnet.solana.com`, and `https://solana-rpc.publicnode.com`. Duplicate URLs are removed, and keyed provider URLs are redacted from terminal errors. This reduces false reserve incidents caused by single-endpoint Solana RPC reachability failures inside the Worker runtime.

`onchain-evm` reads build their endpoint list per call in `worker/src/lib/evm-rpc.ts`: the chain's registry endpoints first, then adapter-pinned `extraRpcUrls`, then the supplemental Dwellir endpoint, and only when the scheduled runtime enabled it (key present, credit ledger usable, `dwellir-evm` circuit closed). Dwellir is appended strictly last, never replaces a reviewed pin, and is skipped for numeric historical block tags on endpoints whose state history is near-head-only; a request-path build never enables it. Readers with their own fetch loops keep their existing endpoint sets: the Solana account census in `worker/src/cron/reserve-adapters/solana.ts` uses registry endpoints plus its own public Solana URLs, and the Movement fungible-asset supply read in `worker/src/cron/reserve-adapters/token-supply.ts` uses the Movement REST API.

`hylo-solana` binds hyUSD to the Hylo exchange v2.0.5 account graph: discover the registry LUT, then re-read it with all SPL vaults, mints, and pool states in one bounded finalized `getMultipleAccounts` census. `minContextSlot` is only a lower bound, not a historical-slot selector; a changed registry fails closed, and the census's returned slot plus `getBlockTime` is the observation. Pinned program owners, Anchor discriminators, mint/vault links, and exogenous Pyth identities are verified without runtime PDA derivation. Per-LST DefiLlama prices roll up into one LST slice alongside cbBTC, HYPE, and USDC pools; sub-precision USDC dust is disclosed in details. Unknown LSTs, newly activated reviewed-inactive exogenous candidates, or liability reconciliation failure reject the attempt. Paused routes and collateral shortfalls retain measured composition with degraded warnings. The feed is independent on-chain dynamic-mix evidence with not-applicable freshness; exogenous completeness is bounded to the six SDK candidates reviewed on 2026-09-09.

Branch-balance adapters (`evm-branch-balances` and `liquity-v2-branches`) can set a per-branch `priceToken` when the measured collateral balance is a protocol receipt token whose live price should be sourced from a separate underlying token address. The balance still comes from the configured branch token; only the DefiLlama price lookup address changes. Branch-balance configs can also attach reviewed `sourceUrls` so redemption telemetry emitted from same-run on-chain reads carries source provenance into the Redemption Backstop API. Successful adapters can attach structured snapshot metadata that is stored on the authoritative snapshot row (`reserve_composition.metadata`), not on the mutable latest-attempt state.

**Reserve methodology — `collateralizationRatio` basis.** The field is a measured assets-backing-liabilities ratio, and only two bases may populate it: `gross` (reserve total ÷ supply, both sides from the same feed snapshot and recomputable from the published `totalReserveUsd`/`supplyUsd`) and `net-of-protocol-owned` (the same quotient after removing the issuer's own inventory and protocol-owned liquidity from both sides, which the feed's own scalars must reconcile). Every other basis publishes the issuer's number as `reportedCollateralizationRatio` and no canonical ratio at all, and the coverage/undercollateralization warnings are built only from a canonical ratio: an `unreconciled` headline (matches no derivable denominator) keeps its degraded `collateralization-unreconciled` warning instead of a coverage claim, and an `underived` one (the feed publishes no `total_supply`) publishes no coverage warning because nothing reproducible backs it. Adapters whose published totals are not assets-backing-liabilities — Avant's gross longs over gross financing debt is the reviewed example — publish those numbers under explicit detail keys (`details.grossLongUsd`, `details.grossFinancingDebtUsd`, `details.grossLeverageCoverageRatio`) and no ratio field.

`details.freshnessSource = "same-run-render-clock"` marks a verified snapshot whose timestamp is the source's own render or response clock — the instant the payload was rendered or served — rather than an independently dated as-of time for the reserve state (Mento's dashboard SSR fetch time, Flying Tulip's `lastUpdated`, DOLA FiRM's response timestamp). Such clocks are accepted as `verified` per the settled freshness policy, and the basis is named explicitly so consumers can distinguish a render clock from a dated disclosure.

Redemption telemetry is validated before persistence. Negative capacity or constraint values, capacity ratios outside `0..1`, negative fees, malformed redemption source URLs, invalid redemption freshness/capacity/holder-eligibility enum values, invalid `routeStatusReviewedAt` dates, capacity emitted by adapters that do not declare redemption-capacity support, fee telemetry emitted by adapters that do not declare fee support, and direct/proxy/queue capacity evidence emitted by an incompatible adapter capability fail the adapter output validation. Queue capacity that omits queue depth, settlement delay, or daily-limit semantics is stored only with a degraded warning. Legacy flat capacity/fee fields (`immediateRedeemableUsd`, `immediateRedeemableRatio`, `redemptionFeeBps`) are no longer written: the store-row decoder still reads them from historical rows (30-day retention) and maps them into the nested `redemption` shape at decode time, but every other consumer and all producers use the nested contract.

Same-run adapters populate `routeStatusSource: "onchain"` or `"protocol-api"` only when that run evaluates a route-specific executable predicate such as a pause, halt, shutdown, redeemability, cap, collateral-health, or allowlist gate. Capacity, balances, collateral, TVL, backing, and static configuration are not route-openness evidence by themselves; those paths publish `unknown` / `static-config` or omit the source. The shared metadata builder requires an explicit non-persisted `routeObserved: true` proof before it preserves live route-source attribution.

An unreadable route predicate is absence of evidence, never an optimistic verdict. A failed or malformed required probe publishes `unknown` without live route-source attribution; a readable open predicate supports `open`, while an observed closure preserves `paused` or `degraded` with its on-chain source. Balance-only paths including wrapped M0, collateral-position, Superstate, f(x), OpenEden, and Astherus Earn do not claim live openness. Falcon exposes redemption timing but no unsupported route status. HoneyFactory status uses the same-run pause and cap guards, Sky LitePSM uses the halt-fee sentinels, and Reserve DTF requires both a sound fully collateralized basket and positive throttle capacity.

`freshnessMode = "not-applicable"` is the expected scoring-eligible path for intrinsically current reads such as `evm-branch-balances`, `liquity-v1`, `liquity-v2-branches`, `liquity-native-active-pool`, `m0-wrapper-underlying`, `escrow-balance`, `btcfi`, `xdai-bridge`, `hive-hbd-protocol`, `usdai-hub`, and `collateral-positions-api`, where reserve composition comes from latest-state contract/API state rather than a separately timestamped disclosure. Timestamp-less APIs that expose balance-sheet snapshots without a reviewed latest-state contract remain `freshnessMode = "unverified"` and are detail-visible but not score-grade collateral. `reservoir` remains in this unverified bucket because its balance-sheet payload carries no independently verifiable source timestamp or reviewed latest-state proof, and its validation contract declares `allowedFreshnessModes: ["unverified"]` (decision 2026-09-24) so that the undeclinable miss on its declared 3-day dashboard budget is reported as the informational `freshness-unverified` warning rather than as `stale-source-undeterminable`, which no upstream behaviour could ever clear. The snapshot still cannot enter collateral scoring. Its broader stable-bucket liquidity is retained as diagnostic context; specific strategy labels such as Agua take precedence over their USDC denomination. Normalized redemption capacity is bound exclusively to same-run reads of the pinned USDC PSM's underlying identity, balance and pause state, not to the balance-sheet USDC bucket.

Mento reserve sync combines its analytics API with the dashboard's embedded render clock and per-stablecoin `cdp_backings` totals. The existing USDm/other-CDP paths use the dashboard render clock when available and otherwise publish unverified freshness. GBPm additionally requires pinned whole-system ActivePool/DefaultPool accounting and uses the earlier block/dashboard clock. CDP coherence compares matching collateral/debt scopes: GBPm whole-system pools or the other currencies' API troves against dashboard totals. Readable divergence above 1% publishes degraded evidence; malformed inputs or failed required pool observations fail closed.

InfiniFi reserve sync hydrates freshness the same way: the protocol `/data` payload carries no timestamp, so the `infinifi` adapter probes the transparency dashboard's siUSD rate-history series (written by the same backend snapshotter on a 2-hour cadence) and emits verified freshness from the latest point only when that point's rate matches the live staked exchange rate in the reserve payload. A failed probe or a cross-check mismatch falls back to explicit unverified freshness. The registry caps that probe at 21,600 seconds (6 hours, three missed 2-hour snapshotter writes) instead of the generic three-day dashboard cap, and the rate cross-check tolerance tightens from 5e-4 to 6e-5 — the ±5e-5 rounding envelope plus a sliver of sub-cadence drift — so multi-day accumulator drift now fails the probe into unverified freshness.

The registry now also declares the admissible `freshnessMode` set per adapter family so timestamp-backed disclosures, latest-state on-chain proofs, and explicitly unverified dashboard/API feeds cannot silently drift into undocumented freshness semantics.

Observed bad states (E4/P3), including reserve shortfalls, genuinely reported non-positive liabilities, and paused routes, publish available slices and ratios with `degraded` warnings rather than adapter errors; only unreadable, malformed, or unverifiable evidence fails closed, and undefined ratios are omitted rather than fabricated.

Warnings now carry both a display `severity` and an execution `effect`:

| Effect     | Meaning                                                                                                                          |
| ---------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `info`     | Informational only; the snapshot can still be stored and remain `ok`                                                             |
| `degraded` | Snapshot is stored, but the per-coin sync state becomes `degraded` and that snapshot is excluded from independent scoring passthrough (judged on the snapshot's own warnings via `selectScoringDegradedWarnings`, so a later failed attempt never demotes a clean prior snapshot) |
| `fatal`    | Snapshot is rejected and the attempt is recorded as an `error`                                                                   |

## Cron Behavior

`runFourHourlyReserveSyncSlot()` in `worker/src/handlers/scheduled/hourly-live-reserves.ts` runs the reserve cron on its own 4-hourly trigger so reserve-adapter fetches do not compete with the 30-minute scoring lane or the daily 08:00 jobs.

`syncLiveReserves()`:

1. Filters `ACTIVE_STABLECOINS` to the coins that declare `liveReservesConfig`.
2. Orders the queue by adapter evidence class (`independent`, then `static-validated`, then `weak-live-probe`) and packs coins sharing a `source-invariant` adapter contiguously, so run-budget truncation defers weak probes before score-grade feeds and shared fetches are reused immediately. The durable scheduler checkpoint stores the one next-item resume pointer; a resumed run processes only that deferred suffix and does not wrap back to the queue head.
3. Resolves an adapter from `worker/src/cron/reserve-adapters/index.ts`.
4. Builds a breaker key as `live-reserves:${breakerScope ?? adapter}`.
5. Checks the per-source circuit breaker before each coin fetch.
   Each primary or fallback adapter attempt receives its own start time as `nowSec`, so time spent earlier in the queue cannot make current chain heads appear to be in the future.
6. Persists either a fresh snapshot (`reserve_composition`) plus sync-state row, or an error/degraded/skipped sync-state row.

The `reserve-recovery` lane retains its five-minute trigger. In `recover` mode it fences abandoned in-flight attempts, claims a compatible ready pointer, and replays the suffix after active leases permit takeover. Before scanning, it retires up to 25 incompatible historical checkpoints (`running`, `recovering`, `ready`, or `platform_abandoned`) per tick only when a newer current-hash checkpoint is `completed`, has exhausted the full configured queue, and its slot is `finished`. The aggregate slot result need not be `ok`: a degraded sidecar does not invalidate the completed reserve cohort. Retirement atomically marks the exact checkpoint `failed` with `checkpoint-superseded-by-newer-full-cohort`, records supersession, and fences only its matching pending domain attempt. Unexpired reserve-child or recovery leases and fresh slot heartbeats prevent retirement; leases themselves are never deleted. Remaining incompatible debt with no ready or eligible checkpoint produces degraded status and `statusCause = "reserve-recovery-zero-eligible-incompatible"` rather than green idle status. Metadata reports `retiredCheckpoints` and post-retirement eligibility. `off` keeps the sweep but skips recovery scans. The checkpoint pointer is the sole run-level resume mechanism; the cache table is not used for reserve cursor ownership.
**Adapter output validation:** After each adapter returns, `validateAdapterOutput()` checks
that all slice `risk` values are valid enum members, all `pct` values are finite and strictly positive,
the slice list is non-empty, and the sum is within 2 points of 100%. Invalid output is treated as
an error. Sum deviation above 0.5 points produces a `degraded` warning, while deviation above 2 points
hard-fails the adapter output with a `fatal` warning.

Source freshness validation also rejects source timestamps that are more than 10 minutes in the future, including nested redemption telemetry timestamps and the newest contributing timestamp (`newestSourceTimestamp`) in Re Metrics, so upstream clock mistakes or millisecond/second parsing errors cannot be treated as fresh snapshots. Multi-row reserve feeds should use the oldest material contributor timestamp as `sourceTimestamp` for verified freshness and can persist `latest*Timestamp` / `sourceTimestampSpreadSec` metadata for diagnostics.

Adapters can also declare shared validation policy in the registry:

- `maxSourceAgeSec`: if adapter metadata includes `sourceTimestamp` and the upstream disclosure is older than the policy allows, the sync is marked `degraded`
- `maxUnknownExposurePct`: if adapter metadata includes `unknownExposurePct` and unmapped reserve exposure is material, the sync is marked `degraded`
- `allowedFreshnessModes`: explicit per-adapter freshness contract enforced by output validation
- when `maxSourceAgeSec` exists but the adapter can only attest freshness heuristically, `freshnessMode = "unverified"` produces an informational `freshness-unverified` warning instead of forcing a degraded status on the sync itself; the snapshot still cannot enter collateral scoring
- late monthly disclosures that routinely publish after the next calendar month starts use `LATE_MONTHLY_DISCLOSURE_SOURCE_MAX_AGE_SEC = 4000000` rather than ad hoc per-coin caps
- month-end disclosures whose successor is published weeks into the following month use `NEXT_MONTH_DISCLOSURE_SOURCE_MAX_AGE_SEC = 70 days`, calibrated to the measured period-end-to-publication lag of each publisher rather than to the month boundary
- timestamp-less dashboard/API feeds should also populate `details.freshnessSource` / `details.freshnessReason` so the lack of verified recency is explicit in stored metadata
- `preferredFreshnessMode` records the honest target (`verified` or `not-applicable`) separately from tolerated `unverified` fallback. Adapters with no usable upstream reserve timestamp instead declare `freshnessLimitation`; they are not missing-probe promotion candidates. The coverage audit separates latest-known tolerated-not-preferred observations ("probe not built") from these upstream limitations. Supply `--reserve-states <file>` with an array (or `{reserves: [...]}`) of `/api/stablecoin-reserves/<id>` responses; without those snapshots it reports observations as unavailable, not inferred from configuration.

Source-age tiers are publisher-wide admission ceilings, not fetch intervals:

| Tier / policy constant | Maximum age | Reviewed use |
|---|---:|---|
| `DASHBOARD_SOURCE_MAX_AGE_SEC` | 3 days | Timestamped reserve dashboards; faster publishers may declare a tighter bound |
| `WEEKLY_SOURCE_MAX_AGE_SEC` (same duration as `DISCLOSURE_SOURCE_MAX_AGE_SEC`) | 7 days | Circle's weekly reserve chart, not its monthly assurance report |

Per-coin `scoring.maxSourceAgeSec` may only tighten a declared adapter cap; the shared config schema rejects widening. Omit redundant equal values and inherit the publisher policy. Where an adapter has no age cap, a coin may supply one (for example, reviewed Chainlink NAV windows); latest-state `not-applicable` freshness still bypasses source-age checks. The September 2026 review removed obsolete 4,000,000-second dashboard overrides and duplicated assurance caps rather than widening publishers to clear alerts. EUROP now expires after 100 days instead of its former 10,000,000-second allowance, so an overdue report can lose score-grade eligibility until a newer examined period is published.

For on-chain branch-balance adapters, branches marked with `depType: "wrapper"` are not treated as plain USD-pegged wrappers for the depeg guard. This avoids false degraded states for yield-bearing wrappers such as sUSDe whose market price can intentionally rise above one dollar while the reserve slice still links dependency risk to the underlying asset.

Unknown exposure now follows one repo-wide policy:

- quantify it and persist `unknownExposurePct` when the adapter can do so honestly
- emit an explicit unknown slice when the reserve mix would otherwise hide that exposure
- use threshold-driven warning effects so immaterial unknowns stay informational and material unknowns degrade the sync
- fail closed when the adapter cannot quantify the missing exposure without inventing precision

These policy warnings preserve last-known live detail/status data but exclude the affected snapshot from V9 Backing/dependency compilation. Admission judges the snapshot's own warnings, not the latest attempt's status.

**Circuit breaker recording:** Breaker outcomes are deferred until the entire sync loop
completes, recording the worst outcome per breaker key (failure trumps success). This
prevents first-coin-wins bias where a successful early coin would mask failures of later
coins sharing the same breaker key. Finalization records failure outcomes first and
skips success heartbeats for breakers that are already closed with no failure debt;
those closed-success skips avoid rewriting hundreds of live-reserve circuit rows at
the end of every healthy run. Only upstream HTTP/network failures contribute negative source-health outcomes. Configuration, output validation, corroboration disagreement, schema-shape, D1/storage, and run-budget failures are neutral: they remain coin failures without opening a shared source breaker. Failure metadata distinguishes `storage-write` (including `D1_ERROR`), `corroboration-mismatch`, `schema-shape`, and `run-budget-exhausted`.

Cron result statuses:

| Status     | When                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `ok`       | At least one configured coin synced, no coin was budget-deferred (`deferredCoins === 0`), and `failed + skipped <= ceil(total * 0.1)`                                                                                 |
| `degraded` | At least one configured coin synced and either `deferredCoins > 0` or `failed + skipped > ceil(total * 0.1)`; OR no coin synced but every skip was a circuit-breaker hold (no real failures, no budget-deferred tail) |
| `error`    | No configured coin synced and at least one coin truly failed or was budget-deferred (`failed > 0` or `deferredSkipped > 0`)                                                                                           |

Per-coin warnings still matter operationally, but they affect `reserve_sync_state.last_status` for that coin (`degraded`) and the cron metadata warning list, not the run-level `CronResult.status`.

JSON parse failures include the HTTP status, content type, and final response URL when a redirect changed it, with provider credentials redacted. This distinguishes an issuer API schema change from an upstream redirect to a marketing or challenge page. A locally successful request does not prove that Worker egress reaches the same response; inspect the next production attempt before changing the configured source.

The cron loop remains sequential with at most two adapter HTTP/RPC operations in flight, preserving the six-connection trigger budget. The leased wrapper grants 12 minutes; the internal run budget is 9 minutes. Admission reserves one 20-second attempt, 30-second D1 finalization window, and 5-second margin. Every admitted coin shares the absolute run deadline minus that margin across checkpoint, begin, breaker read, fallback chain, and terminal writes. Primary and fallback attempts each receive at most 20 seconds and cannot extend the shared adapter-chain deadline (which reserves finalization time). Checkpoint/begin/failure writes are bounded by the lifecycle deadline; SQL deadline/ownership fences prevent late D1 work from gaining authority. A timed-out or ownership-rejected begin stops the run with its checkpoint retained, never advances an ambiguous pointer. Success still uses authoritative readback on ambiguous acknowledgement.

Run metadata exports in-memory `phaseTimingsMs.stages` for checkpoint starts, breaker reads, begin writes, failure writes, authoritative writes, and progress reporting alongside aggregate setup/queue/adapter/D1/finalization timings. Adapter telemetry uses `ioActivityBurstCount` for zero-active-to-active I/O transitions, not concurrency “waves”. Status snapshot compaction preserves the producer-bounded latency groups (128 groups / 36 KiB) and histogram samples rather than silently truncating them to 40; producer overflow remains explicitly counted. Per-attempt `durationMs` lives under composition `metadata.diag`, excluded from the payload digest and public responses.

The cron reports `setup`, `syncing`, and `finalizing` progress. Budget exhaustion defers untouched tail rows as skipped with `failureCategory = "run-budget-exhausted"`; these are capacity pressure, not source failures, and are excluded from persistent-stale independent alerts. Deferred rows clear attempt/pending IDs and checkpoint ownership retains the resume pointer. Metadata exports `runBudgetTruncated`, `deferredCoins`, `nextCursorStablecoinId`, and resolved budgets. Exhausted finalization-tail budgets skip remaining breaker writes, artifact cleanup, and history pruning explicitly. Success composition/state and both authoritative-gated histories now commit atomically; there is no post-success history-gap warning or resume-repair hook. Source-invariant adapters alone reuse complete config-keyed source results. Cleanup removes sync-state and breaker artifacts for removed live sources.

Artifact cleanup remains best-effort, but the cron metadata now records `artifactCleanup` delete counts, `artifactCleanupWarningCount`, and structured `artifactCleanupWarnings` when cleanup fails. `/status` and the admin status dashboard summarize non-zero cleanup deletes and cleanup warning counts so ghost-artifact cleanup regressions do not require log scraping.

## Storage Model

### `reserve_composition`

Latest successful live snapshot per live-enabled coin.

| Column                                            | Meaning                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `stablecoin_id`                                   | Canonical Pharos coin ID                                                                              |
| `slices`                                          | JSON-serialized `ReserveSlice[]`                                                                      |
| `fetched_at`                                      | Unix timestamp of the successful snapshot                                                             |
| `source`                                          | Adapter key that produced the snapshot                                                                |
| `metadata`                                        | Snapshot-scoped adapter telemetry (freshness, redeemable capacity, live fee, disclosure totals, etc.) |
| `warning_count` / `warnings`                      | Warning summary persisted alongside the successful snapshot                                           |
| `adapter_source_model` / `adapter_evidence_class` | Registry-derived classification copied onto the snapshot row for authoritative reads                  |
| `attempt_id`                                      | Attempt-fencing identifier for the successful snapshot when the writer stamped one                    |
| `config_fingerprint`                              | SHA-256 of canonical adapter/version/semantics/inputs/params; missing legacy values are temporarily accepted with a diagnostic until the next successful write |
### `reserve_composition_history`

Append-only index of successful live snapshots. Each row carries the attempt ID, adapter classification, and a nullable `payload_sha256` digest of the snapshot payload excluding `metadata.diag`; `slices`, `metadata`, and `warnings` are intentionally empty because `reserve_sync_attempt_history` is the append-only source for attempt payload metadata. Non-null attempt IDs are unique per coin and inserts are idempotent. Both histories commit in the same D1 batch as the composition and success state, gated on the resulting authoritative attempt. A history failure rolls back the entire success; ambiguous acknowledgements use authoritative readback. The 4-hourly cron prunes history older than 30 days while preserving frozen coins and referenced attempts.

### `reserve_sync_state`

Per-coin operational state for the most recent attempt.

| Column                                                               | Meaning                                                                                                                                   |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `stablecoin_id`                                                      | Canonical Pharos coin ID                                                                                                                  |
| `adapter_key`                                                        | Adapter used for the most recent attempt                                                                                                  |
| `breaker_key`                                                        | Per-source circuit-breaker key                                                                                                            |
| `last_attempted_at`                                                  | Unix timestamp of the latest attempt                                                                                                      |
| `last_success_at`                                                    | Unix timestamp of the latest successful live snapshot                                                                                     |
| `last_status`                                                        | `ok`, `degraded`, `error`, or `skipped`                                                                                                   |
| `warning_count`                                                      | Number of adapter warnings                                                                                                                |
| `warnings`                                                           | JSON-serialized warning objects                                                                                                           |
| `last_error`                                                         | Latest failure message, if any                                                                                                            |
| `metadata`                                                           | Attempt-scoped operational metadata only (for example skip/failure reasons or warning-effect counts), not authoritative reserve telemetry |
| `last_attempt_id` / `pending_attempt_id` / `last_success_attempt_id` | Attempt-fencing identifiers used to reject orphaned or partially written live snapshots                                                   |
| `config_fingerprint`                                                | Canonical configuration fingerprint for the latest attempt; written at begin and finalization |

### `reserve_sync_attempt_history`

Append-only source of all reserve-sync attempt payload metadata, including `ok`, `degraded`, `error`, and `skipped` outcomes with warnings, error message, and attempt-scoped metadata. Non-null attempt IDs are unique per coin and inserts are idempotent. The composition-history digest is computed from the successful snapshot payload while this table remains the durable source for warning/metadata payloads. The 4-hourly reserve cron prunes rows older than 30 days with the same frozen-coin and referenced-attempt preservation as the composition history.

Scheduling deferrals use an explicit history mode: null-attempt `skipped` rows record the deferred outcome even when the state upsert deliberately preserves an existing healthy snapshot. They do not claim successful snapshot authority; actual attempt finalizations remain gated on their completed attempt identity and state.

Scheduled healthy persistence uses six SQL statements in three round trips: checkpoint start, SQL ownership-gated domain begin, and the four-statement authority/history batch. Begin checks the exact checkpoint identity and current coin/attempt pointer. It is never folded into its own success batch. Stage B checkpoint/start batching remains deferred until exact-owner gating and crash/ambiguous-acknowledgement recovery are proven. Failure finalization and its guarded attempt history use one atomic batch. Apply migration `0239_live_reserve_config_fingerprint.sql` before Worker activation; Worker rollback leaves its nullable additive columns intact.

Freshness and consistency rules now live across the `worker/src/lib/live-reserves/store*.ts` helper family, with `worker/src/lib/live-reserves/store.ts` kept as the public facade:

- `LIVE_RESERVE_FRESHNESS_SEC = 172800` (48 hours)
- A live snapshot only counts as consistent when `reserve_sync_state.last_success_at === reserve_composition.fetched_at` and, when attempt IDs are stamped, `reserve_sync_state.last_success_attempt_id === reserve_composition.attempt_id`
- Stored snapshots are parsed strictly: unreadable JSON, invalid payloads, empty slice arrays, invalid slices, or materially invalid sums are rejected instead of being partially served
- `resolveReserveResult()` is the canonical detail/API resolver used by `GET /api/stablecoin-reserves/:id`
- `computeReserveCompositionOverview()` is the status-summary resolver used by `/api/status` and `/admin/`
- `loadFreshIndependentLiveReserveMap()`, API `provenance.scoringEligible`, and overview `independentFreshEligible` reuse `evaluateLiveReserveAdmission()` with one evaluation clock. A retained clean snapshot remains eligible after a later failed attempt; operational error/degraded counts remain separate.
- transient network/upstream failures retain the last successful composition for reserve detail/status views while the current sync state records `error` / `skipped`; that composition keeps scoring until the freshness bound retires it, because the failed attempt wrote no snapshot and says nothing about the one on disk (before 2026-09-07 a single failed attempt dropped the coin onto the audited fallback, and one Tether `502` fanned out into ~580 inherited open data points)
- `getLatestSuccessfulReserveSnapshotMetadata()` is the canonical accessor for downstream consumers that need snapshot telemetry such as redeemable capacity or live redemption fees
- failed `reserve_sync_state` / `reserve_sync_attempt_history` rows now also retain `metadata.failureCategory` so parser drift, network issues, upstream HTTP failures, validation failures, and storage write failures are distinguishable without log grep
- authoritative `live` / `live-stale` API responses now also carry a `provenance` envelope plus a separate `displayBadge` so the frontend can distinguish true live feeds from curated-validated and proof-style reserve views

### Snapshot admission gates

The shared result is `{ eligible, reasons: AdmissionRejectionCode[] }`. All gates must pass for independent reserve scoring:

| Gate | Rejection code | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current configuration | `unconfigured`, `suspended` | The coin must have an unsuspended live configuration. |
| Snapshot authority and integrity | `missing-snapshot`, `inconsistent-snapshot` | Strictly decoded slices and matching positive success/fetch timestamps; matching nonempty attempt IDs when either is stamped. |
| Semantic configuration identity | `config-mismatch` | Stored fingerprint equals SHA-256 of canonical `{adapter, version, semantics, inputs, params}` (`params` defaults to `{}`). Display/scoring settings do not change the digest. Missing legacy fingerprints are accepted with a diagnostic until the next successful write stamps one. A mismatch is not served as current live detail or redemption metadata. |
| Evidence class | `non-independent` | Only `independent` reserve evidence qualifies. |
| Fetch and source age | `stale` | Fetch age is at most 48 hours; verified source age uses the tighter coin/adapter cap, falling back to 48 hours. |
| Explicit freshness | `invalid-freshness` | Require `verified` plus a finite positive timestamp no more than ten minutes ahead, or explicit `not-applicable`. Absent/malformed modes or timestamps cannot become verified through decoding. |
| Snapshot warnings | `degraded-snapshot` | Apply the current per-coin warning policy to the retained snapshot, not the newest attempt. |
| Slice count | `insufficient-slices` | At least the requested count (one by default). |

This fixes the HBD 164-versus-165 discrepancy: its later node-disagreement error remains an operational error, while its clean retained snapshot contributes to eligible coverage in all three consumers. Redemption capacity consumes the same structural, configuration-identity, and freshness rejections but retains its separate evidence-class and degraded-telemetry policy; independent reserve composition is not a prerequisite for valid same-run redemption telemetry.

`computeReserveCompositionOverview()` aggregates the status-card summary used by `/status`:

- `configuredCoins`
- `freshCoins`
- `staleCoins`
- `missingCoins`
- `degradedCoins`
- `errorCoins`
- `corruptCoins`
- `independentFreshEligible`
- `independentFreshUnverified`
- `staticValidatedFresh`
- `weakProbeFresh`
- `persistentlyStaleIndependentCoins` (independent feeds older than the persistent-staleness window that can escalate status beyond normal short-lived lag; includes old active failures and circuit-open skips, but not run-budget deferred rows)
- `writeTimeoutUncertain`
- `deferredCoins`
- `runBudgetTruncated`
- `deferredAt`
- `nextCursorStablecoinId`
- `cursorRecordedAt`
- `lastSuccessAt`
- `oldestFreshAgeSec`

`errorCoins` includes active adapter failures even before a coin has ever produced a successful live snapshot; those rows no longer remain hidden inside `missingCoins`.
`corruptCoins` counts rows where a matching latest-success snapshot exists in D1 but fails strict integrity validation, so the system fails closed to fallback presentation instead of serving truncated or malformed live data.
When a coin has both an old latest-success snapshot and a newer failing attempt state, the overview now prioritizes the active `error` / `degraded` attempt classification over generic `stale` labeling so status surfaces better reflect live incidents.
`writeTimeoutUncertain` counts coins whose latest attempt hit the D1 write-timeout / finalize-rejection path and whose authoritative success state could not be proven by readback.
`runBudgetTruncated`, `deferredCoins`, `deferredAt`, and `nextCursorStablecoinId` mirror the newest checkpoint attempt when that attempt still has an active next-item resume pointer, so terminal newer attempts cannot resurrect older orphaned pointers on status surfaces. The pointer survives a run-budget truncation and is replayed by the five-minute `reserve-recovery` lane; it is cleared only when the checkpoint reaches the queue end. Atomic authority/history persistence removes the history-gap reconciliation and repair subsystem.

## API Contract

`handleStablecoinReserves()` in `worker/src/api/stablecoin-reserves.ts` is only available for known tracked IDs that also declare `liveReservesConfig`.

404 behavior:

- Unknown stablecoin ID
- Tracked coin without live reserve support

Known live-enabled IDs with no usable snapshot or curated/template fallback return HTTP `200` with `mode: "unavailable"`, empty `reserves`, and live `sync` state. This distinguishes supported coins awaiting usable data from unknown or unsupported IDs.

Successful responses return `StablecoinReservesResponse` with one of these modes:

| Mode                | Meaning                                                                                    |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `live`              | Fresh live snapshot from `reserve_composition`                                             |
| `live-stale`        | Stored fetch is older than 48 hours, or verified source evidence exceeds its configured/adapter reporting allowance (48-hour fallback only when neither declares one) |
| `curated-fallback`  | Live snapshot unavailable; falling back to curated `StablecoinMeta.reserves`               |
| `template-fallback` | Live snapshot unavailable; falling back to reserve templates from `getReserves()`          |
| `unavailable`       | Coin is live-enabled, but neither live data nor fallback reserve presentation is available |

`live` / `live-stale` only apply when the stored snapshot matches the latest successful sync state by `fetched_at` / `attempt_id` and passes strict integrity validation. Staleness independently checks the stored fetch time against 48 hours and verified upstream source age against the source-specific allowance. The detail response, overview freshness counts, and scoring snapshot loader share this decision; refreshing an old document never resets its source age. Orphaned partial writes or corrupt stored snapshots fail closed to the fallback modes.

`StablecoinReservesResponseSchema` in `shared/types/live-reserves.ts` is the runtime contract for successful `200` responses, including `unavailable`, and is used by the frontend reserve API client. Adapter-specific `metadata`, `metadata.details`, and nested redemption telemetry remain passthrough. Internal instrumentation lives in declared `metadata.diag`, which `resolveReserveResult()` removes before public serialization.

Cache control:

| Response mode                               | Cache-Control                        |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| `live` (sync `ok`, no uncertain write)      | `public, s-maxage=3600, max-age=300` |
| `live` (sync not `ok`, or `uncertainWrite`) | `public, s-maxage=300, max-age=60`   |
| `live-stale`                                | `public, s-maxage=1800, max-age=120` |
| fallback / unavailable modes                | `public, s-maxage=300, max-age=60`   |

The optional `provenance` object is present only when the response is serving an authoritative `live` or `live-stale` snapshot:

| Field             | Meaning                                                                               |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `evidenceClass`   | `independent`, `static-validated`, or `weak-live-probe`                               |
| `sourceModel`     | `dynamic-mix`, `validated-static`, or `single-bucket`                                 |
| `freshnessMode`   | Optional explicit freshness policy (`verified`, `unverified`, `not-applicable`)       |
| `scoringEligible` | Whether the current snapshot is eligible for V9 Backing/dependency fact compilation right now |

The optional `displayBadge` object is also present only for authoritative `live` / `live-stale` snapshots:

| Field   | Meaning                                                                                      |
| ------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `kind`  | `live`, `curated-validated`, or `proof`                                                      |
| `label` | User-facing badge text rendered on the detail page (`Live`, `Curated-Validated`, or `Proof`) |

`displayBadge` is intentionally separate from `mode` and `provenance`:

- `mode` answers whether an authoritative snapshot exists and whether it is stale
- `provenance` answers scoring/evidence semantics
- `displayBadge` answers the honest user-facing reserve label

The optional `displayUrl` and `evidenceUrls` fields are also intentionally separate:

- `displayUrl` is the curated reserve-card destination configured in `liveReservesConfig.display.url`
- `evidenceUrls` are adapter-emitted URLs tied to the authoritative live snapshot metadata
- the detail page can show both when a coin has a curated overview page plus narrower evidence links for the exact live snapshot

The optional `metadata` object is also present only for authoritative `live` / `live-stale` snapshots. It exposes the adapter snapshot metadata already stored with the reserve snapshot row so the UI can surface feed-specific context without re-querying D1. For example, `crvusd` now exposes `yieldBasisCollateralPct` when Yield Basis positions account for part of the live reserve mix.

The optional `sync` object exposes the last operational state:

| Field             | Meaning                                                                                                                             |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `enabled`         | Coin is live-enabled                                                                                                                |
| `status`          | Last sync status (`ok`, `degraded`, `error`, `skipped`)                                                                             |
| `stale`           | Latest successful live snapshot is older than 48 hours                                                                              |
| `bootstrap`       | No successful live snapshot has been recorded yet                                                                                   |
| `lastAttemptedAt` | Latest attempt timestamp, when present                                                                                              |
| `lastSuccessAt`   | Latest success timestamp, when present                                                                                              |
| `warnings[]`      | Warning messages surfaced by the latest attempt and, when relevant, storage-integrity warnings injected by the fail-closed resolver |
| `lastError`       | Most recent adapter error message (truncated to 200 chars), when present                                                            |
| `failureCategory` | Machine-readable failure class from attempt metadata, when present                                                                  |
| `uncertainWrite`  | `true` when the latest attempt hit the D1 write-timeout / finalize-rejection path and authoritative state could not be proven       |

Uncertain write attempts are intentionally exposed as `sync.uncertainWrite = true` instead of being collapsed into generic stale/error narration. The API may still serve the last consistent snapshot or fallback presentation, but operators can tell that the latest attempted write is ambiguous until a clean follow-up run resolves it. Detail-page polling uses the normal 4-hour reserve cadence only for clean live responses; live responses with `sync.status !== "ok"` or `sync.uncertainWrite = true` use recovery polling and render the active status, failure category, and last error above the reserve card footer.

### Edge Cache Implications for Monitoring

A `live` response with a healthy sync (`sync.status === "ok"` and no uncertain write) is edge-cached for 1 hour (`s-maxage=3600`). When the latest sync is not `ok` or has an `uncertainWrite`, the same `live` response uses the fallback edge cache (`s-maxage=300`, 5 minutes) so the frontend's 2-minute recovery polling can observe the failure and its recovery instead of being served a stale healthy `sync` block for up to an hour.

**For real-time monitoring**, use the Access-gated admin surface (`/admin/`, backed by admin-only `GET /api/status`), which is never edge-cached and always reflects current D1 state.

Fallback, template-fallback, and unavailable responses use a shorter edge cache (`s-maxage=300`, 5 minutes). `live-stale` responses use an intermediate cache (`s-maxage=1800`, 30 minutes); healthy live responses use `s-maxage=3600` (1 hour).

`collateral-positions-api` can now optionally attach direct redemption-capacity telemetry alongside the collateral mix when a reviewed bridge-backed stable exit exists. `zchf-frankencoin` uses this path to publish the current CHFAU StablecoinBridge inventory as `redemption.capacityUsd` for redemption-backstop modeling without changing the reserve-slice composition itself. `cap-vault` reads Cap cUSD's Ethereum vault state directly. Reserve slices are based on each supported asset's total supplied balance, while redemption-capacity telemetry uses unpaused available balances after borrows so the route does not treat borrowed or paused collateral as immediate exit capacity. The adapter emits nested `metadata.redemption` with `capacityKind = "live-direct-bounded"`, `freshnessKind = "same-run-onchain"`, `routeStatusSource = "onchain"` from per-asset pause checks, and zero-second settlement-delay telemetry. `3jane-usd3` reads USD3's Ethereum strategy, MorphoCredit market, waUSDC conversion, and idle USDC accounting directly. It splits the strategy NAV between the pro-rata liquid waUSDC/USDC buffer and deployed private-credit exposure, then uses `availableWithdrawLimit(address(0))` as direct bounded USDC redemption capacity. The same-run telemetry also carries shutdown state, the configured commitment delay, and the documented zero withdrawal fee.

`idle-cdo-epoch-variant` reads an Idle/Pareto credit vault's own epoch accounting. These vaults are deliberately not ERC-4626 — `asset()` and `totalAssets()` revert, and deposited underlying is forwarded straight to the whitelisted borrower's wallet — so the adapter uses the CDO's `getContractValue()`, `lastNAVAA()` / `lastNAVBB()`, `epochDuration()`, `epochEndDate()`, `defaulted()`, and `isEpochRunning()`, plus the tranche's `totalSupply()` and `minter()`, in one multicall round. `getContractValue()` is by construction the unlent underlying balance plus the valued receivable, so `getContractValue() - underlying.balanceOf(cdo)` is exactly the borrower receivable with no double counting. The receivable is published as a `private-credit` slice **with no `coinId`** — it is not a claim on the deposit token, and linking it would present a single-obligor loan book as that token's reserves; the cash-equivalent slice is emitted only when the vault actually holds an unlent balance, never assumed. The vault's `token()`, `AATranche()` / `BBTranche()`, and the tranche's `minter()` are all verified against config before any composition is published. The credit slice's liquidity horizon comes from the structural `epochDuration`, not the time remaining in the current cycle, and drops to `unknown` once `defaulted()` is true. A senior tranche with no live junior NAV beneath it degrades with `idle-cdo-no-junior-subordination` rather than being re-rated upward for its seniority label. When the config declares `expectJuniorTranche: false` for a reviewed single-tranche vault — as `aa-falconx-mev-capital` does — the missing junior is recorded as an `info` structural risk fact instead of a `degraded` warning, leaving the first-loss reality on the slice's `risk`/`riskFactors`. NAV reconciliation compares the tranche NAV sum against `getContractValue()` net of `unclaimedFees()`, so accrued protocol fees alone no longer manufacture a reconciliation degrade. The adapter declares no redemption telemetry: the monthly epoch exit's stressed depth is not observable on-chain, and publishing capacity from NAV would fabricate it. `aa-falconx-mev-capital` binds this adapter; it keeps `variantOf: usdc-circle` and its serial USDC dependency edge while its backing is scored from the honest composition instead of inherited from Circle.

`origin-vault-balances`, `blast-usdb-yield-manager`, and `nest-vault-positions` cover additional strategy-vault reserve sources without widening generic adapters. Origin reads OUSD Vault `checkBalance(asset)` plus `totalValue()` so only mapped assets are credited, and it separately reads idle ERC-20 `balanceOf(vault)` as direct bounded redemption capacity while excluding deployed strategy balances. The vault contract publishes no ERC-20 `decimals()` of its own, so `totalValue()` is scaled by `decimals()` read from the tracked coin's own deployment at the same pinned block, required to equal that deployment's reviewed decimals (otherwise the attempt fails closed) and published as `details.totalValueDecimals`; an assumed scale would misplace the `Unmapped Origin vault exposure` remainder by twelve orders of magnitude. Blast reads Ethereum USDYieldManager `totalValue()` and reconciles it against Blast USDB supply. Nest consumes the Nest Alpha Vault positions, NAV, and last-price-update APIs, preserving the latest verified update timestamp, classifying tokenized JTRSY/USTB fund shares at the existing `low` tokenized-layer tier, and grouping private/structured vault exposure as high risk. nOPAL and nBASIS additionally reconcile explicitly reported pending deposits using strict pending-transaction validation; pending subscriptions remain a separate high-risk unknown slice and are never counted as settled fund shares. Positive pending withdrawals remain fail-closed except reviewed nBASIS USCC redemption receivables: the issuer sibling `/calculated-price` endpoint must corroborate settled positions plus all pending movements against gross NAV, and gross NAV less nonnegative claimable fees against net NAV, each within $0.01, with no upstream errors. The corroborated net NAV must also match the vault's own `/price.nav`, the second valuation of the same vault, within a documented cross-source tolerance (1e-4 relative with a $1 absolute floor; live reads differ by ~$0.13 on a $184k NAV): a divergence fails the attempt closed naming both values as `corroboration-mismatch` rather than suppressing the published-NAV residual and reporting reconciled coverage over a contradiction. Admitted receivables remain a separate high-risk unknown slice; total reserves use corroborated gross NAV while calculated net NAV and fees remain separate metadata. Missing, malformed, or mismatched corroboration fails closed. Other Nest vaults retain settled-only accounting. Any Nest vault whose mapped positions (plus admitted pending deposits) cover less than its reported NAV now publishes an explicit high-risk `Nest NAV reconciliation residual` slice and `unknownExposurePct`, rather than silently dropping the unexplained share (previously gated to nOPAL).

`escrow-balance` covers coins whose exit route is paid out of one pinned escrow or reserve contract, where redemption capacity is a single token-denominated view read. Component sums are reconciled against both published totals with a tight absolute tolerance: Bridge rounds its headline totals to cents (pathUSD's components sum $0.32 high), so drift up to $1 is treated as rounding while larger drift publishes as a degraded `bridge-component-sum-drift` warning, and a liability shortfall beyond the same tolerance publishes the below-1 ratio with `reserve-undercollateralized`. `last_updated` dates the reserve observation (it holds across repeated requests and moves only when balances change), so it is a `verified` source timestamp under the dashboard age tier. The origin class is `issuer-attested` — independent measurement of an issuer-published feed, mirroring the frax/tether balance-sheet precedent, not third-party assurance. No redemption capacity is derived from the cash label. The configured `params.slug` must match the primary URL's final path segment, so one coin's binding can never silently read another stablecoin's payload.

`megausd-custody` reads MegaUSD's public `backing-and-supply/current` REST endpoint for `usdm-mega`. `backingAssets` amounts are summed per asset across custodian entries; USDC maps to `usdc-circle` and USDtb to `usdtb-ethena`. USDm's own self-held balance is excluded from backing as self-referential (an info warning fires if it is ever nonzero), and any future unmapped backing-asset key degrades into an "unmapped" bucket rather than failing the sync closed. The endpoint's local `supply` field is a single-chain figure that does not represent the tracked global supply, so it is never used as the liability denominator: the adapter persists `totalReserveUsd` without a `collateralizationRatio`, and `lastUpdatedAt` is a verified source timestamp under `DASHBOARD_SOURCE_MAX_AGE_SEC` (3 days). The diagnostic source origin is `issuer-attested` (the issuer's custody inventory), separate from the unchanged score-bearing `evidenceClass: "independent"`. Numeric strings in `backingAssets` amounts are deliberately converted, and a non-numeric value fails the sync closed instead of silently summing as zero.

`krwq-custodian` reads KRWQ's public `custodian-assets` REST endpoint (`https://www.krwq.cash/api/custodian-assets`) for `krwq-iq`. The payload's three legs — USDC custodian, frxUSD custodian, and Base treasury USDC (documented as the Korean Treasury Bond transition bucket) — map to `usdc-circle` and `frxusd-frax` and are cross-verified against on-chain `balanceOf` on the pinned Circle/frxUSD token addresses (the Ethereum legs read in one Multicall3 aggregate, the Base treasury as a second-chain balance). Published stable-token amounts are derived from readable on-chain raw balances using the pinned token decimals; the issuer display value cannot independently resize or erase a leg. Unreadable balances retain the issuer amount only with a `degraded` warning, and raw-amount mismatches also degrade. The adapter pins the three holders reviewed on September 15, 2026 and checks both Ethereum custodians’ live `KRWT_TOKEN()` and `CUSTODIAN_TKN()` getters in the existing Multicall3 request. Changed issuer holders, unreadable getters, or mismatched token bindings emit degraded `krwq-holder-unverified`; balance reads always target the reviewed holders. The treasury Safe’s shared 3-of-5 control with the custodian-owner Safe is dated review provenance, not continuous owner monitoring or legal segregation evidence. Proxy upgrades remain a custody/control risk: token getters establish current identity, not immutable future behavior, and owner recovery can withdraw collateral. Multichain `totalSupply` is retained only in `metadata.details.grossSupplyTokens`, denominated in KRWQ, with omitted-chain diagnostics. It is neither USD-valued nor a reconciled circulating liability perimeter, so the adapter withholds `supplyUsd` and `collateralizationRatio` until FX and bridge/treasury exclusions are verified. The payload's ISO `timestamp` is a verified source timestamp under `DASHBOARD_SOURCE_MAX_AGE_SEC`. The reviewed treasury source key classifies the observed Base USDC balance; it does not claim that the planned Korean Treasury Bond sleeve already exists.

`kerne-signed-por` reads Kerne's hourly EIP-191-signed proof of reserves (`https://app.kerne.fi/api/por/signed`) for `kusd-kerne`. It rehashes `signed_payload_canonical` to `attestation_hash`, recovers the signer over the raw SHA-256 digest against the pinned `params.signerAddress` (failing closed on hash or signer mismatch), then reads the combined USDC `balanceOf` at the configured Base PSM contracts in one Multicall3 aggregate and degrades rather than fails when that cross-check read or divergence occurs. The signed `timestamp` is `verified` freshness under the issuer's published 7,800-second stale threshold, and the single measured slice is `kerne-signed-por:psm-usdc` (`usdc-circle` collateral) with `collateralizationRatio = psm_usdc_reserve / outstanding_kusd`.

`ethena-whitelabel` reads Ethena's whitelabel transparency endpoint (one `data[]` payload shared by every whitelabel stablecoin, selected by `params.stablecoin`) for `suiusde-sui`. The adapter sums the `custodians` array, never the display-level `rows` projection (which merges USDe+USDC under one custodian into a single "USDe/USDC" row), and maps USDe to `usde-ethena` and USDC to `usdc-circle`. Custodian rows on the off-chain Coinbase Prime network are kept as a separate slice without a `coinId` and surfaced with an informational `off-chain-custody` warning so the adapter never describes every row as on-chain verified; a genuinely unmapped asset degrades into an "unmapped" bucket instead of failing closed. `sourceTimestamp` comes from the payload's millisecond-epoch `lastUpdated`, and `collateralizationRatio` is the summed custodian backing over `totalSupply`.

`tether-transparency` reads Tether's public `transparency.json` (one payload shared by `usdt-tether` and `xaut-tether`, selected by `params.currencyIso`). This is a coarse issuer balance-sheet feed like `frax-balance-sheet`: it does not itemize reserve composition, so `params.slices` carries a configured static mix reviewed alongside each coin's `collateral` disclosure, while the live payload validates `total_assets` / `total_liabilities` and freshness. `collateralizationRatio` is `total_assets / total_liabilities`; the USD-denominated `totalAssetsUsd`, `totalLiabilitiesUsd`, and `shareholderEquityUsd` metadata fields are only persisted for `usdt-tether`; XAUt's unit is gold-equivalent, not verified USD, so only the dimensionless ratio is kept. Per-chain issued liabilities (`totalAuthorized - notIssued`) and quarantined amounts from `data_formatted[].blockChains` populate `metadata.details.chains`, and a nonzero quarantined balance on any chain raises an info warning. The freshness timestamp comes from the entry's `id` (Unix seconds), validated against `DISCLOSURE_SOURCE_MAX_AGE_SEC` (the 7-day disclosure tier) rather than the 3-day `DASHBOARD_SOURCE_MAX_AGE_SEC` tier, because the feed publishes on a Friday-publish/weekend-skip cadence that left no margin under the narrower bound. USDT liabilities are substantially reconstructible from the known native ledgers, and XAUt's canonical Ethereum liability is directly reconstructible, but the live reserve-asset totals are not: cash, securities, loans, custody, and vaulted gold remain off-chain between quarterly BDO assurance dates. The diagnostic source origin therefore remains `issuer-attested`, and the detail page uses the proof-style badge rather than claiming an independently measured live reserve feed. The separate `evidenceClass: "independent"` remains unchanged pending a score-methodology decision.

`ondo-ousg` replaces OUSG's NAV-only composition with the dated seven-position portfolio embedded in `https://ondo.finance/ousg`. The bounded parser requires the tokenized-interest scope disclosure, one portfolio object, seven reviewed stable Sanity IDs with matching names/symbols, a source date within the four-day business-day limit, and amounts that sum to the declared total within one cent. New, missing or duplicated positions fail closed. Each ID supplies a stable `ondo-ousg:position:<id>` classification key; Other Assets remain explicit unknown exposure. The HTML is consumed before the existing NAV reader runs, preserving its redemption telemetry and other warnings while replacing only the unverified-composition warning. Portfolio amounts retain their own observation date; current NAV and supply diagnostics do not create a mixed-date coverage ratio. Origin is `issuer-attested`, distinct from an independent examination. The measured BENJI dependency remains linked with a reviewed quarantined-target disposition; it does not acquire an upstream score.

`midas-mtbill` reads the issuer's mTBILL transparency JSON. Its `main_positions` values are USD millions at $1,000 display precision, not dollar units. Runtime accepts only the reviewed USTB/BUIDL tuple identities, equal positive PV/delta, and an Ethereum wallet-only portfolio whose total assets equal equity. Named positions cannot exceed total equity; differences between named amounts and total remain an explicit unclassified residual rather than being allocated to either fund. Rounded display totals have a bounded $1,000 reconciliation tolerance. Source timestamps must be current under the three-day dashboard policy, and the ordinary unknown-exposure cap still applies. Three stable classification keys distinguish USTB, BUIDL and the residual. The issuer source is not an independent examination and produces no circulating-liability or collateralization-ratio claim. Other Midas strategies keep their NAV composition warning until complete holdings, liabilities and counterparty scope are established; the mMEV archive's joint mMEV/mROX total is not mMEV-only backing.

`rlusd-independent-assurance` replaces RLUSD's configured May composition fallback with reviewed Deloitte PDF evidence. The official Ripple archive's month/year report links must resolve to the newest reviewed manifest; runtime checks the allowed report host, exact PDF bytes/hash and assets/liabilities reconciliation through the shared independent-assurance verifier. The July 31, 2026 17:00 EDT observation contains $1,575,160,913 reserves and $1,463,694,758 liabilities, with Treasury bills, government MMFs and cash extracted from the visually reviewed page-five table. Signing, publication and fetch dates cannot refresh the observation. The reviewed report uses `NEXT_MONTH_DISCLOSURE_SOURCE_MAX_AGE_SEC` (70 days), not Gemini's separate 75-day examination exception: Ripple publishes each month-end report 25–29 days later, so this observation expires October 9 at 21:00:00 UTC. The text-only offline compiler does not support that table: refresh requires downloading the newest official PDF, rendering and reviewing its table, recording its exact byte hash/length and observation time, and reconciling the transcribed components against the extracted report totals before updating the manifest and sidecar. GBPm's optional `mento.params.cdpSystem` pins ActivePool, DefaultPool and collateral-token addresses. - Shared bucketed-composition accumulation and classification helpers (`classification.ts`) reject non-finite or negative rows before classification; zero rows are ignored. Unknown-exposure warnings use the adapter's reviewed policy threshold (strictly greater than the maximum, matching validation), rather than a separate hardcoded cutoff. Cap reports unconfigured asset value, Origin includes the measured coverage residual, Nest includes unmapped positions and reconciliation residuals, and DOLA includes non-FiRM issuance relative to fresh circulating supply; unavailable DOLA supply degrades the observation instead of claiming zero unknown exposure.
- Shared unverified-freshness metadata helper so timestamp-less dashboard feeds explain why they remain non-scoring, plus a not-applicable freshness helper for reviewed latest-state on-chain/API reads
- DefiLlama valuation quotes (`fetchDefiLlamaPrices`) require timestamps no older than one day and confidence at least 0.8 for clean admission. Missing/failed quality evidence emits a degraded warning; a numeric value is retained only with that warning. Upstream quote requests use sorted, deduplicated asset identities, while each caller receives its own logical-key price map and warnings, including on cache hits. Branch underlying-token and tracked-cache substitution requires an explicit reviewed `underlyingPrice1to1: true`; fixed prices and configured `priceToken` remain explicit configuration choices. Collateral-position APIs use the oldest per-asset timestamp for fully timestamped baskets, unverified freshness with a warning for partial coverage, and not-applicable freshness when no timestamps exist. ### Adding a New Adapter

To register a new adapter for a coin's `liveReservesConfig.adapter`, edit these surfaces in order: **5 files, 6 edit sites** (4 files / 5 sites when the adapter takes no per-coin params). The shared schema and Worker registry tests fail if definition or fetcher coverage drifts.

1. **Params schema + descriptor declaration** — both live in `shared/types/live-reserve-adapter-declarations.ts`. Define the Zod params schema as a const above the table (or reuse `noParamsSchema` / an existing schema), then add one entry to `LIVE_RESERVE_ADAPTER_DESCRIPTOR_DECLARATIONS` whose `paramsSchema` **references that schema object directly** — there is no separate schema module and no string identifier to register. Declare accepted primary input kinds, source/evidence class, source-sharing policy, supported semantics/versions, redemption telemetry, validation policy, and only non-default provenance or display metadata. Prefer a named validation tier from `shared/types/live-reserve-adapter-policy.ts` (`LATEST_STATE_VALIDATION`, `DASHBOARD_VALIDATION`, `DASHBOARD_WITH_UNKNOWN_CAP_VALIDATION`, `MONTHLY_VERIFIED_VALIDATION`, `LATE_MONTHLY_VERIFIED_VALIDATION`, `DISCLOSURE_VALIDATION`, …); an inline `validation` block should carry a comment explaining why the adapter is an exception. EVM addresses use `EvmAddressSchema`, never a bare `z.string()`. The key union, `LIVE_RESERVE_ADAPTER_KEYS`, and the enriched `LIVE_RESERVE_ADAPTER_DEFINITIONS` map all derive from this entry.
2. **Adapter fetch function** — add `worker/src/cron/reserve-adapters/<key>.ts` exporting `async function fetch<Name>Reserves(coin, config, signal, ctx?): Promise<AdapterResult>`. Adapter contract lives in `worker/src/cron/reserve-adapters/types.ts` (`AdapterFn`, `AdapterContext`, `ReserveAdapterDefinition` — the last is a `Pick<>` of the shared declaration, so declaration fields reach the Worker without being re-declared). Use helpers from `./helpers` rather than rebuilding fetch/parse/freshness primitives.
3. **Worker fetcher wiring** — import the fetch function and add it to `LIVE_RESERVE_ADAPTER_FETCHERS` in `worker/src/cron/reserve-adapters/index.ts`. This stays a hand-written map because Worker implementations cannot enter the runtime-neutral shared registry and several keys deliberately share one fetcher; its `Record<LiveReserveAdapterKey, AdapterFn>` annotation makes a missing key a compile error, and `registry.test.ts` fails on the same gap.
4. **Coin config** — bind the coin's `liveReservesConfig` in `shared/data/stablecoins/coins/<id>.json`. A `params` block is required whenever the adapter's schema rejects `{}`.
5. **Adapter test and fixture** — add `worker/src/cron/reserve-adapters/__tests__/<key>.test.ts` and capture an HTTP-html fixture when applicable. HTML, JSON, and txt fixtures are all age-gated by `scripts/ci/check-html-fixture-age.ts` under the same `captured-at` stamp rule; a JSON or txt fixture that cannot carry the HTML-comment header is exempted in that script with a documented reason.

Docs are not a per-adapter step: the Adapter Registry notes above are for non-obvious semantics only, and the roster/counts are derived from the declaration table rather than copied.

Minimal scaffold (HTTP-json single-asset shape):

```ts
import type { StablecoinMeta } from "@shared/types/core";
import type { LiveReservesConfig } from "@shared/types/live-reserves";
import { parseLiveReserveAdapterParams } from "@shared/lib/live-reserve-adapters";
import type { AdapterContext, AdapterResult } from "./types";
import {
  fetchJsonWithRetry,
  freshnessMetadataFromTimestamp,
  parseTimestampLikeToUnixSeconds,
  requireJsonInput,
} from "./helpers";

interface MyAdapterPayload {
  totalReserves: number;
  updatedAt?: string;
}

export async function fetchMyAdapterReserves(
  _coin: StablecoinMeta,
  config: LiveReservesConfig,
  signal: AbortSignal,
  ctx?: AdapterContext,
): Promise<AdapterResult> {
  const input = requireJsonInput(config.inputs.primary, "my-adapter");
  const params = parseLiveReserveAdapterParams("my-adapter", config.params);
  const payload = await fetchJsonWithRetry<MyAdapterPayload>(input.url, signal, 12_000, ctx);
  const sourceTimestamp = parseTimestampLikeToUnixSeconds(payload.updatedAt);

  return {
    slices: [{ name: params.assetLabel, pct: 100, risk: params.assetRisk }],
    metadata: {
      ...freshnessMetadataFromTimestamp(sourceTimestamp, "issuer-api", "payload has no source timestamp"),
    },
  };
}
```

## Frontend Consumers

Reserve composition and collateralization footers separate the source/report-as-of date from the last checked time, retain a stale label for out-of-window evidence, and never label a refreshed historical attestation as a new live measurement. Attestation-mix snapshots use the proof badge with an `Attestation` label. A source-age-only degradation explains that evidence is out of date; collection failures retain their separate sync-error notice.

- `src/hooks/use-stablecoin-reserves.ts` uses mode-aware polling: `live` responses follow the 4-hour reserve producer cadence (`staleTime = 4 hours` / `refetchInterval = 8 hours`), while stale or fallback modes tighten to `1 minute` / `2 minutes` so the UI re-checks recovery faster
- `src/hooks/use-stablecoin-detail-view-model.ts` injects the reserve result into the detail-page view model
- `src/lib/coverage.ts` uses the adapter badge taxonomy in `shared/lib/live-reserve-display.ts` so `/coverage` distinguishes true `Live` reserve feeds from `Curated-Validated` and `Proof` reserve-sync paths

## Scope Boundaries

- Live reserve sync is detail-page and status-surface infrastructure, not a replacement for curated reserve metadata everywhere else.
- [Safety Score V9](./report-cards.md) uses fresh authoritative independent live reserve slices as Backing reserve exposures when available. `dynamic-mix` adapters can qualify when the retained snapshot passes the shared admission gates, regardless of a later failed attempt; only a subset of `single-bucket` adapters carry `evidenceClass = independent`, while `validated-static` / `weak-live-probe` feeds remain detail/status data only. The compiler reconciles accepted tracked slices to effective dependency edges, and falls back to reviewed static/curated rows only when no accepted live rows were captured.
- Reviewed metadata joins to live reserve rows by unique adapter-owned `sourceKey` when present, with unique normalized-name matching retained only for unkeyed compatibility captures. Percentages never define the join, and classification plus dependency compilation share the same matched identities.
- Blacklist attribution no longer treats live reserves as invisible just because most adapters lack `coinId` links. The report-card resolver enriches both live and curated reserve names with the same blacklist clue pipeline, then resolves inherited exposure to a fixed point across the tracked set so cyclic upstream graphs do not depend on traversal order. The collateral drift alert only compares comparable live reserve mixes: snapshots must still resolve to at least two live slices after normalization, so single-bucket proofs and collapsed one-slice snapshots do not generate noisy curated-vs-live drift alerts.
- [Dependency Map](./dependency-map.md) remains authoritative for graph behavior; dependency edges come from the effective V9 dependency fact set, including accepted live reserve links when present and reviewed static/curated reserve metadata plus manual dependencies on the defined fallback path.
