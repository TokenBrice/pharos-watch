# Depeg Duration Resolver (DDR)

> **Agent navigation** — Grep the heading you need: Methodology Versioning · Trigger & Scope · Public Forecast Contract · Stage 1 — Resolution Outlook · Stage 2 — Expected Duration · DDRR — Depeg Duration Resolver Reviewer · Honest Limitations & Failure Modes · Validation / Backtest Plan · Data Plumbing.

When Pharos confirms an active depeg, the Depeg Duration Resolver answers the two questions an analyst actually asks, in order, as a public forecast contract instead of a live-moving widget:

1. **Will it come back?** — a *Resolution Outlook*: an ordinal evidence class (No break signals / Stress signals / Terminal evidence / Insufficient Signal) driven by transparent mechanistic rules over the coin's structure and the depeg's fingerprint. **No fitted ML, no false-precision forecast probability** — the death-label corpus is too thin to fit a supervised terminal classifier. Each verdict shows the contributing factors.
2. **When?** — an *Expected Duration*: an empirical landmark cumulative-incidence estimate over Pharos's clean depeg-incident corpus, conditioned on the depeg's structural stratum (depth, direction, structural class, peg currency), with explicit data-sufficiency gates and per-horizon (6h / 24h / 7d / 30d) recovery odds. Since DDRv5 the odds are unconditional: comparable incidents that ended terminal count as a competing outcome and still-open ones are censored, so the estimate keeps non-recovery visible instead of describing recovered incidents only.

Stage 2 only renders when Stage 1 is not Terminal evidence. The result is a two-readout module: a verdict with reasons, and — when recovery is plausible — a "typically resolves within X–Y" band. Since DDRv4, that headline readout is frozen once per canonical incident when the incident reaches forecast readiness or the 72h backstop, and later live facts are shown separately.

DDR is **not investment advice and not a credit rating.** A "Terminal evidence" verdict is a structural read, not a guarantee, and the inverse is equally true.

**Tier labels.** The wire tier codes are unchanged across versions: `recovery_likely`, `at_risk`, `recovery_unlikely`, `insufficient_signal`. DDRv5 changes what they mean and how they are labelled: `recovery_likely` reads **No break signals**, `at_risk` reads **Stress signals**, and `recovery_unlikely` reads **Terminal evidence**. Predictions locked before `v5.0` keep their original labels (Recovery Likely / At Risk / Recovery Unlikely) because they were issued under the earlier rubric.

## Trigger & Scope

DDR maintains one official public lock outcome per **canonical confirmed depeg incident**. The live `/api/depeg-resolver` board is active/current only, but each row is a stateful projection of the durable incident:

- `depeg_events.ended_at IS NULL` (the event is still open), and
- the tracked stablecoin has not already entered a terminal lifecycle state (`frozen`, `dead`, `defunct`, `failed`, or `cemetery`), and
- the event passes confirmation: legacy null verdicts remain eligible; known verdicts must not be in `DDR_INELIGIBLE_AUDIT_VERDICTS` (`false_positive`, `disputed`, `no_data`). Unknown non-null verdicts fail closed as of v4.6.

Closed and terminal events feed DDRR coverage and review, not new live DDR predictions. Terminal lifecycle events also leave the live DDR board even when the raw depeg row remains open: once registry status proves the asset is frozen/dead, the "time to repeg" question has no finite observable answer, so DDRR owns the audit result instead of DDR publishing an infinite-duration live incident. DDR inherits the clean confirmed-event stream from the [depeg detection pipeline](./depeg-detection.md) (100 bps USD / 150 bps non-USD thresholds plus multi-source confirmation), so it does not re-run depeg detection.

`shared/types/depeg-audit.ts` owns the five-verdict vocabulary and the distinct PegScore/DDR exclusion constants. Runtime predicates live in `shared/lib/depeg-audit.ts`; Worker SQL eligibility uses the bound `auditVerdictNotInSql()` helper. Migration `0252_depeg_audit_verdict_vocabulary.sql` adds vocabulary guards without rewriting archives or applied baseline triggers. Known invalidating verdicts still require the existing sealed-prediction repair authorization and erratum; unknown writes are rejected outright.

## Public Forecast Contract

DDRv4 keeps the **forecast readiness or 72h backstop** lock contract introduced in DDRv3: exactly one official lock outcome per canonical incident key. The public identity is `incidentKey`, not the mutable `depeg_events.id`, so delete/reinsert repair, merge, split, or start-time repair cannot silently create a second public prediction. The lock policy and readiness contract are methodology-owned:

- `DDR_PREDICTION_POLICY_VERSION = "sticky-24h-v1"` remains the stored public-prediction policy version for compatibility with existing policy-universe rows.
- `DDR_FORECAST_READINESS_VERSION = "readiness-72h-v1"`
- `DDR_FORECAST_READINESS_STRICT_EARLY_LOCK_THRESHOLD = 0.75`
- Readiness passes only when the forecast readiness score is **strictly greater than `0.75`**. A score equal to `0.75` is not ready.
- `DDR_FORECAST_READINESS_BACKSTOP_DELAY_SEC = 72 * 3600`
- `DDR_LOCK_ON_TIME_GRACE_SEC = 20 * 60`

The first healthy run that observes `readiness.score > 0.75` seals either:

- a frozen prediction (`prediction.state = "frozen"`) with the Stage 1 verdict, anchored duration, lock timestamp, lock timing, and first-publication metadata, or
- a no-call (`prediction.state = "no_call"`) when the run is healthy but row-level inputs are insufficient.

If no healthy run sees readiness before the backstop, the **first healthy run at or after `started_at + 72h`** seals under the same rules. The 72h backstop is not a weaker forecast-readiness score; it is the public accountability deadline for incidents that stay open without becoming ready sooner.

Before a readiness or backstop trigger, rows use `pending_lock` and show live facts plus forecast-readiness/backstop metadata without a verdict or duration. If a trigger arrives while global/system health predicates fail, rows use `lock_deferred`; system-health deferrals do not create no-calls, predictions, or accuracy samples. After a deferral, the first later healthy run seals if the incident is still unresolved and non-terminal, preserving the original trigger path and deferral count in immutable metadata. A promoted event's confirmation clock comes from `depeg_pending_outcomes`; when that read fails, its eligible row stays unsealed (the sealed verdict would otherwise freeze a default lock timing) and the run records `v2ConfirmationTimingError` plus `v2ConfirmationSealSkipped`. If sealing succeeds but the first-publication manifest has not finalized, rows use `publication_retry_pending`; the sealed verdict, duration, and no-call details remain hidden until first publication succeeds.

Frozen means frozen. Later prices, supply changes, methodology versions, better input coverage, or a worse peak do not mutate the official public prediction. Live incident status, current deviation, age, staleness, and degraded overlay details are shown as live facts beside the frozen lock outcome. Stale-snapshot warnings on the resolver list and stablecoin detail name the stale live overlay and retained frozen forecasts; they never claim anchored durations were suppressed.

DDRv5 leaves the lock contract, readiness version, threshold and backstop unchanged, but it moves what readiness sees. More rows read No break signals rather than Stress signals, and the readiness resolution component scores that class higher, so some incidents reach readiness and lock earlier than they would have under v4. Readiness copy now names the evidence class ("shows no break signals", "shows stress signals", "has terminal evidence"), and a `not_informative` horizon cell contributes `0.65` to the duration-support component. This is a lock-timing consequence of the rubric, not a change to the publication rule.

**Decision-input snapshot.** New rows carry `inputs` (`version: "ddr-inputs-v1"`, `DdrInputsSnapshotSchema` in `shared/types/depeg-resolver.ts`): the raw values the rubric read this run — break depth, admitted circulating USD, supply coverage and changes, exit inputs (liquidity score, 7-day and since-onset TVL change, redemption capacity ratio and route family, bank-run flag, V9 reason codes, stress request, executable USD and completion ratio), blacklist surge, and the structural registry projection. `null` means the input was unavailable, never zero. Because the row becomes the sealed `frozen.sourceRow`, the snapshot is covered by the frozen payload hash. Rows sealed before `v5.0` have no `inputs`; their factors remain the only replay evidence.

Only valid frozen `prediction` rows contribute to live forecast totals and verdict distributions on `/depeg/` and the homepage. Pending, deferred, delayed-publication, no-call, and invalidated rows retain explicit states but no live verdict or duration; invalidated original outcomes are audit-only. Cards label deviation-derived values as a percentage of peg, never as an observed currency price.

The manifest-only API fallback has no observed ongoing-event duration: `live.ageSec` is `null`, not elapsed wall time since `startedAt`. Its `live.peakDeviationBps` is the frozen source peak when available, otherwise `null`; the live facts and timeline render unavailable values as `—` rather than age 0 or peak 0 bps. Frozen forecast facts remain intact. Fallback expiry preserves the persisted `expiresAt`, or uses manifest `publishedAt + API_FRESHNESS_MAX_AGE_SEC.depegResolver` when absent, never a new request-time window.

**Stale is evaluated at serve time, not only at build time.** For an otherwise-fresh frozen row, `applyDurationStaleness` in `shared/lib/depeg-resolver/public-contract.ts` sets `live.stale = true` when `now > frozen.duration.medianResolveAt`, retaining an existing reason or adding `duration-exceeded`. Equality and a missing median do not trigger this path. The producer applies it at snapshot build; `GET /api/depeg-resolver` re-applies it to unexpired caches. Expired caches use the stale-cache/closed-pending-review overlay instead. Frozen duration evidence never changes.
When a refresh cannot run but a validated prior public snapshot exists, the Worker carries those rows forward with `_meta.degradedReason = "stale-cache"` so the DDR cards remain renderable. The failed-run diagnostic is separate in `_meta.degradedReasonDetail` (for example `stablecoins-cache-unsafe`, `depeg-pipeline-unhealthy`, `stablecoins-cache-stale`, or a comma-joined scheduler pair). The carried snapshot's `_meta.computedAt` remains the original observation time; `_meta.lastRefreshAttemptAt` records the failed refresh attempt, while `_meta.expiresAt` may advance for the next serve window. Missing or invalid snapshots retain their direct degraded reason and have no carried rows. The cache schema and `/api/depeg-resolver` preserve both fields for the client.

Errata and lock-deferral reads distinguish a successfully empty overlay from unreadable evidence. A failed read preserves accepted immutable forecast facts but returns `_meta.degraded = true`, `_meta.degradedReason = "secondary-overlay-unavailable"` and `_meta.degradedReasonDetail` naming `errata-overlay-read-failed` and/or `lock-deferral-overlay-read-failed`; row live overlays also name the failed dependency. Such responses are `no-store` and do not attest that current corrections or deferrals are absent.

Lock trigger and readiness metadata are part of the immutable public exposure. Public prediction metadata records `lockTrigger` as `forecast_readiness`, `readiness_backstop`, or legacy/default `scheduled_24h`; the stores preserve the same trigger plus optional `readiness` and `backstop` objects. Legacy rows without explicit trigger metadata default to `scheduled_24h`. The public `readiness` object preserves `version`, `score`, `threshold`, `strictEarlyLockReady`, reasons, and weighted components, while `backstop` preserves `version`, `delaySec`, `backstopAt`, and `reached`. Deferral count, lock timing, and policy version stay attached to the same exposure. Errata may correct or invalidate an exposure, but they do not rewrite those lock-time fields in place.

### Incident lifecycle (pre-lock close, resurrection, flap adoption, regime split)

Canonical incidents keep `incident_state = 'active'` in D1. Pre-lock recovery uses a **derived** logical state driven by nullable `closed_pre_lock_at` (migration `worker/migrations/0228_depeg_resolver_incident_closed_pre_lock.sql`):

- **Pre-lock close reaper** (`closeRecoveredPreLockIncidents` in `worker/src/lib/depeg-resolver-incident-store.ts`, called directly from `worker/src/cron/compute-depeg-resolver.ts`): each run sets `closed_pre_lock_at` on unsealed active incidents whose **current** source event has an explicit recovery reason (`recovered-primary`, `recovered-dex`, or `recovered-native`) and is past `ended_at + DDR_INCIDENT_REOPEN_MERGE_WINDOW_SEC (6h) + DDR_PRE_LOCK_CLOSE_SETTLE_MARGIN_SEC_V1 (20min)`. Legacy rows whose `close_reason` is NULL fall back to a non-null `recovery_price`, matching the Stage 2 label rule; any other explicit closure reason (direction supersession, coverage-lost, orphan) never closes an incident pre-lock.
- **Resurrection:** a new same-coin/peg/direction live event that starts within the 6h merge window of a closed-pre-lock incident's closing `ended_at` reopens that incident (clears `closed_pre_lock_at`, append-only link + revision). Candidate selection prefers the most recent closed incident so older flap closes are not resurrected first.
- **Flap-tolerant unsealed adoption** lives in the primary link path (`canAutoRepairUnsealedTail`): recovered tails may adopt under strict same-coin/peg/direction ordered succession, `source === 'live'`, relative recency anchored at `current_started_at` (window = the run's policy delay, 72h under the readiness backstop), or succession within the 6h merge window after a recovered current. Caps: `DDR_FLAP_TOLERANT_MAX_LINK_COUNT_V1 = 30` links and `DDR_FLAP_TOLERANT_MAX_INCIDENT_SPAN_SEC_V1 = 21d` from `first_started_at`. Exceeding a cap quarantines rather than silently adopting.
- **Regime-escalation split** (sealed tails): when a live tail's peak depth is ≥ `DDR_SEALED_TAIL_REGIME_ESCALATION_MIN_PEAK_BPS_V1` (1000 bps) **and** ≥ `DDR_SEALED_TAIL_REGIME_ESCALATION_MULTIPLIER_V1` (4×) the sealed lock-time peak (`frozen.sourceRow.peakDeviationBps` on the immutable prediction payload — not the incident row's first-observed peak), the writer starts a **new** canonical incident (new key, new lock eligibility) instead of sealed-tail adoption. Same-regime tails may still adopt under the sealed-tail path.

Errata are append-only. If a source event or input is later proven wrong, DDR keeps the original first-publication exposure visible as `invalidated`, shows the original frozen prediction or no-call outcome, and attaches the latest erratum/history. Before any public prediction is sealed, nearby same-coin/same-direction source rows may be adopted into the unsealed canonical incident through append-only incident links and revisions. Once a public prediction exists, source-event repairs require explicit authorization/lineage rather than silent nearby-event linking. Automated sealed-tail repair uses runner/worker `createdBy` identities and never rewrites sealed prediction payloads.

Canonical public event histories require a successful incident-projection read even when that read returns no links. Projection failures never switch to the raw event universe: analytics throws `IncidentProjectionUnavailableError` with reason `incident-projection-read-failed`, retaining the previously accepted analytics cache. `/api/peg-summary` can serve that retained snapshot as degraded/no-store with its original computation clock; without one, it returns explicit `503` dependency unavailability. `/api/depeg-events` returns the same machine-readable dependency reason with `503` rather than double-counting constituent flap events.

### Repair debt and the repair-task runner

Repair-required events no longer halt the run. When the writer encounters an event that needs an explicit repair (ambiguous overlap with a canonical incident, or an unlinked event whose key maps to an existing incident, or a flap-tolerant cap miss), it quarantines that event for the run — no canonical incident, no fallback pseudo-incident, no public row — and continues resolving and publishing every other incident. The run records the quarantined event ids and reasons in `repairRequiredEvents` cron metadata and writes deterministic `worker_repair_tasks` rows with kind `ddr-repair-required-event`. DDR publication can stay otherwise healthy; `/api/status` derives the legacy DDR fields and the broader `dataQuality.repairDebt` summary from the task table, while the data-quality warning cause remains `ddr_repair_debt_present`. A newly quarantined event id also raises a one-shot `quarantine-repair-required` cron event, and every alerted id is appended to `cache["ddr:quarantine-alerted-event-ids"]`; that ledger is never trimmed, so an event that stays quarantined or is quarantined again never re-alerts and clearing the row is the only way to re-arm it. The `ddr_repair_debt_present` cause, not the alert, is the standing signal.

Repair-debt reconciliation compares every active event id as a string against one complete, deduplicated JSON subject set, keeping the closure statement within D1's bind limit even when 97 or more events are quarantined. It closes only obsolete open/deferred tasks and failed tasks whose backoff is due; claimed tasks and failed tasks still in backoff remain untouched. Independent exclusion chunks are unsafe because each chunk would treat other active subjects as obsolete. Successful upserts alone do not establish successful reconciliation: closure failures still propagate to the resolver's repair-task persistence error.

Before new locks, the resolver also checks each open policy-included source event against its linked canonical incident (stablecoin, canonical peg currency, direction, current event id, and start time). Genuine identity mismatches enter the same repair-debt quarantine before assessment/seal writes, without hiding an existing immutable public exposure. Currency identity follows `shared/lib/peg-taxonomy.ts`: `peggedREAL` and `peggedBRL` both mean `BRL`, and retained `REAL` currency identities remain compatible. Migration `0257_ddr_canonical_peg_currency_guard.sql` makes the durable seal guard use that alias equivalence while retaining every assessment/link/policy/open-event/timing predicate. Assessment creation, lock state, seal, and lock audit remain one ordered atomic batch; this change does not rewrite public predictions or change the lock methodology.

The daily `03:00` lane runs the real repair-task executor (`runWorkerRepairTaskRunner` in `worker/src/lib/repair-tasks.ts`) for allowlisted `ddr-repair-required-event` tasks that match the flap-tolerant safe class. It claims via `locked_by` / `locked_until` (lease `DDR_REPAIR_RUNNER_CLAIM_LEASE_SEC_V1 = 15min`), hard-caps at `DDR_REPAIR_RUNNER_BATCH_LIMIT_V1 = 5` rows per run, backs off failures with `DDR_REPAIR_RUNNER_BACKOFF_SEC_V1 = 6h`, and is gated by env `DDR_REPAIR_TASK_RUNNER_ENABLED` (default on; kill switch also blocks claim). Before writing it asserts identity/lineage invariants and the non-vacuous pending check against `depeg_resolver_lock_opportunity_audit`. Auto-repair counts land in cron metadata and `/api/status` unconditionally. Ambiguous cases defer rather than act — wrong repairs are append-only irreversible.

Execution exceptions publish cron `degraded` with reason `repair-execution-failed`, retaining isolated task failures and fenced backoff. Safe-class deferrals remain successful evidence; healthy sentinel modes cannot clear another source's retained failure.

Within each priority, due tasks are selected by durable `last_attempt_at` (never-attempted first, then least-recently attempted), with `attempt_count`, creation time, and task ID as tie-breakers. Repeatedly ambiguous deferrals therefore rotate behind newer unattempted debt without changing the five-row cap, claim fences, safety predicates, or retained deferred state.

Historical reviewed closures remain as durable migrations (for example `0178`, `0203`, `0206`, `0208`, `0209`, `0227` for BRLA/cNGN/EURQ flap lineages). Those migrations do not change DDR methodology or rewrite a public prediction; under v4 the primary path plus runner is intended to prevent new hand-written repair migrations for the same flap class.

Short depegs are not predicted after the fact. If a confirmed incident recovers before a healthy readiness/backstop lock, DDRR classifies it as `resolved_before_prediction` (including the closed-pre-lock cohort); reliable terminal evidence before a healthy lock becomes `terminal_before_prediction`. Health-deferred incidents that recover or become terminal before the next healthy sealing run remain pre-lock coverage outcomes rather than retroactive predictions. For incidents already active when the DDRv2 public prediction contract was enabled, the reviewer floors the pre-lock boundary at the DDRv2 effective timestamp so historical outcomes that predate any possible public prediction do not count as live missed locks. Incidents that crossed eligibility under the applicable public-contract boundary but never received a public prediction become explicit coverage debt such as `missed_lock_recovered`, `missed_lock_terminal`, `publication_failed`, `orphan_closed`, or `data_quality_gap`.

### Compatibility with earlier public predictions

DDRv4 does not rewrite or re-score old public predictions created under DDRv2 sticky-24h or DDRv3 readiness/backstop seals. Existing rows remain valid first-publication exposures with their original policy delay, lock timing, hashes, and review status. API consumers must continue to accept `predictionPolicyVersion = "sticky-24h-v1"`, `policyDelaySec = 86400`, and default/legacy `lockTrigger = "scheduled_24h"` for those rows. DDRR reports policy-version breakdowns so readiness/backstop outcomes and sticky outcomes can be audited side by side without silently mixing their trigger rules.

**Both directions are in scope.** For a below-peg break (underpeg) the kill signals do real work — this is where the terminal-vs-recoverable call matters. For an above-peg break (overpeg) recovery is quasi-certain (a premium mean-reverts as soon as minting/arbitrage works), so Stage 1 is almost always `recovery_likely` (No break signals) and the headline shifts to Stage 2 duration: how long the premium persists. Exceptions: any K5 exit-collapse signal surfaces as `at_risk` (Stress signals) rather than terminal, and a terminal registry status or severe K6 (issuer wind-down announced at or before evaluation time) is `recovery_unlikely` (Terminal evidence) in either direction.

## Stage 1 — Resolution Outlook (terminal vs recoverable)

Stage 1 combines **what the coin *is*** (structural fragility — can supply be weaponized, can backing fail, can it be frozen, has the issuer announced wind-down) with **what the depeg *looks like*** (event fingerprint — depth, speed, supply behavior, live stress signals). The output is an ordinal verdict plus the dominant reasons. It is transparent and inspectable, never a black box.

### Inputs

**Structural fragility** (static registry plus slow scores): `mintAuthority` posture / path plus the published V9 Economic Control mint-posture band (stored in the legacy-named `mintAuthorityScoreBand` field, with curated authority-posture fallback when no compatible V9 publication is installed) and recent reviewed mint-authority incident dates, optional issuer `windDownAnnouncedAt` / `windDownSourceUrl` (token-scoped; distinct from `frozenAt`), `mechanismArchetype`, governance flag, collateral quality, custody model, reserve risk, blacklistability, dependencies, redemption capacity and route family, and the V9 report-card safety grade / exit pillar as anchors when identified.

Since DDR `4.5`, the published V9 mint posture also supplies the structural authority posture, so curated annotations cannot override the current derived judgment. Missing derived posture falls back to the curated review; an explicit `unknown` remains unknown. V9's `none-resolved` is mint-scoped and maps to `none-resolved-mint` unless an independent curated `none-resolved` review establishes whole-chain absence. This preserves wrapper scope for robustness and recovery anchors. Missing compatible installed publication retains the existing curated-band fallback; once a compatible publication is installed, its absent band is authoritative and is not replaced with a curated favorable band. Existing sealed predictions are not rewritten.

The admitted V9 serial-dependency and mint-posture projection is local to one resolver run. Both historical training incidents and active coins use the same explicit structural projection after publication admission; an overlapping run or an unavailable publication cannot replace another run's authority.

Safety Score `10.06` replaces the availability-specific unbounded fallbacks with `unbounded-adverse` in the single shared posture sets, without changing K1/R1 rules. `FRAGILE_MINT_POSTURES` contains `concentrated-admin`, `collateral-gated`, `unbounded-reconciled`, `unbounded-governed`, `unbounded-veto-guarded`, `unbounded-operationally-governed`, `unbounded-adverse`, and `compromised`; `UNBOUNDED_MINT_POSTURES` contains the same values except `concentrated-admin` and `collateral-gated`. All three governance process postures retain K1's unbounded-minter severe-surge treatment and Stage 2's fragile structural class despite their Governed public band. H distinguishes public governance of discretionary/envelope changes from immediate reviewed operation; it does not claim a supply cap. `compromised` describes an active incident; `unbounded-adverse` describes positively known unbounded power without a qualifying governance, reconciliation or supervisory process, not measured non-reconciliation or a new incident. Adverse is Exposed/risky, fragile and unbounded, and satisfies neither no-privileged-mint predicate. Unknown reconciliation creates no recovery anchor; existing whole-chain `none-resolved` strong and user-collateralized/mint-scoped `none-resolved-mint` weak R1 anchors remain unchanged. Shared membership remains owned by `shared/lib/safety-score-v9/mint-posture.ts`; neither DDR's version nor sealed verdicts change for this Safety Score release.


**Event fingerprint** (event row plus live and reconstructed signals): break depth bucket; direction; supply behavior (Δ7d / Δ30d from daily supply history plus mint/burn net flow into the break); live DEWS context; liquidity and exit signals (including V9 exit reason codes and measured primary-route capacity when available); and concurrent blacklist surge. Start-to-peak speed and related orphan closures are not Stage 1 inputs.

**Break depth (v5).** Stage 1 depth, the K6 calm-catastrophic test, the V9 dislocation sizing and the Stage 2 active stratum all use `breakDepthBps()` in `shared/lib/depeg-resolver/resolution.ts`: the worse of `|stored peak deviation|` and `|live current deviation|`. A break that keeps deepening after the stored peak is therefore read at its live depth rather than at a stale peak.

Runtime DDR context hydrates those live fields from `stress_signals.signals_json` (DEWS supply and blacklist sub-signals), `dex_liquidity` plus `dex_liquidity_history` (current liquidity score, 7-day / 30-day TVL trends, independent 30-day volume trends, and — for events younger than 120 days — the TVL change since the last admitted snapshot at or before event onset), the latest admitted circulating supply (USD, unavailable when older than three days), `redemption_backstop_run_rows` (from the latest completed `redemption_backstop_runs` snapshot), mint-burn hourly net inflow where the coin has `MINT_BURN_CONFIGS` coverage, and the published V9 report-card snapshot (safety grade, exit pillar/breakdown, serial dependency graph). A stale, missing, or mismatched V9 safety identity leaves the resolver's safety context explicitly degraded and cannot apply V9 safety/exit anchors; other resolver signals remain available. Every new input null-degrades on read failure rather than silently scoring an absent field.

TVL trends use the shared public DEX trend selector with the stored current row's `methodology_version`, not the runtime version. Historical rows from a different `liquidityTvlBasisEpoch` are skipped under the unchanged confidence, nearest-date and inclusive tolerance policy. Same-epoch minor versions and explicitly legacy epoch-0 pairs remain comparable; an omitted current/history version projection cannot establish comparability. Incompatible TVL evidence stays unavailable rather than creating an exit-collapse signal, without discarding independent current liquidity scores or 30-day volume trends. This input-admission correction can move new assessments and forecasts; sealed predictions, first-publication payloads and the duration corpus are not rewritten.

K1's `mintSurgeCoverage` vocabulary distinguishes `mint-burn-hourly`, `supply-history-proxy`, and `unavailable`. The hourly label requires at least one finite `mint_burn_hourly` observation inside the event window; an empty or non-finite row set is unavailable, not an observed negative mint-surge verdict. The supply-history proxy is used only when the coin has no configured mint/burn coverage and a finite 7-day change exists.

Mint/burn v6.31 applies the same admitted, enabled config scope to DDR's hourly rows and event-window scan proof. Pending-admission and operator-disabled legs contribute neither totals nor proof obligations. A configured coin with no admitted legs remains unavailable and does not fall back to a supply-history proxy.

Supply anchors are the latest usable observation at or before their respective event-time targets, never a later snapshot. Required supply coverage needs at least two observations, positive onset supply and a finite measured onset-versus-7-day-baseline change; a missing onset or 7-day baseline leaves coverage unavailable even with multiple post-onset rows. Missing 30-day/current anchors leave that change null. Hourly mint-surge ratios also require a positive measured onset denominator.

Every per-coin read in that hydration path (`supply_history`, `mint_burn_hourly`, `dex_liquidity`, `dex_liquidity_history`, and the promoted `depeg_pending_outcomes` confirmation times) is issued in bind-limit-safe chunks through `chunkArray()` + `buildInClause()`, and the chunk results are merged in JS. A market-wide depeg is exactly when the active-coin list exceeds `D1_MAX_BOUND_PARAMETERS`, so an unchunked `IN (…)` would fail every one of those reads and degrade the run at the moment the resolver must publish.

### Kill signals (terminal pressure)

The six kill signals below are rated `elevated` or `severe`; a signal that does not fire is absent from the factor list.

| # | Kill signal | Fires when |
|---|---|---|
| **K1** | Supply weaponization | Below-peg only. `riskyMinter` (concentrated/unbounded authority posture, risky mint path, **or** mint-authority score band ∈ {`concentrated`,`exposed`} per `DDR_K1_MAS_RISKY_BAND_MAPPING`) **and** abnormal supply/mint expansion into the depeg (`mintSurge` from event-time `mint_burn_hourly` net inflow when covered, else the explicit `change7dPct > 20` proxy with coverage marker) **or** an unresolved/unremediated mint-authority incident in the asymmetric window [start −60d, start +7d]. Severe when unbounded minter + mint surge, or a recent mint incident with a deep break. D33's `unbounded-adverse` remains risky/unbounded; unknown reconciliation alone is neither a mint surge nor an incident |
| **K2** | Backing impairment | **Severe:** `dependencyImpaired` (V9 serial dependency with frozen/dead upstream when the V9 card is available; otherwise the legacy registry dependency weight ≥ 0.3 frozen fallback), **or** — for every archetype except `cdp` and `synthetic-delta-neutral` — very-high-risk reserves ≥ 30% paired with a severe/catastrophic below-peg fingerprint. **Elevated:** for those same non-exempt archetypes (including `algorithmic` and coins with no reviewed archetype), exotic collateral or high/very-high reserve concentration. `cdp` and `synthetic-delta-neutral` are exempt from both static reserve legs, so static concentration or exotic collateral alone raises no K2 factor there; K2 requires observed impairment (`dependencyImpaired` or the measured impairment path above) |
| **K3** | Freeze / seizure | **Severe:** a concurrent blacklist surge on a freezable (or inherited-freezable) token, or sanctioned custody (`institutional-sanctioned`). **Elevated:** `possible` blacklistability during a surge, or CEX-custodied reserves (fires on custody alone) — recovery administratively blocked |
| **K4** | Reflexive death-spiral | Algorithmic **and** supply expanding into a below-peg break — the UST / IRON price-down-while-supply-chases signature (severe when the break is deep). Separately, `synthetic-delta-neutral` **and** a deep below-peg break with supply Δ7d < −10% (hedge under redemption/unwind pressure) grades elevated |
| **K5** | Exit collapse | **Severe (dynamic only, v5):** liquidity score < 20 **and** DEX TVL down ≥ 40% from the last admitted snapshot at or before event onset (onset-referenced; looked up only for events younger than 120 days). **Elevated (static evidence caps here):** a sustained one-sided outflow (bank-run) signal, V9 `no-viable-exit-path`, V9 `correlated-exit-routes`, thin liquidity (score < 30), thin redemption capacity (`rcr < 0.02`), or V9 measured capacity re-scaled to the dislocation — thin when the primary route's executable USD within the V9 cost bound is below `circulating supply × break depth`, unless the route completed the full V9 stress request. Missing supply, capacity or onset TVL null-degrades rather than fires |
| **K6** | Issuer wind-down | **Severe:** curated `windDownAnnouncedAt ≤` evaluation/lock time on **this coin's own issuer/token id** (never a venue or dependency — venue wind-downs must not fire). Reasons name the announcement date. **Elevated (fingerprint only when severe does not fire):** (a) supply Δ30d ≤ −40% **and** an exit-trend collapse (TVL or volume Δ ≤ −40%), or (b) calm-catastrophic: depth ≥ 1000 bps, \|supply Δ30d\| < 5%, and 24h DEX volume ≤ $100. Missing supply Δ30d or trend/volume inputs null-degrade rather than fire |

**Why K5 changed in v5.** V9 sizes its exit stress request for the Safety Score, with a $1M materiality floor. For small-float coins (for example ZARP, AUDF, VNXAU) that request is far larger than the arbitrage needed to repeg, so the v4 completion-ratio thresholds read their exit as "effectively exhausted" in calm and stressed periods alike; five of v4's seven false-terminal calls came from that path. v5 measures the route against the dislocation's value gap instead, and treats every static exit weakness (thin route, no viable route, correlated routes, thin liquidity, thin redemption capacity) as elevated stress. Only an in-incident collapse measured from the onset baseline is severe. The legacy 7-day TVL trend and the `rcr < 0.005` severe leg no longer fire K5 severe.

### Recovery anchors

Each anchor is rated `weak` or `strong`. Since v5, anchors are published context: they no longer gate a tier, so the absence of anchors does not keep a coin out of No break signals. Strong R1/R2 remains the one structural exception in the compound-stress leg of the tier rule below.

| # | Recovery anchor | Strong when |
|---|---|---|
| **R1** | Non-inflatable supply | Whole-of-chain `none-resolved` authority / immutable user-collateralized mint path — supply cannot be printed (LUSD / BOLD). Two weak rungs sit below it: `user-collateralized-governed` mint path, and (DDR 4.2) mint-scoped `none-resolved-mint`, which states the token has no minter of its own while its wrapped parent still does. Weak rungs are published attribution and never satisfy the strong-anchor leg of the tier rule |
| **R2** | Hard collateral + live redemption | Native or very-low-risk collateral, **and** a live redemption path: either `rcr ≥ 0.1` with a known route family **or** measured V9 executable exit above threshold (`completionRatio ≥ 0.1`, `executableUsd ≥ $100k`, functioning `primaryRoute`) when the V9 safety context is `v9-identified` |
| **R3** | No supply anomaly | Requires usable event-window supply coverage, affirmative `mintSurge === false` and finite measured Δ7d. Strong: \|Δ7d\| < 10%. Weak: \|Δ7d\| < 20%. Unknown surge or missing change never awards an anchor. DEWS/flow sub-signals are not tested |
| **R4** | No single freeze point | Decentralized governance, on-chain custody, not blacklistable — nobody can block recovery |
| **R5** | Proven mean-reversion | V9 safety **grade** mapping (`DDR_R5_GRADE_MAPPING` / `r5-grade-v1`) only when the resolver safety context is `v9-identified`: strong = grade ∈ {A+, A, A-}; weak = additionally {B+, B, B-}. Numeric score cutoffs are not used |

### Verdict mapping

The ordinal tiers are `recovery_likely` (No break signals) › `at_risk` (Stress signals) › `recovery_unlikely` (Terminal evidence), plus the `insufficient_signal` escape hatch. They are evidence classes, not recovery probabilities. In either direction, a terminal status or a severe issuer wind-down signal is Terminal evidence. Otherwise, above peg, an exit-collapse signal reads as Stress signals and everything else as No break signals, even a severe kill signal. Below peg, any severe kill signal, or two or more kill signals without a strong non-inflatable-supply or hard-collateral/redemption anchor on a break deeper than 1,000 bps (the worse of the stored peak and live deviation), is Terminal evidence. Any other below-peg kill signal, or a deep break with none, reads as Stress signals. An above-direction event whose live signed deviation is itself a deep below-peg break is evaluated by the below-peg mapping; a stale stored direction cannot publish No break signals for a live deep break. No break signals never means recovery is assured.

Live DDR deviation uses `deriveCurrentPegObservationMap`, the same observed-price and authoritative-reference admission as peg analytics and peg-summary. Nominal references, `protocol-par`, and any composite containing a nominal component yield null deviation rather than observed zero. DDR additionally requires explicitly available positive current supply; missing or zero supply cannot satisfy its live-input gate.

```
if key inputs missing (no MA review, no usable supply history, or no live price), AND coin not frozen  → insufficient_signal
else if coin is frozen/terminal (any direction), OR severe K6 wind-down (any direction)              → recovery_unlikely
else if direction is above-peg:
    if K5 exit collapse fires (any severity)                                                       → at_risk
    else                                                                                            → recovery_likely
else (below-peg; depth = breakDepthBps, "deep" = severe/catastrophic bucket, > 1,000 bps):
    if any K severe, OR (>= 2 K at any severity AND no strong R1/R2 AND deep)                       → recovery_unlikely
    else if any K fires, OR deep                                                                    → at_risk
    else                                                                                            → recovery_likely
```

Reasons surface the top fired kill signals (with severity) and the strongest anchors, for example: *"Terminal evidence — issuer announced wind-down on 2026-05-24 (K6 severe); thin exit liquidity (K5 elevated)."*

Two v5 consequences follow directly. Static weaknesses condemn a coin only alongside a severe below-peg fingerprint: two elevated kills on a shallow break are Stress signals, not Terminal evidence. And a deep below-peg break with no kill signal is Stress signals, never No break signals.

K6-elevated alone does not force `recovery_unlikely`; it is honest hedge evidence and the operational prompt for wind-down curation (see [runbooks/depeg-lifecycle-review.md](./runbooks/depeg-lifecycle-review.md)). A scheduled ops follow-up issue also opens when a catastrophic below-peg incident is active, so issuer wind-down and lifecycle status can be curated before DDR locks.

### Why Stage 1 is calibrated, not fitted

Stage 1 is a **mechanistic rubric whose thresholds are calibrated by backtest, not learned weights.** This is a deliberate response to the data we actually have.

Terminal labels are small, editorial and not event-linked: roughly 88 curated dead stablecoins (month-level dates/narratives), a handful of frozen tracked coins (day-level dates), and the PSI historical assets UST/IRON with backfilled events. Their use in terminal validation does not enroll them in public forecasting. First-seen off-catalog membership excludes both from the DDR policy universe, lock and publication paths. The runtime name `psiOffCatalogAtFirstSeen` preserves the existing broad off-catalog classification; immutable D1 `psi_shadow_at_first_seen` / `psi_shadow_excluded` names remain historical storage facts, not a shadow feature. Hundreds of timed recoveries do not make ~90 mostly month-precision deaths a fitted supervised classifier; transparent mechanistic priors are calibrated against both corpora.

Rubric thresholds are tuned against the label corpus and validated with DDRR reviewed outcomes, never fitted as ML weights. Publish backtest summaries as methodology facts, not per-event probabilities; disclose the small sample. Bounded calibration changes and rationale live in `shared/data/methodology-changelogs/depeg-resolver/`.

## Stage 2 — Expected Duration (unconditional recovery odds)

Stage 2 is exposed only when Stage 1 is `recovery_likely` or `at_risk`; the shared engine computes it before suppressing terminal/insufficient results. For `recovery_unlikely`, the row card states that DDR does not expect the depeg to recover and that comparable structural failures did not return to peg, with a link to the cemetery. For `insufficient_signal`, duration is suppressed until enough live signal exists.

### Method

Stage 2 is an empirical **landmark cumulative-incidence** estimate of recovery (`duration-landmark-v3`, `duration.estimand = "cumulative_incidence"`). Rows sealed before `v5.0` have no `estimand` field; their odds and bands were conditional on recovery (`conditional_recovery`) and must be read that way.

Each comparable incident contributes one of three outcomes, measured from its own onset (`incidentOutcome` in `shared/lib/depeg-resolver/duration.ts`):

- **Recovery** — a recovery-classified closure, at its duration.
- **Terminal** (competing outcome) — the coin is in a terminal registry status with a day-precision `frozenAt`, and the incident did not recover and was still open or closed within 30 days before the freeze; the event time is `frozenAt − startedAt`. Unknown or unparseable freeze dates leave the incident censored rather than terminal at a guessed time.
- **Censored** — a non-recovery closure at its duration, or a still-open audit-eligible event at the evaluation clock. Open events come from a separate bounded read (`readDdrOpenTrainingHistory`, `OPEN_TRAINING_ROW_CAP = 500`) and use the live severity-observation aggregate because they have no rollup row. The active incident's own open history (same coin and direction) is excluded: it is what is being forecast.

1. **Incident grouping + quarantine.** Live/canonical grouping merges same-coin/same-direction fragments reopening within `MERGE_GAP_SEC` (6h) of the prior union endpoint. **Training labels for Stage 2 use a separate stickiness constant** `DURATION_LABEL_MERGE_GAP_SEC = 24h` (`shared/lib/depeg-resolver/incident-groups.ts`) so corpus durations match sticky canonical-incident grading. Both passes take interval unions: any open member keeps the group open; otherwise the latest end wins, never a nested shorter end. The member establishing that terminal boundary owns recovery classification; equal-end conflicts require every tied boundary member to be recovery-classified. Recovery requires an explicit recovery `close_reason`, or a legacy null reason plus non-null `recovery_price`; explicit non-recovery closures are never recoveries even when a price is stored (since v5 they enter as censored observations). Historical rows are read through `depeg_events_with_provenance`, applying the canonical `DDR_INELIGIBLE_AUDIT_VERDICTS` policy **before** the historical row cap: `false_positive`, `disputed`, `no_data`, and unknown non-null verdicts are excluded; confirmed, repaired, and null-verdict legacy rows remain eligible. Quality also comes from the minimum-severity/duration floor and quarantine of coins with more than 200 incidents and median duration under ~2h. Ordinary high-frequency coins remain eligible because coin weighting already bounds their influence.

2. **Stratification, most-dependable-first.** A dependable wide band beats a precise band built on three incidents. The MVP strata, in order of how readily they are dropped:
   - **direction** (`above` vs `below`) — overpeg and underpeg resolve on different clocks and are never pooled.
   - **depth** — `minor` (≤250 bps) / `moderate` (250–1000) / `severe` (1000–2500) / `catastrophic` (>2500), validated by the monotonic depth→duration relationship in the corpus. Support-gated collapse can pool catastrophic with severe, then with the non-minor severe/moderate bucket when a bucket is thin; severe/catastrophic live events do not borrow the minor-flap clock.
   - **structural class** — a coarse two-way split (`robust`: a real-collateral archetype — `cdp`, `fiat-cash`, `tbill`, `rwa-credit-fund`, `commodity-claim` — or an immutable user-collateralized / whole-of-chain non-inflatable mint path, provided the mint posture is not fragile and collateral is not exotic; `fragile`: everything else, including algorithmic, fragile mint postures, exotic collateral, and unknown archetypes) from the same Stage-1 structural inputs. Posture set membership comes from the shared predicates in `shared/lib/safety-score-v9/mint-posture.ts` (`isFragileMintPosture` for the adverse leg, `isNoPrivilegedMintChainPosture` for the robustness leg), so the vocabulary is classified once for every engine. The robustness leg is deliberately whole-of-chain: mint-scoped `none-resolved-mint` does not by itself make a wrapper robust, because its parent can still print.
   - **peg currency** (USD vs non-USD) — applied as a refinement. **Currency guard:** USD subjects drop currency before structure; non-USD subjects preserve currency while dropping structure first so their clock cannot borrow USD history merely to retain a structural class (`candidateStrata` in `shared/lib/depeg-resolver/strata.ts`).

   The fallback order follows that candidate list through exact full stratum → currency/structure refinement → depth pooling → fully open structural/currency wildcards, reporting the actual stratum used. Finer splits (full 4-bucket depth at all times, 3-way structural class) are deferred until real-data counts justify the support.

3. **Landmark estimate at age `t`.** The risk set is the stratum's incidents still unresolved and under observation at age `t` (terminal or recovery at or after `t`, censoring after `t`). A **coin-weighted Aalen–Johansen** estimator computes recovery cumulative incidence over remaining time with terminal as a competing risk: each coin's comparable incidents share one unit of weight, fixed at the landmark. Historical depth is evaluated as observed by landmark age `t`, not from a closed incident's final peak. The secondary readout is per-horizon (6h / 24h / 7d / 30d) recovery odds read off that curve. The headline **median and typical range [p15, p85]** are the remaining times at which the incidence curve reaches 0.50 / 0.15 / 0.85. A quantile the curve never reaches stays `null` ("may not resolve"), and the typical range is published only when both ends are reached, so a stratum where most comparables stay open or end terminal shows no band rather than one drawn from survivors. `duration.unresolvedShare30d` publishes `1 − incidence at 30d`: the estimated share of comparables not recovered within 30 days (terminal or still open). API field name `iqrSec` remains for backward compatibility; `_meta.durationBand` labels it `typical_range` with percentiles 15/85 (`DDR_DURATION_BAND_META` in `shared/types/depeg-resolver.ts`). Age status (`ordinary` / `chronic_tail` beyond P99) still compares the open event with completed recoveries in the matched stratum.

   Depth evidence carries its observation clock through both grouping windows. A unique matching promoted pending outcome supplies `first_seen_bps` at onset and `last_seen_bps` at `last_seen_at`; untimed `peak_seen_bps` is never admitted. Matching diagnostic assessments add sparse `currentDeviationBps` samples at `assessed_at`, the time the resolver knew that observation, not an invented upstream price clock. Their aggregate `peakDeviationBps` is also excluded. This is sparse retained evidence, not a reconstructed full price tape. Legacy rows without timed evidence cannot select any depth stratum until genuine evidence is available at that landmark. Reduced support suppresses bands rather than fabricating comparability. Corpus corrections affect future estimates only; sealed predictions and first-publication payloads are not rewritten.

4. **Support gates + intervals.** The estimator emits `benchmarked`, `thin_support`, `no_comparable_closures`, `chronic_tail`, `not_informative`, or `unsupported` cells; an empty matched stratum has `ageStatus = "data_issue"`. Horizon support gates (`support-rules-v3`) count only comparables whose horizon outcome is observable — an event before `t + h`, or follow-up past `t + h` — so a recently opened incident cannot pad a long horizon. Effective closures are the incidence at the horizon times the observable coin count; Wilson intervals use those **unrounded** weights with 5pp display rounding. `rawAtRisk` reports the whole landmark risk set, while `intervalClosures` / `intervalNonClosures` count the observable incidents. A supported cell whose horizon is listed in `DDR_NOT_INFORMATIVE_HORIZONS` (methodology-owned in `duration.ts`) is published as `not_informative` with no percentage: that horizon failed the replay skill gate against the time-ordered pooled base rate. The set is currently empty because the v5 replay found every horizon beat that base rate. Leave-one-coin sensitivity is a validation-plan check, not a runtime gate. Failed gates show support states, never fabricated 0% / 100%. Age beyond stratum P99 is "unusually prolonged."

No leakage: a closed event's final peak severity is never used to estimate a live event's duration. Explicitly rejected for duration calibration (tested, worsen calibration): backfill-row exclusion and minimum-age gating.

### Training corpus storage

`worker/src/cron/depeg-resolver/training-history.ts` owns the bounded history read; `TRAINING_WINDOW_SEC` and `HISTORICAL_ROW_CAP` in `constants.ts` remain the window/cap authorities. Migration `0269_ddr_training_corpus_rollup.sql` adds `depeg_resolver_training_corpus`, keyed by event id, containing the exact SQLite severity-observation aggregate plus the event identity and start/end boundaries. Empty observations remain `[]`; invalid/missing/nonnumeric deviations keep the live aggregate's bytes and are rejected as numeric evidence by the existing mapper.

SQLite triggers refresh the aggregate in the event/assessment writer's own transaction, including close, reopen, late assessment insert, diagnostic update/delete, and authorized identity repair. Existing assessment persistence and event writers therefore require no second write convention, and old Workers also maintain the rollup. Pending-onset evidence and audit eligibility remain live reads, not cached verdicts.

The loader admits rollup JSON only when every selected historical row has a matching identity and interval in one SQLite snapshot. It never returns a partial training corpus. If any row is missing or mismatched, that invocation uses the complete original live aggregate query and repairs at most `TRAINING_ROLLUP_BACKFILL_EVENT_CAP` event ids already identified by the bounded read. Failed rollup reads/backfills also fall back to the live query; a failed live read still fails the load. Backfill runs inside the existing resolver lane, without a new trigger or migration-time history scan. Once complete, history reads perform event-key lookups instead of scanning assessments per event; no methodology or frozen prediction changes.

### Output

- `Expected to resolve within ~6–24h` (median plus typical-range band), labeled with the stratum and its support state; an unreached median or band end is shown as unavailable, not extrapolated.
- Horizon cells with recovery-odds display where gates pass, otherwise the support state (including `not_informative`).
- `unresolvedShare30d`, the estimated share of comparables not recovered within 30 days.
- A `chronic_tail` indicator when the open event is already past the stratum's P99.

## DDRR — Depeg Duration Resolver Reviewer

The Depeg Duration Resolver Reviewer (DDRR) is the audit companion to DDR. DDR answers an open-event question; DDRR asks whether a stored DDR answer later matched canonical Pharos event data. Reviewer version: `ddr-reviewer-v5`.

DDRR does **not** replay today's resolver over old events. DDR scores only frozen outcomes that entered the first-publication manifest through `checkpoint = 'public_prediction'`. Diagnostic checkpoints (`first`, `age_1h`, `age_6h`, `age_24h`, `age_7d`, and `latest`) remain useful for audit, but they are not headline public predictions. The review layer compares the frozen first-published outcome with the later canonical incident state and tracked-coin lifecycle status.

The public module on `/depeg/` sits directly below DDR and separates coverage/accountability from accuracy. It surfaces prediction coverage, scoreable coverage, publication success, no-call share, invalidation rate, pre-lock recovery/terminal outcomes, and missed-lock/deferred states before treating any row as an accuracy sample. An accuracy-by-version strip consolidates the summary's per-`(methodology, policy)` segments into methodology majors (recovery-call accuracy plus scored-count-weighted mean duration miss per major); the current major renders as maturing until its first frozen rows score.

Coverage and accuracy have independent universes: crossing the 20-scored-outcome gate switches only accuracy, duration error, and horizon calibration to the current prediction policy. Headline accountability counts and rates retain all relevant policy-universe incidents. The coverage ledger, including scoreable coverage, uses the authoritative `segmentKind: "all"` metrics, so missed locks, publication failure/retry debt, pending locks, older predictions, and no-calls remain visible when current-policy accuracy matures.

The calibration ledger reads `falseTerminalCount`, `falseRecoverableCount`, `withinIqrCount`, and `iqrScoredCount` directly from `summary.headline`, with the same policy scope as accuracy and duration metrics. These full-universe counts never come from the capped browsing rows; older cached summaries without them render unavailable.

`pendingRecoveryLikelihoodCount` counts only `prediction_review` rows whose verdict is `pending`, in that same headline accuracy scope (all official predictions until the current-policy gate is met). It supplies the calibration bar's maturing forecasts; `pendingLockCount` stays in coverage accounting because an unpublished lock is not a forecast. New producers always emit the count, while retained cached bodies may omit it and render maturing unavailable, never zero. Publish the additive producer/schema, refresh its review snapshot/cache and confirm the live field before deploying the Pages reader. This is reporting only, not a prediction-policy or DDR methodology change.

- **Recovery likelihood** — strict accuracy for scored DDR recovery verdicts. Correct recoverable and correct terminal calls count in the numerator; false terminal, false recoverable, and `at_risk` terminal outcomes are scored in the denominator. Pending, insufficient-signal, data-issue and `terminal_at_lock` rows are excluded.
- **Recovery duration** — signed observed-minus-DDR duration error for recovered rows with a duration estimate. Positive means the observed recovery took longer than DDR's median remaining-time estimate; negative means it recovered faster. Mean signed and absolute seconds are dominated by a few long incidents, so reviewer v5 adds scale-free context: `medianLog2DurationRatio` (median `log2(actual / predicted)`, both floored at 600 s; positive means slower) and `within2xCount` (rows within a factor of two).
- **Horizon calibration** — expected-vs-observed cells alongside hit rates: for each horizon, mean predicted probability over scored duration rows, realized closure share, bias in percentage points (mean predicted − realized), and a Poisson-binomial z-score under the normal approximation `z = (observed − Σp) / sqrt(Σp(1−p))`. Surfaced in the DDRR snapshot summary as `horizonCalibration` and in `npm run calibrate:ddrr`.
- **Baselines and skill (reviewer v5)** — additive, optional summary fields; snapshots that predate them render unavailable, never zero. `baselineRecoverableCorrectCount` is what a constant "will recover" forecaster would score on the same scored rows. `terminalCallCount` / `terminalCallCorrectCount` / `terminalOutcomeCount` separate terminal-call precision from how many terminal outcomes exist at all. `tierOutcomes` counts recovered and terminal outcomes per frozen tier. `horizonSkill` gives, per horizon, the Brier score of matured odds (hits and misses, including non-curation terminal and still-open misses; `terminal_at_lock` rows stay out) against a leave-one-out base-rate Brier and their skill `1 − brier / baseRateBrier`; fewer than two observations publish nulls. `terminalAtLockCount` and `overdueOpenCount` are listed below.
- **Coverage accounting (`v4.4`)** — the two completeness shares, `stateAssignedPct` and `finalizedCoveragePct`, count each incident once: rows are grouped by `incidentKey`, each incident contributes exactly one unit however many review rows it carries, and that unit is assigned by the row's single `predictionState` — there is no per-row, depeg-second, or duration weighting anywhere in the shares. An `operationalCoverageCause` is an overlay on that state, never a second assignment, so a data-quality gap or orphan closure attributed to a cron gap, system deferral or missed lock is one share rather than two. Both shares are therefore at most one by construction, not by clamping; they read below one whenever policy-universe incidents have no rows or disagree. An incident whose rows disagree about its state counts as unassigned so the shortfall stays visible. Per-state counts, `missedNoPredictionCount` and `operationalMissRatePct` deliberately keep their own denominators and may overlap the state buckets — they are cause filters, not assignments.

Review outcomes are deliberately conservative:

- A still-open event remains `pending` unless tracked lifecycle status supplies terminal evidence; open status alone is never counted as proof that a terminal call was right. Its duration, however, can already be graded (reviewer v5): once elapsed time since lock exceeds the published upper band, the row is `slower_than_band` with `withinIqr = false` and counts in `overdueOpenCount`, whenever the incident eventually closes.
- Terminal evidence whose whole interval ends at or before the lock is `terminal_at_lock` (reviewer v5): the outcome was already true when DDR published, so the miss is registry curation lag, not foresight. It is never scored as a forecast and is counted as curation debt in `terminalAtLockCount`.
- A closed event with an explicit recovery reason is `recovered`, including native-fiat recoveries whose quote-domain-safe `recovery_price` is null; legacy null-reason rows with a recovery price remain compatible. Terminal evidence already settled by the closure overrides either recovery label with `terminal`.
- Coverage states and prediction reviews share the same episode chronology. A later freeze cannot relabel an earlier recovery terminal; day/month evidence must have its entire interval settled by the recovery end before it overrides recovery. The same classification controls both pre-lock/missed-lock state and actual outcome, while rollout-active eligibility retains its DDR enablement floor.
- Explicit direction-superseded, coverage-lost, orphan, or unknown closures are never recovered merely because a recovery price is present.
- A terminal/frozen tracked asset without a recovery-classified closure is `terminal` (shown in the UI as "terminal observed"), even if the underlying depeg row remains open; that lifecycle evidence matures the review as a terminal outcome rather than a pending duration case.
- Missing source rows or closed rows without recovery/terminal evidence are data issues, not wins or losses.

Every current generation-4 public review row carries its actual outcome natively: prediction and no-call rows via `actual.kind`, coverage rows via `actualOutcome` alongside `sourceEventState` (the same evidence in the public outcome vocabulary), and invalidated rows via their literal `invalidated` state. Consumers on the current payload therefore never re-derive an outcome from the source-event state.
During the 2026-09-06 Worker/Pages cutover, consumers tolerate generation-3 coverage rows that omit `actualOutcome` by deriving it from `sourceEventState`; generation-4 producer payloads continue to require and publish the field.

Cards read row-level `originalKind` to distinguish invalidated predictions from no-calls. `publication_retry_pending` means delayed publication, not a promised retry time; the API publishes no next-retry timestamp.

`GET /api/depeg-resolver-review` serves one snapshot bound to `_meta.computedAt`: headline/policy metrics and `summary.byStablecoin` counts plus median duration errors are computed before the 400-row public browse cap. Detail track records read those coin summaries even when every coin row is outside the sample; retained snapshots without them withhold aggregates. `rows` are display-only, with `_meta.publicRowLimit` / `publicRowsTruncated`; `incidentRowsTruncated` separately qualifies the producer cohort. Assessment-cap fields remain compatibility metadata (never truncated in v2; empty degraded responses zero limits). Missing/invalid snapshots return degraded `200` with empty rows; stale snapshots retain their generation and rows with `degradedReason="stale-cache"`.

`summary.byStablecoin` is optional in both the runtime and OpenAPI contracts. Missing or rejected snapshots (including a methodology-version mismatch during deployment) omit it and name the failure in `_meta.degradedReason`; an empty array is reserved for a successfully reviewed empty cohort. Dossier track records show unavailable with the snapshot reason and retry control, not “no records,” when this aggregate is absent.

Reviewer v5 changes how outcomes are classified, not what was predicted: v2–v4 sealed rows keep their bytes, hashes and methodology, but a row whose terminal evidence predates its lock moves to `terminal_at_lock`, and an overdue still-open row is graded `slower_than_band`, so headline counts for older majors can shift when the snapshot first rebuilds under v5.

For calibration passes, run `npm run calibrate:ddrr` to generate the advisory DDRR calibration report described in [process/ddrr-calibration.md](./process/ddrr-calibration.md). The report groups factor attribution, no-call/coverage debt, duration signed error, horizon expected-vs-observed calibration, K5 exit-collapse evidence, reserve/dependency nuance, and mint-incident timing without replaying current DDR logic over historical rows.

## Honest Limitations & Failure Modes

- **Stage 1 is calibrated, not learned.** There are roughly 90 terminal labels, mostly month-precision and not event-linked. Verdicts are domain-prior judgments validated on a small set plus DDRR reviewed forecast outcomes. We state this plainly; forecast readiness is a publication trigger, not a stronger/weaker verdict label.
- **K6 depends on curation.** Severe K6 only fires when `windDownAnnouncedAt` is curated on the coin entry with a date ≤ lock time. K6-elevated fingerprints and the scheduled catastrophic-break ops issue are the operational prompts to research and backfill that field (≈3-day SLA) — not a substitute for the announcement. A death curated only after lock surfaces in DDRR as `terminal_at_lock` curation debt.
- **Supply resolution is coarse.** Supply history is daily, so it can miss intra-day spikes; mint/burn coverage is limited to the contracts and issuance chains in `MINT_BURN_CONFIGS`. A coin with neither usable source degrades to `insufficient_signal` on the supply-dependent kill signals rather than guessing. K1's mint-surge path marks coverage explicitly when falling back to the Δ7d proxy.
- **Legacy null provenance remains eligible.** A null audit verdict is unaudited legacy evidence, not an unknown verdict. DDR includes it and relies on incident grouping, quarantine, and the severity floor for quality. Unknown non-null verdicts are ineligible; `no_data` is also ineligible for DDR even though it remains usable for PegScore.
- **Terminal ≠ event-recovery.** A backfilled dead coin (for example IRON) can have replay closures on transient in-band prints. Stage 1 uses terminal `status`, issuer wind-down evidence, and the K1–K6 rubric, never historical `recovery_price` or open age alone.
- **Survivorship / selection.** The event corpus spans only the tracking window plus backfill (roughly 2026 onward plus replays). Pre-tracking deaths are not event-linked, so the corpus skews toward the modern, surviving set. v5 counts terminal incidents as a competing outcome, but only when the registry records a terminal status with a `frozenAt` date; an uncurated death is censored, not terminal, so terminal incidence is understated until curation catches up.
- **Residual overconfidence.** The v5 replay still shows predicted recovery odds above observed recovery at longer horizons (for example, at the 1h landmark: 7d mean predicted 0.79 vs observed 0.66; 30d 0.90 vs 0.79), and p15–p85 coverage below the nominal 0.70. Read the odds as an upper-leaning estimate, not a calibrated probability.
- **Terminal skill is unmeasurable.** Only three forward-looking terminal outcomes exist in the scored record, none since July 2026. Terminal-evidence precision and recall cannot be estimated with useful confidence; DDRR publishes the counts rather than a rate.
- **Abandoned slow-deaths are out of scope.** A coin that fades without a sharp depeg never triggers DDR. This is documented, not hidden.
- **Backfill granularity.** About 95% of corpus rows are replayed at daily/hourly granularity, so very-short backfilled durations can be sampling artifacts. This is acceptable for distributions and is flagged here.
- **Mechanism blind spots.** A novel mechanism with no archetype or mint-authority review degrades to `insufficient_signal` rather than guessing.
- **Reflexivity at the edges.** A "No break signals" read could itself be taken as a signal; the copy stays descriptive, never directive. It states that no break evidence fired, not that recovery is assured.

## Validation / Backtest Plan (acceptance gates)

DDR is validated by a replay harness over historical events plus the label corpus. The backtest numbers are recorded here and re-run whenever the rubric or grouping version changes.

1. **Stage 1 recall on clear deaths.** For clearly-attributable deaths (UST, IRON, USR, plus datable cemetery cases), require `recovery_unlikely` at the death-precursor depeg; state the recall target and exclude `abandoned` cases. `usr-resolv` is frozen and therefore excluded from new live predictions, not a live acceptance case. Locked-verdict fixtures in `shared/lib/depeg-resolver/__tests__/` gate rubric scenarios against stored verdicts.
2. **Stage 1 specificity on recoveries.** For major recoveries (USDC during SVB, DAI on Black Thursday, LUSD / BOLD / crvUSD wobbles) the rubric must **not** say `recovery_unlikely`. The gate is zero false-terminal on the curated recovery set. Robust CDP and fiat coins have no deep below-peg events in the corpus; USDC-SVB-class severe recoveries must not be misread as terminal.
3. **Stage 2 accuracy.** For incidents with traceable timestamped severity (recovered, terminal, or still open and censored), replay the predicted median / typical-range band and horizon odds at age `t` point-in-time, and report per-horizon Brier against the prior model and a time-ordered base rate, calibration and held-out band coverage. The 07-29 golden archive has no observation-time provenance for its final-peak fragment pairs; it is preserved as unsupported legacy evidence, not relabelled as a current calibration gate. Its regression enforces suppression without timed severity. A methodology recalibration requires a genuinely timestamp-evidenced corpus; frozen DDRR outcomes remain separate advisory evidence.
4. **Leakage & stability.** Leave-one-coin sensitivity on Stage 2; the canonical lineage hash stays stable under row-order changes.
5. **Degradation.** A missing or stale cache returns a degraded `200`; suppressed cells always state a reason.

### DDRv5 replay evidence (advisory)

Recorded for the `v5.0` release. The samples are small; treat these as advisory evidence for a structural correction, not as a fitted accuracy claim.

- **Stage 1 mapping replay** over sealed rows with recorded factors. Rows sealed before v5 have no raw `inputs`, so the replay re-maps their recorded factors and depth under the v5 rules; the expected per-row tier changes are pinned in `shared/lib/depeg-resolver/__tests__/locked-verdict-replay.test.ts`. On 74 forecast-scored outcomes (3 `terminal_at_lock` rows excluded): v5 correct on 71/74, v4 on 61/74, and a constant "will recover" baseline on 70/74. False terminals fall from 11 to 1 (AxCNH); every forward-looking correct terminal call is kept. Outcomes by v5 tier: No break signals 39 recovered / 0 terminal; Stress signals 30 / 2; Terminal evidence 1 / 2. v5 beats the constant baseline by one call, which is the honest size of the margin.
- **Stage 2 time-ordered point-in-time replay**, landmarks 1h / 6h / 24h / 72h over incidents from 2026-05-01 (n = 121 / 99 / 65 / 46), each estimate using only incidents observable at that time. v5 Brier is below v4 in every landmark × horizon cell, and v5 beats the time-ordered pooled (direction-only) base rate when aggregated per horizon, so `DDR_NOT_INFORMATIVE_HORIZONS` is empty. p15–p85 coverage is 0.55 (v4 0.38) at the 1h landmark and 0.41 (v4 0.33) at 6h against a nominal 0.70; many upper quantiles are unreached and therefore unpublished. Residual overconfidence is disclosed under [Honest Limitations](#honest-limitations--failure-modes).
- **Terminal skill** cannot be measured: three forward-looking terminal outcomes, none since July 2026.

## Data Plumbing

Both stages are precomputed by the D1-only `compute-depeg-resolver` job in the fenced `depegResolverOffset` lane at `:13/:28/:43/:58`. It keeps the quarter-hour cadence and the core-slot/publication plus V9 memory-lane admission fence, but runs in a separate scheduled invocation rather than retaining the V9 supply-attribution run-scoped graph in that invocation. The Worker service may reuse a warm isolate, so operational acceptance still requires observed clean executions. V9 publication admission is independent of DDR's scheduled outcome; see [the worker topology invariant](process/worker-infrastructure-appendix.md#deployed-trigger-topology). It reads the latest `sync-stablecoins` capability metadata, caches results in D1, and serves them by cache-backed endpoints. DDR admits that input for the producer's 900-second cadence; the public stablecoins endpoint retains its deliberately stricter 600-second warning budget. The writer reaps recovered pre-lock incidents, resolves canonical incidents (including flap-tolerant adoption and regime-escalation splits), evaluates forecast readiness, records health deferrals, seals immutable predictions/no-calls at readiness or the 72h backstop, finalizes first-publication manifests, and then projects the public DDR cache from sealed outcomes plus live overlays. Lock-opportunity audit writes with a run ID use a SHA-256 attempt key over incident, run, and action; the nullable key is protected by a partial unique index so retrying the same attempt neither appends a duplicate audit row nor increments `deferral_count` again, while historical null-key rows remain untouched. The same job rebuilds the DDRR review snapshot from first-publication exposure, errata, and policy-universe coverage rows. DDRR persistence or snapshot failures are recorded in cron metadata without failing an already-written DDR run unless the cron abort signal has fired. Scheduler-health or context-health failures still publish a degraded DDR cache artifact: a valid previous snapshot keeps its rows with `live.stale=true` and the current degraded reason, while a missing/invalid previous snapshot falls back to degraded empty rows. Repair-debt markers are separate from scheduler health. The frontend reads the caches only; there is no model math at request time. The compute layer honors the repo's trigger-wide outbound-request budget and writes degraded lock-deferral overlays rather than inventing verdicts during unhealthy runs.
The fenced slot requires at least 45 seconds of its two-minute execution window to remain before DDR starts; the absolute pre-quarter deadline still aborts overlong work.

The runtime-neutral engine lives in `shared/lib/depeg-resolver/` (`inputs.ts`, `strata.ts`, `incident-groups.ts`, `resolution.ts`, `duration.ts`, `forecast-readiness.ts`, `hash.ts`, `public-contract.ts`, and `index.ts` exposing `resolveDepeg`). Shared types and Zod schemas live in `shared/types/depeg-resolver.ts`. The worker precompute writer seals first-publication manifests through `worker/src/cron/depeg-resolver/publication.ts`, projects public rows through `worker/src/cron/depeg-resolver/public-projection.ts`, and the cache-backed `GET /api/depeg-resolver` handler degrades to a `200` with empty rows when the cache is missing, and serves stale rows with warnings. Pre-publication rows never expose verdicts or duration bands; frozen rows expose anchored predictions plus immutable trigger/readiness metadata and live overlay facts. Incident state and repair authorization live in `worker/src/lib/depeg-resolver-incident-store.ts`; the repair-task runner lives in `worker/src/lib/repair-tasks.ts`.

The D1 tables behind that store are named below; schema and guard triggers live in `worker/migrations/0000_baseline.sql` (compaction generation in `worker/migrations/0229_d1_capacity_compaction.sql`), and scored payloads live in `depeg_resolver_assessments`. `worker/src/lib/depeg-resolver-publication-store.ts` owns publication writes and persisted sealed-payload decoding: it validates the stored outcome discriminator, base identity, prediction metadata and canonical frozen/no-call outcome before returning a typed seal. Malformed outcomes fail at admission rather than being reconstructed from later live inputs. Validation retains the original JSON bytes and outcome object, including historical optional fields and retired safety contexts, without applying schema defaults or stripping archive fields; immutable row hashes are unchanged. The cron adapter forwards that materialized seal without a second decoder, and public projection adds only publication metadata, errata and live overlays.

Sealed-payload admission accepts historical partial `readiness` and `backstop` metadata, validating the types and bounds of fields that are present without inserting missing current fields. Current public readiness/backstop schemas remain unchanged and require their complete current shape.

Assessment persistence uses one local SQL-column/record-property descriptor for diagnostic checkpoints and public-seal inserts. The `first`, age-threshold and `public_prediction` checkpoints remain immutable; only `latest` refreshes mutable assessment fields and `updated_at`, preserving its original `created_at` and event/checkpoint/methodology identity. Duration rounding, unavailable values and the stored diagnostic JSON are unchanged.

Detector duplicate repair retains any source event referenced by a canonical incident's first/current pointer or constituent link, whether sealed or unsealed. Reference checks run inside the deletion statement in the existing per-asset atomic transition, so repair cannot commit a dangling canonical reference. It does not move incident pointers, remove links, or grant sealed-event repair authorization; those remain the responsibility of the authorized canonical repair path.

Canonical event-history projection admission validates first/current event existence, their incident membership, and every constituent link target for each active incident, including when the first and current pointers are equal. Missing references produce `incident-projection-dangling-reference` through the existing unavailable channel rather than an empty result or raw-event fallback. This read-only check does not repair references or change sealed-event protections; genuine empty cohorts remain successful. See [event API admission](./depeg-detection.md#api).

The injected cron store port shares the owning stores' sealed-prediction and manifest DTOs and forwards materialized records without projection copies. Sealed records use `id`; public prediction metadata and first-publication memberships retain `publicPredictionId`. Narrow incident and staged-membership views do not invent persisted audit fields before an incident or manifest exists. Adapters retain source-fingerprint normalization, canonical seal hashing, the 72h load default, publication defaults and lock-action dispatch. Incident and lock-opportunity inputs retain stricter required run identity and registry fields. The errata store maps persisted rows once into typed `DdrPredictionErratum` records shared by DDR publication and DDRR; their append-only history and read-error channels remain unchanged. Scheduled capability evidence is consumed only by the existing cache-safe/pipeline-healthy gates, not forwarded as an unused lock-audit input.

Ancillary DDRR health is separate from canonical DDR publication. The parent cron propagates a stored review's explicit degraded state and original reason through `ddrrDegraded` / `ddrrDegradedReason`, including nonthrowing incomplete reviews; review execution failures retain their error detail in the same diagnostic fields. A successful canonical publication remains cron `ok` with `quality.reason = "ddrr-review-degraded"` or `"ddrr-review-failed"` when the ancillary review has findings or fails. Existing primary degradation remains degraded. These diagnostics do not change sealed prediction bytes, row hashes, manifest admission, reviewer scoring, or public forecast severity, and reviewer cancellation still propagates.

The cache-safe gate does not require exact active-set publication. DDR reads live deviation per asset, and an asset absent from the cache resolves as missing live price (`DDR_INSUFFICIENT_LIVE_PRICE_REASON`) on that asset's rows only. The gate consumes the `sync-stablecoins` `stablecoinsCache` capability, which stays true for a published cache unless `classifyStablecoinPublicationGap` (`worker/src/lib/stablecoin-publication-coverage.ts`) rates the active-set omission `systemic` (more than 10% of the expected active set). Unpublished, blocked, unreadable, or systemically incomplete runs remain `stablecoins-cache-unsafe`.

Manifest contracts expose token, generation, sequence, published time, payload hash and prediction IDs. First-publication membership also preserves incident identity and actual finalization time. The temporary migration bridge coalesces only identical complete legacy/v2 tuples and rejects contradictory duplicates; the legacy declaration predicate remains until the reconciled production import and bridge-aware rollback floor are accepted.

Latest-publication reads retain their publication-time/sequence ordering across supported storage generations. Migration `0277_ddr_publication_latest_indexes.sql` indexes that ordering for compressed payloads and clock references, avoiding an archive scan and temporary sort on each cache-backed public read. Selection, errata exclusion, payload decoding, reference reconstruction and published-hash verification are unchanged; this is an indexed-read optimization, not a changed forecast methodology.

None of these tables are age-pruned: `runPruneCronHistory` (`worker/src/cron/prune-cron-history.ts`) does not touch any of them, and rolling back a DDR Worker release does not roll their rows back either. That is why the rollback notes in `worker/migrations/MANIFEST.md` tell operators to keep the append-only authorization, consumption, link, and revision records — detaching an event or reopening a closed repair task is a separate corrective migration, not a delete. What the ledger does not cover: the underlying `depeg_events` rows and their provenance side-table are guarded by the same authorizations but owned by the depeg pipeline, and the public cache artifact is a projection that is rebuilt from these tables rather than repaired in place.

New manifests use gzip v2 payloads or clock-reference rows. V1 JSON reads remain live pending complete finalized-exposure reconciliation and a lossless append-only canonical chronology import, not merely a recent-v2 sample. Supported rollback must be reference-aware and, before import, identical-tuple-bridge-aware; an arbitrary old writer is not safe. Original JSON snapshots/rows/finalizations and shared errata remain a frozen D1 audit archive by default with status/growth accounting and unmeasured retention cost. A later optional drop is a separate destructive release requiring indefinitely retained R2 export, verified token/hash/errata restoration and a recorded Time Travel bookmark.

Snapshot lifecycle: the quarter-hourly publication only stores a payload when the published content changed. `writePublicationManifest` splits the publication clock (`methodology.asOf` and `_meta.lineage.trainingWindow`, the fields that advance every run) out of the canonical payload and hashes the remainder into `base_payload_content_hash`. When that content identity equals the newest stored payload's, the run writes a narrow row to `depeg_resolver_publication_snapshot_refs` — its own `base_payload_hash`, the referenced `payload_snapshot_token`, and its clock — instead of another BLOB. Readers rebuild the published payload from the referenced payload plus the recorded clock and reject the row unless the rebuilt payload hashes back to the hash that publication was published under, so every published generation remains byte-recoverable and hash-verifiable. Reference rows are append-only and are counted in the publication sequence, so snapshot sequence and first-publication membership are unaffected. Guard triggers (`0248_ddr_publication_sequence_cross_table_unique.sql`) enforce that counting at the schema level: `snapshot_sequence` is globally unique across the legacy, compressed, and reference tables, and a Worker rolled back to an allocator that ignores reference rows fails its publication loudly instead of reusing a claimed sequence.

Phase B (not yet implemented) adds the archive lifecycle: recent payloads stay in D1 while older compressed payloads move to R2 under their `base_payload_hash`, with hash/sequence manifests retained in D1, and `trg_ddr_publication_snapshots_v2_no_delete` is replaced by archive-authorized deletion through a reviewed destructive migration. Reference rows must survive that archive, because they are the only record of what an unchanged generation published. Until Phase B ships, no payload is deleted.

#### Finalized chronology reconciliation and retirement gate

`worker/scripts/reconcile-ddr-finalized-chronology.ts` is an offline, read-only tool over a complete frozen SQLite export:

```sh
npx tsx worker/scripts/reconcile-ddr-finalized-chronology.ts --sqlite <frozen-export.sqlite> --report <packet.json> --sql <reviewed-proposal.sql>
```

It walks every finalized v1/v2/reference manifest, reconstructs each original payload, validates hashes/ID sets/finalization claims and immutable prediction identities, and inventories the earliest finalized exposure tuple for every prediction, regardless of `first_published`. Never-finalized legacy first declarations and suppressed canonical memberships are reported separately alongside both errata ledgers and payload row/byte accounting. Contradictory canonical/legacy tuples or unresolved hash/errata evidence abort before any SQL proposal. The proposal appends original tokens/times only; it never calls the publication writer, ignores conflicts or rewrites append-only rows.

The operator must approve the complete packet, exported archive identity, row/byte bounds and pre-window bookmark, deploy/replay the bridge and establish that release as the rollback floor **before** the integration owner registers the reviewed additive import. Apply the proposal atomically with pre-write tuple/source checks; rehearse and re-export/reconcile full canonical equality, including corrected previously missing DDRR outcomes (not falsely labelled equivalent). The tool does not export production, approve the floor, execute import or demonstrate production completeness. Do not remove v1 latest/token/sequence/membership or first-claim suppression paths until import is executed and verified, current latest manifests are v2/ref, two DDR runs/next DDRR plus 24h are healthy. Keep shared reconstruction/hash/errata and sequence guards.

The runtime-neutral reviewer lives in `shared/lib/depeg-resolver-review/`, with shared schemas in `shared/types/depeg-resolver-review.ts`. The worker snapshot builder lives in `worker/src/cron/compute-depeg-resolver-review.ts`; its public cache helper and endpoint are `worker/src/lib/depeg-resolver-review-snapshot-cache.ts` and `worker/src/api/depeg-resolver-review.ts`. When an accidental duplicate incident is superseded by a canonical incident, DDRR follows the superseded alias to the effective current source event before classifying the canonical prediction outcome.

`worker/src/cron/depeg-resolver/context.ts` owns bounded context reads:
- Supply starts at `min(min(active startedAt) - 7d, nowSec - 30d)` with two earlier rows per coin, preserving all four nearest-earlier lookup targets and the two-observation guard without a post-target fallback.
- Training history applies audit eligibility before `HISTORICAL_ROW_CAP`; retained promotion/assessment severity samples remain available, while raw training rows are released after incident grouping. Still-open audit-eligible events are a second bounded read (`OPEN_TRAINING_ROW_CAP = 500`) for Stage 2 censoring; the closed-row cap alone sets `trainingRowsTruncated`.
- Lineage is published only from a completed context load: an empty board or a degraded run publishes `_meta.lineage: null`, never a fabricated zero-size corpus.
- DEX liquidity history starts at the earlier of `nowSec - 32d` and eight days before the earliest active onset within the 120-day onset lookback (`resolveDexHistoryWindowStart`), so the K5 onset baseline is readable without widening reads for very old events.
- Whole-cohort reads and V9 card parsing still consume isolate memory; bounded reads are not proof of a memory-limit incident or of operational headroom.
