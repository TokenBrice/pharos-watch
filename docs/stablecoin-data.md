# Stablecoin Data Registry

Stablecoin metadata is the checked-in source of truth for the asset universe. Use this document when adding, removing, or correcting a stablecoin entry.

> **Agent navigation** — Start at [Registry editing entry](#registry-editing-entry), then read the authoring and admission contracts relevant to the change rather than the whole file.

## Registry editing entry

Read [Editing Rules](#editing-rules) for every catalog change, then [Source Files](#source-files) to locate the owning base row or domain sidecar. [Required Checks](#required-checks) and [Cache Admission](#cache-admission) cover generation and active-asset admission; locating a source file alone is not an authoring checklist.

`ACTIVE_STABLECOINS` contains only active or omitted-status entries. Pre-launch, quarantined, delisted and frozen tracked records are excluded from live producers and aggregates. `READABLE_STABLECOINS` preserves the post-launch catalog for read-only identity, not live cache publication. Separate off-catalog PSI historical assets preserve authoritative PSI/monitoring and supply-history continuity when observations exist; they are not catalog or Bank-Run-Gauge members and are excluded from first-seen DDR forecasting. Canonical resolution has distinct tracked, readable and PSI-inclusive scopes (`shared/lib/stablecoin-id-registry.ts`); PSI-inclusive means active tracked IDs plus historical IDs.

The editable stablecoin catalog uses base identity/routing files under `shared/data/stablecoins/coins/*.json` plus research sidecars under `shared/data/stablecoins/domains/<domain>/<id>.json`. The sidecar migration is complete: the catalog loader validates each sidecar, merges it into the base coin, and then validates the merged record with the normal `StablecoinMeta` schema. [Stablecoin Research Sidecars](./process/stablecoin-research-sidecars.md) owns the supported domains, migration rules, and generator contract. `shared/data/stablecoins/coins.generated.json` is a gitignored runtime aggregate produced from that merged catalog; do not edit it by hand. Regenerate the aggregate and its dependent projections after catalog edits with:

```bash
npm run bootstrap:generated
```

The legacy category shards (`usd-major.json`, `usd-minor.json`, `non-usd.json`, `commodity.json`, `pre-launch.json`) were emptied and then deleted. `npm run check:stablecoin-data` fails if any of those filenames reappears under `shared/data/stablecoins/`.

The client, compliance, Telegram Mini App, Worker runtime, legacy redirect, report-card registry fingerprint, and client UI projections are gitignored local/build artifacts generated from the merged catalog. `coins.client.list.generated.json` (the browser list) and `coins.client.detail/<id>.generated.json` (one per-coin detail projection, loaded on demand by detail consumers and `/compliance/` GENIUS row folds) power lightweight browser-facing metadata; `coins.compliance.generated.json` bundles GENIUS summary posture plus MiCA and canonical reserve-report data, while full GENIUS references, negative-evidence review, applicability basis, and notes remain in the per-coin detail projection and are session-cached after fold loading; `coins.telegram-mini-app.generated.json` powers the Mini App registry; `coins.worker-runtime.generated.json` carries stablecoin identity (`id`, `symbol`, `name`, `geckoId`, `pegCurrency`), lifecycle, runtime classification (`governance`, `navToken`, `commodityOunces`), `contracts` and `tradedContracts` deployment rows, live-reserve circuit keys, and the bounded DDR inputs below for memory-constrained Worker lanes (mint/burn, Telegram, blacklist, DEX discovery, daily housekeeping, and DDR); `legacy-llama-redirects.generated.json` maps reviewed numeric and named legacy stablecoin IDs to canonical destination paths. The `stablecoin-client-projections` artifact emits separate lightweight modules for shell/homepage constants and command-palette tuples under `src/generated/`, keeping the full validated registry out of those client import paths. `report-card-registry-fingerprint.generated.ts` pins the canonical registry identity hash so Worker report-card requests never serialize and hash the full catalog at runtime.

The Worker runtime projection also carries DDR's bounded structural inputs: mechanism archetype, curated mint posture/path and incident dates/status, collateral quality, custody model, reserve risk/percentage pairs, reviewed blacklist status, dependency ID/weight pairs, wind-down date, frozen date, and obituary death date. Its `flags` slice preserves peg/governance/NAV classification for the resolver's existing metadata contract. Outside the configured live-reserve inputs described below, it excludes issuer evidence, source prose/URLs, controls, and other large research trees. Both DDR resolution and its review/terminal-evidence path read this projection; the latter reads the separate dead-stablecoin registry directly rather than importing the UI cemetery merger. These are lossless projections of the fields consumed, not independently curated classifications.

Live-reserve producers and recovery also consume the Worker runtime projection. Non-suspended configured feeds retain their complete `liveReservesConfig`, `flags`, `reserves`, and `reserveReview` inputs; other entries keep DDR's bounded reserve risk/percentage slice. All entries retain `flags.yieldBearing` because branch-balance adapters check underlying assets even without configured feeds. Suspended configs are omitted during generation, matching the full registry's load-time suspension policy. Field parity across every configured feed is enforced in `shared/lib/__tests__/worker-runtime-registry.test.ts`. Regenerate with `npm run bootstrap:generated`; `node scripts/build-data/build-client-registry.mjs` is the registered projection generator.

The dynamically loaded redemption and sentinel replay sidecars use the same projection. They retain parent/peg-reference/protocol IDs, launch date and audited peg-coverage start date, reserve coin-ID/percentage pairs, proof-of-reserves URL/provider, and only the public link labels used by redemption docs (`Docs`, `Proof of Reserve`, `Transparency`, `Website`). These small all-catalog slices preserve replay availability and public documentation without loading the full registry, including when checkpoint recovery runs the downstream sidecars.

Worker consumers that require the full reviewed catalog use its lossless transport, not a reduced scoring projection. `stablecoin-worker-full-catalog` is a registered bootstrap artifact generated after `stablecoin-catalog`; `scripts/build-data/generate-worker-stablecoin-catalog.ts` emits ignored `coins.worker-full.generated.json` with complete per-coin gzip/base64 records, exact UTF-8 lengths, and the largest record length. The frontend/tooling private package import `#pharos-full-catalog` resolves to the canonical JSON wrapper; Wrangler alone aliases it to the Worker decoder. One reusable output buffer bounds inflation to the largest single record. All source fields, order, hashes, registry views, and suspension behavior retain full-cohort parity. See [ADR-37](./architecture.md#adr-37). Regenerate with `npm run bootstrap:generated`; check with `npm run check:generated-artifacts -- --only=stablecoin-worker-full-catalog`.

### Durable legacy URL identities

`shared/data/stablecoins/legacy-route-redirects.json` preserves reviewed URL aliases independently of the current `llamaId` ingestion configuration. The September 2026 baseline reconciled 275 historical generated-registry snapshots, the March 6 canonical-ID migration (`55089693bb`), and the current catalog. It retains the old `cg-`, `gold-`, and `silver-` identifiers as well as numeric IDs. The supplemental `430` → `sofid-sofi` alias was checked against the [DefiLlama stablecoin list](https://stablecoins.llama.fi/stablecoins) on September 7, 2026; it does not select a new ingestion provider. GYD's old named and numeric IDs lead to its cemetery record's hub, consistent with its April 14 retirement (`17b6e3729e`). Unknown provider IDs without a reviewed Pharos destination remain 404.

The existing `legacy-stablecoin-redirects` generator combines this ledger, current catalog numeric IDs, and exact permanent `/stablecoin/<id>` rules from `public/_redirects`. The latter remains the authored retirement policy. Generation rejects conflicting identities, current canonical routes used as aliases, and destinations without a current profile or the explicitly allowed coverage/cemetery hub. Before changing or removing a provider ID, retain its old alias here; when retiring a destination, review its aliases and choose a truthful replacement or retirement destination.

`functions/stablecoin/[[path]].ts` applies the generated map directly for GET/HEAD profile requests, preserving query strings. This enforces the same authored retirement decisions without depending on asset-binding redirect behavior: the September audit found existing retirement rules in both source and exported `_redirects`, while their production profile URLs still returned 404. The audit did not establish a Cloudflare platform root cause. Unknown routes retain normal static 404 handling. Run the generator and the focused legacy-redirect tests after changing this contract.

## Editing Rules

- Keep IDs canonical and stable: lowercase hyphenated IDs, conventionally a ticker plus issuer slug, aligned with `shared/lib/stablecoin-id-registry.ts`.
- Add or update exactly one base file under `shared/data/stablecoins/coins/*.json`, then update `canonical-order.json`.
- The base file name and the JSON `id` must match exactly; `foo-issuer.json` must contain `"id": "foo-issuer"`.
- Domain-owned research fields always live in a sidecar, never in the base coin. Follow [Stablecoin Research Sidecars](./process/stablecoin-research-sidecars.md); the domain directories are the current ownership inventory.
- Sidecars are strict and must use the same `id` as the base coin. A field may exist in the base coin or in a sidecar, not both; duplicate fields fail catalog loading.
- `proofOfReserves.latestReport` in the base coin owns report observations for both detail and compliance views. `periodEnd` and `publishedAt` have distinct meanings and are omitted independently when unsupported. When a report prints only a signature or "as of" date, `publishedAt` may use that exact date as a conservative lower bound ("published no earlier than the signed date") with `publishedAtBasis: "signed-date-standin"`; never infer a later upload date. An explicit publication date may carry `publishedAtBasis: "explicit"`; omission of the basis retains existing explicit-date semantics, and a basis requires `publishedAt`. Stand-ins display as "signed" and use the ordinary chronology/freshness gates without changing report-period/composition lockstep or assurance scope. Sourced legacy dates with unproven semantics use `reviewReference` (`date`, `reviewedAt`, `dateKind: "unspecified"`); they display only as review references. GENIUS sidecars retain historical report evidence as of their review, never an independently authored “latest” date.
- `reserveReview.compositionSource: "live-adapter"` explicitly identifies adapter-owned composition evidence. It requires `liveReservesConfig`. A verified review with sourced `compositionAsOf` may coexist with a separately sourced report whose `periodEnd` and `publishedAt` are known and whose confidence is not unknown. Omission retains curated-only report-period lockstep. The exception never claims that an older report examined newer holdings: audit-grade V9 reserve admission still requires matching periods; independently dated fallback composition uses only its own evidence at `static-validated` strength, with the ordinary completeness and freshness gates.
  Composition and report sources must be complete; differing dates neither refresh timestamps nor resolve unknown baskets or relax attestor/assurance-method gates. Independently audited or corroborated admission still requires report-period agreement.
- Curated reserve composition admission requires a complete, verified, sourced, dated `full-composition` review under the existing chronology and freshness bounds. A positive explicitly unclassified tail may enter only at or below `semantic.backing.reserve.maxUnclassifiedCuratedResidualPct` (0.1%): each residual slice must join its exact index, name and percentage to an `insufficient-evidence` non-link disposition, have no tracked-asset link, and use `other` or no asset class; their total must match `knownUnknownExposurePct`. Identified weights retain the unknown share rather than being normalized to 100%; the evaluator charges that share at bounded-unknown reserve quality with `bounded-unknown-reserve-exposure`, never favorable `other` credit. The same residual gate applies to whole-asset portfolio observations, standalone curated compositions and curated live fallbacks. Above the policy threshold, or at/below the evaluator's scoring precision (≤0.0001%), positive residuals remain rejected so no admitted unknown tail can disappear as zero; zero-residual admission is unchanged.
- V10 scoped financial reports use `latestReport.coverage`: exact `scopeId`, `liabilityBookKey`, normalized `deploymentRefs` (B4 native economic key in `nativeLiabilityRef`, never a wrapped-token address), and enumerated `liabilityExclusions`. The historical denominator carries exact decimal strings, unit/currency/decimals, included and excluded identities, source digest, evidence refs and the report checkpoint; `assetsAsOfSec` must match it. `reconciliationWithinScope: "full"` does not establish whole-token `liabilityReconciliation: "full"`. Assets-only engagements and technical observations cannot earn financial-assurance quality.
  Numerical assurance additionally requires `currentBookPartition`, matched to the captured admitted B4 economic partition's base/source generation and timestamp, with a complete exact within-deployment book census and current exclusion amounts. Historical report tokens are never divided by current circulating USD. Missing partition, unresolved exclusions or incompatible books leave `currentLiabilityShare: null`; the residual keeps bounded-unknown quality and issuer-undisclosed responsibility. This metadata never changes canonical supply.
- `reserveReview.observations` separates independently dated `portfolio-observation`, `onchain-observation` and `standing-structure` evidence from financial reports. Each envelope carries exact subject/book/deployment identity, reviewer, access/review timestamps, source generation/digest, explicit expiry and obligation omissions. `reportScopeId` instead links report-derived composition to its exact coverage and period. Unannotated historical rows retain report-period lockstep.
  Portfolio rows enter whole-book weighting only with a complete matched positive `wholeAssetDenominator` and full reviewed composition; otherwise the envelope is diagnostic. A standing review has `observedAtSec: null` and establishes only a whole-holder instrument identity, not current holdings/NAV. The conservative feeder path requires one 100% high-risk `fund-share` row with the exact instrument obligor, over-seven-days horizon and no tracked-asset dependency. Live rows retain precedence; re-review never rewrites `compositionAsOf`. Budgets and method eligibility are owned by `semantic.backing.reserveScope` and `semantic.evidence.evidenceExpiry`.
  Obligation closure gates standing/portfolio whole-composition admission, not partial on-chain technical observations. Source-bound technical evidence may retain unresolved economic obligations without whole-asset composition or financial-assurance credit; identity, freshness, block chronology/skew and ratio-denominator checks still apply.
- `custodyModel` is a whole-book custody summary. An institutional label requires evidence for every material reserve leg and custody layer; a predominant or named partial custodian is insufficient. Use `mixed` for evidenced heterogeneous custody and `unknown` for an unestablished aggregate tier. Authored values always win. Omission resolves to `unknown` for centralized and centralized-dependent RWA-backed classes; structural protocol-collateralized classes retain `onchain` (all crypto-backed and algorithmic classes, plus decentralized RWA-backed designs), without establishing any underlying off-chain institutional tier. Keep providers, unknown shares and protective legal categories in the reserves sidecar's `custodyProfile`; changing the scalar does not refresh that profile or infer regulation. `mixed`/`unknown` fail regulated-only and onchain-only Selector filters and do not add numeric V9 credit.
  Whole-book coverage includes immediate issuer holdings and material underlying custody layers. `mixed`/`unknown` imply neither all-onchain nor proven unregulated custody and receive no affirmative custody-filter credit. Correct reviewed labels only on evidence-backed findings; `shared/lib/classification.ts` owns label resolution, while Backing consumes scoped reserve/custody facts.
- Run `npm run bootstrap:generated` after per-coin or sidecar edits to refresh the full, client, compliance, Telegram Mini App, legacy redirect, and client UI projections.
- Preserve existing supply policy. Never replace DefiLlama list-endpoint supply with manual, on-chain, CMC, or DEX values. Assets without a usable DefiLlama path may use only an existing explicit, documented supplemental admission path that fails closed and cannot double-count supply. The one runtime exception for a DefiLlama-backed asset is the bounded CoinGecko aggregate gap-fill for exactly one missing metadata deployment (DEC-01): it admits a CoinGecko/DefiLlama ratio only inside the reviewed band with hysteresis, takes every compared bucket from one CoinGecko series, attributes only the remainder to the missing chain, and publishes `supplyGapFill` provenance that keeps the DefiLlama total visible ([Supply Snapshot](./supply-snapshot.md#coingecko-aggregate-gap-fill-limits-dec-01)). Adding `contracts[]` for a deployment DefiLlama omits can therefore make that asset eligible; do not add a deployment to trigger a raise.
- Contract metadata belongs under each coin's `contracts` array. Use verified chain IDs and decimals from source metadata or explorers before adding them.
- Contract addresses are chain-sensitive: EVM addresses are hexadecimal and deduplicated case-insensitively; Solana and Tron addresses use base58 (Tron starts with `T`); XRPL tokens use a `currency.issuer` composite (the legacy `currency-issuer` separator remains accepted). Other chain addresses must be non-empty and whitespace-free. Case-sensitive non-EVM addresses are never folded for duplicate detection.
- Figure YLDS (`ylds-figure`) tracks the verified FCC certificate deployments on Provenance (`uylds.fcc`) and Stellar (`YLDS-GAC7MOPTQLQUM3KC24AW4GHS3RLF72LPEZO54AH7EZ6TSMGRB5SOAVH3`). The Ethereum `0x6ad038ca6c04e885630851278ca0a856ad9a66cc` and Solana `8fr7WGTVFszfyNWRMXj6fRjZZAnDwmXwEpCrtzmUkdih` identities belong to Hastra wYLDS, whose backing can include Democratized Prime lender interests. They were removed from the FCC row on 2026-10-03; they are not interchangeable with Figure's SEC-registered Treasury-backed certificate. Approval of a chain in FCC's prospectus does not independently verify its deployment address.
- `marketAvailability` is descriptive editorial metadata, not a runtime price/supply source. It can record whether a stablecoin is market-traded, limited-trading, non-traded-utility, or legacy-or-wind-down when a coverage source exposes that distinction.
- `pegScoreCoverage` is curated detector-history provenance, not a launch-date substitute. Use it only when replay and the handoff to live monitoring have been reviewed as continuous; include the replay run ID when available. PegScore and its recent-window companion start at this boundary so unobserved earlier days are not counted as stable.
- Keep `mechanismArchetypeReview` and `implementationLaunchDate` in the base coin file. Resolved reviews co-require `mechanismArchetype`; unresolved reviews intentionally omit it. Variants inherit the parent archetype unless a sourced `archetypeOverride` documents a real departure.
- Use `liveReservesConfig`, `yieldConfig`, and other feature configs only when the relevant pipeline already supports that source family.
- Frozen/delisted rows must not retain an unsuspended `liveReservesConfig`; `getTerminalLiveReserveConfigIssue()` in `scripts/ci/check-stablecoin-data.ts` rejects dead producer bindings. Remove the binding, not archive identity, curated research or D1 history. Explicit quarantined/pre-launch research configs and suspended historical configs remain allowed; their existence does not authorize active collection.
- An authored `liveReservesConfig.bootstrapForSupplyAdmission.reviewBy` expires at UTC start of its date. `getExpiredReserveSupplyAdmissionBootstrapIssue()` in `scripts/ci/check-stablecoin-data.ts` fails catalog checks for an expired field; remove it or explicitly re-authorize from reviewed source evidence. This reserve-only prerequisite does not admit listing or reset a consumed config fingerprint; [listing policy](./listing-policy.md#lifecycle-policy) and [reserve recovery](./live-reserves.md#deploy-time-configuration-recovery) own the operational gates.
- Every `coinId` nested in `liveReservesConfig.params` and every `pegReferenceId` must resolve to a catalog ID. `pegReferenceId` is variant-only metadata and therefore requires `variantOf`.
- An adapter ending in `-independent-assurance` may reference only a `params.product` whose reviewed JSON manifest is imported by `shared/data/live-reserves/independent-assurance/index.ts`. Every JSON manifest in that directory must be wired into the index.
- Use `oracleRisk` only for reviewed price authority or collateral price-feed setup. Safety Score V9 separates genuinely `oracleless` mechanisms from `privileged-internal-pricing`; `not-applicable` is neutral and emits no oracle component. Tracked variants can carry research metadata for coverage, while resolvable wrappers inherit parent exposure instead of duplicating it. Source reviewed profiles with protocol docs, audits, verified contract references, and observed blocks/dates. Every review must record a `role`: `collateral-pricing` when the feed values borrower collateral inside a liquidation engine, `coin-price-feed` otherwise. In practice `branches-required` and unresolved crypto-backed CDPs are `collateral-pricing`; everything else, including `top-level-only` mint/redemption quote authority, is `coin-price-feed`. Every review must also record a source-backed `branchApplicability` disposition: `branches-required` declares `branchModel: "multi-branch"` and requires feed (feeds or a documented fallback behavior), collateral-parameter, liquidation, backstop, and shutdown/bad-debt evidence on every branch; `top-level-only` declares `branchModel: "single-path"` and scores an applicable mint, redemption, NAV, or exchange-rate authority without liquidation rows; `not-applicable` records why no price-sensitive control exists; `unresolved` records the sourced gap and blocks evaluation.
- Do not use `not-applicable` merely because a mechanism has no borrower-specific liquidation branches. A top-level mint, redemption, NAV, or exchange-rate authority is still price-sensitive and belongs under `top-level-only`; stale pre-9.17 dispositions must be re-reviewed under the current meaning rather than copied forward.
- Use `bridgeRouteRisk` only for reviewed cross-chain route setup. Safety Score V9 combines curated route identities and control evidence with bounded runtime materiality. Missing required route evidence becomes an explicit gap or cap. L2BEAT Interop candidate output can point reviewers at protocols and likely tiers, but do not paste it into metadata without checking issuer docs, bridge docs, contracts, or L2BEAT protocol pages and adding `reviewedAt`, `reviewer`, `confidence`, summary, protocol evidence, and sources. See [Mint Authority and Bridge Risk ownership](#mint-authority-and-bridge-risk-ownership) for the deployment-scoped control contract.
- Active coins with multiple authored deployments require `bridgeRouteRisk.routes` rows that exactly match each catalog chain/contract pair. Route IDs use the normalized `chain:contractAddress` identity. Route rows carry protocol, issuance model, native/canonical/third-party class, semantics, reviewed tier, scope, observation/controller evidence, and optional failure-domain keys. Runtime supply is joined by exact chain/contract; same-chain multi-contract supply stays unknown because DefiLlama does not disaggregate it. `npm run audit:bridge-route-coverage` reports static completeness and known ambiguity.
- `flags` carries schema defaults for its four modal members: `pegCurrency` (`"USD"`), `yieldBearing`, `rwa`, and `navToken` (all `false`). Source coin files omit those keys when they hold the default value; `backing` and `governance` are always authored. Parsed output and every generated aggregate still contain the full flag set, so runtime consumers are unaffected.
- `variantOf` and `variantKind` describe direct exposure to another tracked stablecoin. An active child requires an active, non-variant, non-`navToken` parent; historical quarantined, delisted, or frozen children may retain the relationship for accurate readback. The child keeps `pegReferenceId === variantOf`; a `pure-wrapper` child keeps `flags.navToken === false`, and every other kind keeps `flags.navToken === true`. `VARIANT_KIND_VALUES` in `shared/types/core.ts` owns the exact kinds, including `pure-wrapper`.
- `parentBackingInheritance` is a reviewed adverse boundary for a wrapper whose mixed strategy does not establish a measured single-parent claim. Author `state: withheld`, `reason: mixed-strategy-without-measured-parent-claim`, `reviewedAt`, `reviewer`, `rationale`, and primary `sources` in the base coin file; this withholds favorable parent Backing inheritance without removing the serial cap or peg relationship, and remains in force until positive evidence supports a new review.
- Wrapper-local NAV accounting treats `privileged-internal-pricing` as weak even though it imposes no whole-score ceiling. An authored `onchain` custody model alone cannot bypass an incomplete direct-wrapper custody review: require a current fully-onchain allocation review or complete custody checks with zero unknown exposure.
- Pre-launch assets are ordinary per-coin catalog files with `status: "pre-launch"` until they have enough live metadata for active public surfaces.
- Quarantined and delisted rows retain their canonical per-coin file and a sourced `listingStatusReview`; see [Stablecoin Listing Policy](./listing-policy.md). Delisted rows also have an `excluded` class.
- PSI historical assets belong only in `shared/lib/psi-historical-assets.ts`, not the editable catalog. Publicly listing one requires an intentional tracked-everywhere decision; renaming terminology is not admission. Before removal, check PSI, depeg backfill and supply-history continuity. Reliable new source evidence belongs with this module and `PSI_ELIGIBLE_STABLECOINS` consumers. [PSI Historical Assets](./shadow-stablecoins.md) owns the public policy and PSI/DEWS boundary; metadata never certifies missing historical supply.
- Duplicate IDs across per-coin files or legacy compatibility shells are invalid and fail validation.

## Mint Authority and Bridge Risk ownership

Mint Authority assesses native issuance on canonical deployment(s) plus controls that can expand or replace that issuance. Bridge Risk assesses representations and cross-chain machinery: bridge mint/burn, adapters, lockboxes or escrow, messaging, peer configuration, limits, upgrades, validators, and administrators. A controller may be authored in both domains only for distinct capabilities; the same bridge capability must not appear in both.

The shared deployment identity is the exact `BridgeRouteDeployment.id` in normalized `chain:contractAddress` form. Use the canonical lowercase chain ID; lowercase EVM addresses, while preserving the authored casing of case-sensitive non-EVM identifiers. Every reference must name an existing `bridgeRouteRisk.routes[].id`.

For an active multi-deployment asset:

- every `mintAuthority.controls[]` entry needs one or more `deploymentRefs`, and each reference must resolve to a reviewed route with `issuanceModel: "native-issuance"`;
- `mintAuthority.upgradeability.deploymentRefs` is required when `canChangeMintLogic: true` and names only the native deployments whose mint-critical logic the path can replace;
- bridge machinery belongs in `bridgeRouteRisk.controls[]`. Each `BridgeRouteControl` has a stable kebab-case `id`, one or more `routeRefs`, and unique capabilities drawn from `bridge-mint`, `bridge-burn`, `upgrade`, `admin`, `pause`, `rate-limit`, `validator`, `escrow`, and `peer-config`;
- a bridge control may reference a reviewed native route when the capability governs a transfer pool, adapter, peer, or message path rather than ordinary native issuance; and
- a reviewed `bridge-representation` or `wrapped-representation` route referenced by any structured bridge control must have at least one covering control with `bridge-mint`; if the minter cannot be identified, leave the route out of every control's `routeRefs` so the conservative route-derived fallback remains scoreable; and
- active Mint Authority data cannot use `role: "bridge-admin"`, `authorityType: "bridge"`, `routeChecks`, or `mintPath: "bridge-or-oft-synthetic"`. The three bridge-specific enum values remain only for historical readback; `routeChecks` belongs on structured bridge controls.

A single-deployment asset may omit `deploymentRefs` only while its sole authored deployment is unambiguously reviewed native issuance. Add explicit references when another deployment is authored or canonicality becomes ambiguous.

Do not invent a native route for a product with no local issuance. Author `mintAuthority.review.noLocalIssuance` with `reviewedAt`, `reviewer`, and `rationale`; either exception kind is invalid when a reviewed native-issuance route exists. `kind: "inherited-parent-issuance"` additionally requires `mintPath: "wrapped-or-variant-inherited"` and `inheritedFrom`. `kind: "external-only-representation"` is valid only when every authored route is reviewed and every authored contract is covered by one of those routes.
A reviewed `noLocalIssuance` exception of either kind admits the mint-scoped `none-resolved-mint` annotation independently of `mintPath`, mirroring the derivation that scores such records `none-resolved`; mint-capable controls remain forbidden and whole-chain `none-resolved` retains its non-privileged `mintPath` requirement.

A reviewer-scoped open question is authored as `mintAuthority.review.scopedQuestions[]`, each with `controlRef` (an authored control's `chain:address`, or its exact label for non-addressable controls such as Hedera keys), `question`, `reviewedAt`, `reviewer`, and optionally `sources`. The schema rejects a `controlRef` that names no authored control. Use a scoped question only when the reviewer investigated the control and can state exactly what stays unknown; a scoped question does not void the review of the other controls, unlike `unresolvedQuestions`, and it grants the named control the bounded `control-scoped-gap` ceiling only while the review date is inside the `V9_SCOPED_QUESTION_MAX_AGE_SEC` freshness window in `shared/lib/safety-score-v9/evidence.ts` (see [Mint Authority scoring](./mint-authority-scoring.md)).

Bridge reviews use the same contract via `bridgeRouteRisk.scopedQuestions[]`: `controlRef` names a structured `bridgeRouteRisk.controls[]` entry by its `id`, its exact `label`, or its `controllerChain:controllerAddress` pair, and the schema rejects a reference that resolves to no authored structured control. A bridge scoped question softens only the named control within its route's merged overlay; it never attaches to conservative route-derived fallback controls.

V10 control authors may add `executionScope` and mutually exclusive `weightedQuorum` to individual native/bridge controls; [the exact execution and weighted-signing contract](./mint-authority-scoring.md#v10-exact-execution-scope-and-weighted-signing) owns admission. Author complete closure only after runtime/build/signing identity, every target/selector, latent initialization, reactivation and bypass path, and exhaustive Safe-extension reach are independently verified. Partial inventory explicitly retains unknown deployments and authorities; one favorable controller review cannot close another's unreviewed charge. Preserve nondisclosure responsibility and source clocks.

### Governed issuance and cap semantics review

Safety Score `10.02` added governed issuance and cap-semantics review; `10.03` added explicit decision rules and minority-veto evidence; `10.05` requires uniform voting control, class certificates and typed authority roots for governance process candidates and adds optional affirmative operational issuance; `10.06` makes known unbounded mint fallback availability-invariant under D33, requires positive reconciliation/supervision/workflow evidence, and strictly cuts over current annotations to `unbounded-adverse` across every registry status. Validation is owned by `shared/types/safety-score-v9-control-scope.ts`, `shared/types/stablecoin-meta-control-schemas.ts` and `shared/types/stablecoin-meta-mint-authority-refinements.ts`. [D29](./mint-authority-scoring.md#governed-unbounded-issuance-1002), [D30](./mint-authority-scoring.md#minority-veto-issuance-1003) and [D31 / H](./mint-authority-scoring.md#operationally-governed-issuance-1005) own the derived rungs; authoring the label never grants admission.

- `governedIssuance`: required `decisionRule` is `affirmative-vote` or `minority-veto`, with no default. `governorControlRef` is the exact EVM-only `<chain>:<lowercase 0xaddress>` of one authored `authorityType: "dao-governor"` control; non-EVM governors fail closed. `votingPower` is `holding-period-weighted`, `lock-escrowed`, `past-block-checkpoint`, `live-balance`, or `unknown`. Affirmative governance admits escrowed/checkpointed votes; minority veto also admits holding-period-weighted votes (balance × holding duration, with zero votes on newly received/flash-borrowed shares). `votingPowerEvidence` is trimmed and at least 40 characters. `enumerability` has non-empty string arrays `authorizationEvents` and `capacityReads`. Include `observedAt` (YYYY-MM-DD), positive integer `observedBlock`, `reviewedAt` (YYYY-MM-DD), `reviewer`, and at least one `{ label, url }` source.
- `governedIssuance.veto`: present iff `decisionRule` is `minority-veto`, forbidden for affirmative voting. This strict object carries `quorumBps` (integer 1..10000; basis points of total votes at the pin that can block alone, including delegation), `entrypoints` (non-empty exact lowercase four-byte selectors matching `^0x[0-9a-f]{8}$`), `override` (`none`, `symmetric-vote-destruction`, `insolvency-gated-restructure`, or `unknown`) and trimmed `evidence` of at least 80 characters. Symmetric destruction means neutralizing a vetoer's votes destroys an equal number of the caller's own votes. Unknown remains an authored fact but cannot qualify.
- `veto.restructure`: required iff the override is `insolvency-gated-restructure`. It carries non-empty exact selector `entrypoints`, positive `equityThresholdUnits`, nonnegative `observedEquityUnits` (both in **whole asset-token units**, observed at `observedBlock`), possibly empty `dependentPaths` of strict `{ controlRef, pathId }`, and evidence of at least 80 characters. Qualification requires observed equity at least threshold × policy multiple 2, a reachable governor-certificate path containing the restructure selectors, and `disabled-reactivatable` activation on every declared dependent path. Include the equity function in `enumerability.capacityReads`. Prove that only restructure enables each dependent path and that redemption retains at least one governance share; typed path bindings/reactivation alternatives must close, not merely narrative selector labels.
- `governedIssuance.monetaryPolicyPaths`: optional strict array, forbidden for affirmative voting. Each entry resolves an exact authored `controlRef` and execution `pathId`; records positive integer `rateCapPpm` in **annual ppm**, nonnegative integer `rateChangeDelaySec`, rule `minority-replaceable`, `unrestricted` or `unknown`, and evidence of at least 80 characters. D30-S admits only formula-bound deposits × rate × time issuance with a hard-coded cap and minority-replaceable rate changes delayed at least 172800 seconds; any qualified holder at or below the policy veto quorum must be able to replace or reset a pending change before it applies. This reuses `raiseable` with null bound and bounded impairment, never the fourteen-day admission minimum. Evidence names the constant/function, value and pinned block.
- `capSemanticsReview`: `verdict` is `bounded-by-construction` or `raiseable-collateral-only`; trimmed `rationale` is at least 80 characters. Include `reviewedAt` (YYYY-MM-DD), `reviewer`, and at least one `{ label, url }` source proving the authorization/raise powers admit only collateral- or deposit-backed minting.
- `governedIssuance.votingControl`: required exact controller/route census, source-defined pinned voting-supply denominator, own/other-holder power provenance, source-enforced unilateral thresholds, proved residual, affiliate aggregation and privileged/forced vote creation/delegation evidence. Raw powers are arbitrary-precision integer strings; every route conserves controller sum + residual = route total = voting total = successful pinned supply read. Missing terms or decisive unknown ownership deny D32. Above-threshold **own** locked votes are admissible unless positively issuer/team/council-affiliated; unknown beneficial affiliation and concentration are diagnostic. A threshold-capable key with actual **other-holder** casting/replacement authority denies even if its own stake alone suffices; needing other-holder votes to pass alone also denies. Controllers unable to pass alone need no identification.
- `executionCertificates`: required evidence/proof/census container for governed/operational candidates, even when all controls use individual scopes and class/member arrays are empty. Classes bind reusable contract-type runtime/source invariants; members bind exact deployment/proxy/implementation, conditions and path/authority/provenance bindings. Each control has at most one of `executionScope` or `executionClassRef`. Full constructor/history/getter discovery reconciles every authorized native member exactly once, including stopped/zero-current/latent members. An individual scope may cover a reachable member through `affectedDeployments` only with that member's pinned runtime/state evidence. No sample or familiar class name grants coverage; unmatched members retain partial/adverse projections.
- `authorityGraph`: required typed dependent → enabling-authority/origin nodes/edges and exact path bindings. Trace all owner/ward/role/admin/upgrade/delegatecall/execution/reactivation/envelope and vote-caster/replacement alternatives, retaining economic origin/recipient/claim provenance separately. Scope `authorityBinding` names the graph and matching canonical authority-state hashes. Hashes are SHA-256 over `stableJsonStringifyV1` of the canonical identity projection (graph/book/governor, sorted nodes and edges), excluding top-level proofs, review, path bindings and edge path refs; descriptive signer strings cannot grant rooting. Unresolved refs/cycles or changed authority state deny admission.
- `operationalIssuance`: optional affirmative operational-flow block; when present it requires `decisionRule: "affirmative-vote"`, unbounded non-wrapper economics, graph/certificates, exact path classes, envelope setters/raises, formula principal/time/beneficiary/accounting/rate-unit proofs, keeper genuine-activity/paid-fragment/in-flight repeat proofs, unique native-supply and deduplication evidence. Veto-only `monetaryPolicyPaths` cannot be relabeled as H. [H0–H4](./mint-authority-scoring.md#operationally-governed-issuance-1005) own admission/screens, including the 48-hour exposure anchor reusing the existing 5% moderate-exposure threshold; unknown measurements remain null with specific diagnostics, never favorable defaults.

## Mint Authority Review

Mint Authority review is a curated metadata track for answering whether durable native stablecoin supply can be created by privileged parties, governance modules, proxy/cap admins, backend signers, or only by user/protocol mechanics. Cross-chain representations and transfer-rail controls stay in Bridge Risk under the ownership contract above. Safety Score V9 compiles the reviewed native control evidence directly into the Economic Control pillar's mint component; there is no second engine over this block. Missing or unresolved review data returns `NR` for the display score and produces explicit V9 evidence gaps; it does not mean safe.

V10 selects the worst scored graded posture across every applicable non-bridge durable-mint authority, using binding only to break score ties. Root issuance stays asset-wide regardless of chain share. Only proved deployment-local powers with a complete reconciled partition receive supply-share weighting and proportional structural adjustment. Unresolved reach cannot prove immateriality; one scoped adverse path cannot displace another binding root mint path.

The detail page consumes a compact presentation summary when available: mint path, authority posture, confidence, the optional authored `headline`, summary, primary controls, incident metadata, reviewed date, and source links. `headline` is the card's summary-layer verdict: at most 25 words and free of raw identifiers, validated by `StablecoinMetaAssetSchema` in `shared/lib/stablecoins/schema.ts` through `findSummaryBudgetViolations` (`shared/lib/summary-budget.ts`). When absent, the card generates the verdict from structured fields (`buildMintAuthorityVerdict` in `src/lib/mint-authority-verdict.ts`); `summary` is reviewer narrative folded under `Review notes`. Homepage, screener, and coverage projections consume the lighter `mintAuthoritySummary` fields needed for the review-route buckets. Since safety `9.1` there is no second mint score: the published Safety Score V9 mint component is the only mint score any surface renders, and `authorityPosture` is a validated annotation that never affects the Safety Score (`npx tsx scripts/maintenance/generate-safety-score-v9-mint-posture-queue.ts --replay <v9-replay.json> --output <queue.json>` reports curated-versus-derived disagreements). `authorityPosture` is not unused, though: the depeg resolver consumes the curated value as a structural input (`shared/lib/depeg-resolver/resolution.ts`, `shared/lib/depeg-resolver/strata.ts`), so re-curating a posture can move a published depeg verdict.

A reviewer must verify primary source links, current native controls, Safe/multisig threshold and modules/guards when relevant, key-custody attestation, proxy/cap authority, whether caps can be raised, historical `mintIncidents` evidence, and unresolved issuance paths before authoring or amending `shared/data/stablecoins/domains/mint-authority/<id>.json` (defined in `shared/lib/stablecoins/schema.ts` and consumed by the V9 compiler, detail page, screener filters, coverage, and DDR resolver). Use `npm run audit:mint-authority-review` and `npm run audit:mint-bridge-ownership` for reviewed-source and ownership QA, not as substitutes for pinned source/control evidence. Upgradeable profiles can record proxy/implementation/admin addresses, deployment references, delay, observation point, sources, and an exact `controlRef`; immutable claims require sourced evidence. Incidents require `active` or `resolved` status so current compromise is not presented as remediated history. Addressed controls can add `observedAt`/`observedBlock`; a reused controller can add `controllerAssetId` only when review proves which tracked native issuance system owns it, while reviewed off-chain common modes use `failureDomainKeys`. Historical Mint Authority v1.2 required explicit `canRaiseCap: false` for its immutable-cap bonus; current authoring still records cap mutability as V9 control evidence rather than maintaining that retired score.

Reviewed incidents that do not belong in the existing per-domain sidecar fields live in `shared/data/safety-score-v9/incident-reviews-v1.json`, not in a coin base file or an arbitrary sidecar. The typed record names its control, wrapper-local, operational, or peg domain; root-claim, deployment, integration-only, or holder-exit scope; event and review dates; primary sources; and remediation state. The V9 compiler routes the event into the domain component that already owns the risk rather than adding a separate incident score. Keep native issuance incidents that already fit `mintAuthority.mintIncidents` in that field; do not duplicate one event across both sources.

`security-history` records of kind `disclosed-remediated-vulnerability` require `realization: no-reported-exploit`, resolved status, verified primary-source remediation and `informational-only` treatment. Their separate evidence binding creates no mint recency, wrapper discount, operational blocker or peg penalty. `occurredAt` uses public disclosure (`dateBasis: public-disclosure`); `resolvedAt` uses dated primary confirmation (`resolutionDateBasis: primary-confirmation`), never inferred onset or remediation transactions. Integration-only or historical contract-component scope establishes neither current deployment continuity nor measured share. Unremediated or realized events require a separate scoped scoring review and cannot use this vocabulary.

Common admission fields:

- `llamaId` / DefiLlama-backed assets
- `detailProvider: "coingecko"` plus either `geckoId`, Zephyr Scanner support, a curated aggregate supply path, or exactly one runtime-supported on-chain total-supply contract; commodity assets still require `geckoId`
- verified `contracts` for chain-level coverage and explorer links
- `status: "pre-launch"` for assets that should not enter active public surfaces yet

Saturn sUSDat (`susdat-saturn`) uses exact CoinGecko `saturn-susdat` supplemental price and market-cap admission. Its Ethereum ERC-4626 receipt is a `strategy-vault` of `usdat-saturn`, with an explicit local `rwa-credit-fund` archetype override: the dominant reserve is STRCon, an Ondo note tracking Strategy preferred shares, rather than inherited cash-backed USDat reserves. BNB and Monad deployments are CCIP representations and are not independently summed into canonical share supply. Current operational NAV observations do not establish independent financial assurance or legal recovery. Unavailable credit terms and an unresolved native control inventory remain explicit; admission does not promise a numerical Safety Score.
